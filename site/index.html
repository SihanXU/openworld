Places

Built by hand, down to the last stone

Every building is drawn by the project's own renderer, and every place is laid out on paper first, after the great places it learns from: a Gothic cathedral, a fortress in two rings, a harbour on a fjord, a town on piles. These are the models the rest of the world is built to.

People

The people you meet first

Every one of the world's 1,654 people has a look, a home and a day of their own. These six are the first part's own: each drawn to a sheet, with a portrait that moves as they speak. Open one to see the sheet.

The world

A thousand maps, all of them code

Every map, person and thing in OpenWorld is data the engine reads: a type, tags, statuses, a place. Nothing on screen is painted on, so a rule written for one bucket of water holds for every bucket in the world — and the world can be as large as its content allows.

Butterfly effect

Nothing happens on its own

Lightning sets a dead tree alight. Rain puts out a campfire. A theft in the storm puts up a bounty. Every event reaches the people, places and things around it, and each of those reaches more. Here is one stormy day at the Ford, and everything it touches.

How it works

Code runs the world. A model writes the code it lacks.

Existing rules run as code. A model fills the gaps with new rules, checked and shared with every player.
