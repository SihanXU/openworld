Lore

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Lore
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

“Suffering is not God's abandonment. It is the proof the debt is being paid.”

— The core doctrine of the Church of the Five Sufferings

OpenWorld's story takes place in a world that believes in the Church of the Five Sufferings. Several hundred years ago the sun vanished for a time, an event known as the Long Night. The saint Astra bore five holy torments, and the sun rose again. As the game begins, the days are once more growing shorter by the day. The player comes down from the mountain with the fairy Lumi at their side, is named a pilgrim by the Church, and sets out to find the five Seal-bearers.

The authority for the lore is the collaborator's document The Church of the Five Sufferings: Setting and Main Story; the full text is at Original Lore Document. This site only splits that text into pages, arranges them and links them to one another; it does not write new lore. Anything not yet settled in the original or in the team's discussions is marked “To be designed”.

The official history

This is the account every piece of evidence supports early in the game, and the account the game's characters believe (the original, §8, calls it “the first layer of understanding”):

  • Long ago the days grew shorter and shorter until the sun vanished altogether. Crops withered, beasts turned strange, and the dead found no rest. This calamity is called the Long Night; the people in the game call it “the First Long Night”.
  • Astra willingly bore five holy torments, the Five Sufferings: the Crowning, the Scourging, the Starving, the Nailing and the Piercing. When the fifth torment was complete, the sun rose again. Later ages honored him as “Astra, the First Martyr”.
  • His followers founded the Church of the Five Sufferings and enshrined the five instruments of torment as its highest relics, the Instruments of the Sufferings.
  • Later, the power of the five holy instruments was divided among five Seal-bearers, who shared Astra's suffering between them.
  • Now the nights are lengthening again. The Church has declared that the five Seal-bearers can no longer hold. The protagonist is sent on a pilgrimage: to defeat the Seal-bearers twisted by their Seals, recover the five holy instruments, and hold the Great Rite of the Five Sufferings again in the Holy City.

From the protagonist's point of view, this is a pilgrimage to save the world.

The two reversals

The story is understood in three layers, and the last two are reversals. See Main Story for details.

The first and second reversals (click to show)
  • The first reversal: as the protagonist defeats the Seal-bearers and goes deeper into the Church's ruins, the records grow stranger and stranger. The protagonist arrives at a new explanation: “Astra was not the one who ended the Long Night. He may be the Long Night itself.” The five holy torments look more like five seals, and the goal becomes stopping the First Martyr's return.
  • The second reversal: only at the very end does the protagonist understand: Astra carries the First Night, but he did not make it; rather, “he locked the First Night inside himself.” The nights are lengthening because “Astra can barely hold any longer”: “the cage is breaking.”
  • The rule of the world: “The First Night cannot be killed.” The world must always have a Nightbearer. The most deeply hidden doctrine of the Church of the Five Sufferings is “someone must always bear the Five Sufferings.”
  • Why the protagonist was chosen: besides the five holy instruments, the Great Rite of the Five Sufferings needs a new Bearer of the Sufferings. The whole pilgrimage is also a “Sifting”.

A decision the team added (2026-10-05): the Grey Man (Severin) believes the first reversal from the start and works against the protagonist; by the middle of the game the protagonist comes to believe it too, the two of them hunt Astra together, and during the hunt they discover the second reversal. Lumi is the failed Second Vessel.

Reading order

For players who want to avoid spoilers:

  1. The section “The official history” on this page.
  2. The Long Night, the Church of the Five Sufferings and the Five Sufferings (only each section's “The Church's reading”).
  3. Seal-bearers and the walkthrough parts of Chapter 1.

For team members who want the whole lore:

  1. The First Night → the Long Night → the Five Sufferings → the Instruments of the Sufferings, to understand what the Five Sufferings really do in the lore.
  2. Seal-bearers → Astra → the Nightbearer, to understand the whole system.
  3. Main Story → each chapter → Ending.
  4. When in doubt, look things up in the Timeline and the Glossary.

How much the game shows

The game currently goes only as far as Chapter 1 (the First Seal: the Crown of Thorns), so only the first layer of understanding appears in it:

  • The people of Firstlight Village believe the official history. Sister Ilse tells the story of the First Long Night and the Five Sufferings and recites the Church's prayers.
  • The days are getting shorter: Hesper, Sister Ilse and Nessa Quill all say it gets dark earlier every week. After the first Seal-bearer falls, the nights really do lengthen (see Day and Night).
  • The dead will not rest: the dead in the Old Barrow “can't lie down”.
  • The reversals show only at the edges: the Grey Man's actions, Lumi's reactions to the five-wound shrine stone and to the story of the holy lance, the previous pilgrim's camp. These clues are listed in Chapter 1.
