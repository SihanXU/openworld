Residents

From the OpenWorld Wiki · List

The game has 33 characters, plus Lumi, who follows the player everywhere. Each has a home, a schedule and lines of their own, and most of them live in Firstlight Village. This page lists everyone by place and explains the rules of dealing with people.

Dealing with people

Talking

Walk up to someone and press E to talk to them. Someone with something to entrust to you has a yellow “!” over their head; someone with a “?” over their head can move one of your quests forward if you talk to them now (a hand-in, or a quest that sent you to them). When you press E, they first say what moves a quest forward, then what they want to entrust to you, then hurry you along with their own errand, and only if there is none of that do they make small talk. See Quests. After that line, the dialogue box lists what you can say to them: first accepting or declining the quest they offer and the quest's topics, then asking for news, chatting, giving a gift, trading, asking them to follow you, bribing, robbing, stealing, spreading unbelief, apologizing and giving things back; see Dialogue. Everyone has one favorite gift, written on their character page, and they let it slip when chatting; give someone their favorite and their regard rises sharply (see gifts). Everyone has an age: children under 18 cannot be robbed, stolen from, or have unbelief spread to them.

Which line a person says is chosen from the most specific of these classes, in this order:

  1. Something that happened lately (a “topic”, for example someone stealing, or the ferry rope just mended);
  2. A line for after the story reaches a certain step;
  3. The greeting at a first meeting;
  4. A line for the current weather;
  5. A line for the current time of day: morning 05:00–11:00, day 11:00–17:00, evening 17:00–21:00, night 21:00–05:00;
  6. An ordinary line.

Lines marked “said only once”, topics, and first greetings are never said again once said; when a class holds several lines, the one said last time is not repeated straight away. Everyone remembers what they have said, what their last line was, and on which day they spoke with the player.

When the story needs the player to take a stand, the only choices are “Yes / No”.

Giving things

Hold something and press G on a person, and you are asked first (“Give the thing to so-and-so?”); on “Yes” you hand it to them (see Friendly people are asked about first). They take it and answer: some people have a particular reaction to some class of thing (give Hesper rope, for example, and she says “Not to me. To the winch.”); anyone without one says “For me? ...Thank you.”

Giving a thing raises their opinion of you a little (more for more valuable things). Everyone has an opinion of everyone else, and its label is written beside the name in the dialogue box (hostile, cold, neutral, friendly, close); regard is also drawn as ten hearts, and someone at full hearts can be asked to live at The Held Ground; see Friendship. The game has no gift tastes, birthdays or romance. A few story exchanges have outcomes of their own: give food to Tobin and he gives the player the Frost Wand; Garrick and Wren take materials to forge a sword and brew a draught.

Other people's things, and crimes

Things have owners: what a person carries, what is in their house's chests, what is set inside their house (for someone who lives in the open, on the cell in front of their door), are theirs (their household's); so are a merchant's goods. Taking someone else's thing while seen is theft. People remember who did it (and, if they cannot tell who, what the culprit looked like); they shout, ask for it back, tell others, go to the watch, and the guards come to stop you. Striking someone, picking pockets, setting fires, picking locks, smashing things and breaking into someone's house in the middle of the night are the same. See Crime.

These rules are the same for everyone: villagers steal too, get seen and get caught. See People's Needs.

A household

People who live together are a household: they share their things, and if one is wronged they all bear the grudge: the cook's house (Marta, Mia, Pell, Lina), the Watch House (Holt, Fennick, Oskar), the woodcutter's house (Gus, Cass), and the one-person households of Rowan, Edda, Joss, Sister Ilse, Tobin, Hesper, Garrick, Mara, Wren, Tam, Orrin and Hilde. Vigo and those who spend the night in the shelter have no home.

Trade, following and needs

  • Marta, Tobin, Garrick and Wren are merchants you can buy from and sell to; Tobin also fences stolen goods. See Trading.
  • People friendly enough toward you, or hired hands like Vigo, will follow you. See Followers and Hiring.
  • Everyone gets hungry; when hungry they go and eat or buy food, and those with no money and no scruples steal. See People's Needs.
  • Everyone keeps away from what harms them and looks after their household and their things: on fire they run to wet ground, in the rain they go indoors, and when their house catches fire they fetch water in a bucket. See Keeping Safe.

Schedules

Everyone lives by the clock, an hour at a time: sleeping, working, eating, going home. The hour after getting up and the hour before bed are meals, beside their household's larder or wherever the hour finds them (see Two meals a day). Some have a second schedule for rainy or stormy days, and another for after the story moves on; those with no rainy schedule who do not want to get wet go indoors when it rains and come out again when it stops (see Rainy days). Some work needs a particular thing, for example Hesper tending the fire needs the camp's campfire to be lit; when that thing is not in that state, that stretch of work cannot be done. People walk to other maps, for example Gus going back and forth between Firstlight Village and the forest every day.

Stand next to someone, facing them, and they stop and wait a while, so you can talk or hand them something. Walk into someone for about 0.8 seconds and you swap places with them.

Danger

People belong to the “people” faction, and monsters attack them. If the ground under them catches fire, or they themselves do, they see to that first: they step away, or run to wet ground to put it out (see Keeping Safe). When something that will attack them comes near (within 3 cells), they start and run away, keeping as close to a fire as they can; those who fight (Holt, Fennick, Garrick, Bram, Vigo) strike back when struck. See Factions and Temper.

Someone the story cannot do without does not die when their health falls to zero; they are down for a while, then wake with half their health; someone who is down is not attacked again. Everyone else (Cass, Tam, Vigo) can die and becomes a body. See Death of Characters.

Codex

Come within 6 cells of a person and they count as “seen”: they appear on the “People” page of the Codex, their card naming the map they live on.
