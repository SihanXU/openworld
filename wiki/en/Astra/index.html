Astra

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Lore
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

“ASTRA, THE FIRST MARTYR. He bore the Five, and the sun rose. Suffering is not God's abandonment. It is the proof the debt is being paid.”

— Astra's altar, Firstlight Village

Astra is the saint who appeared at the worst of the first Long Night. He willingly bore five holy torments (the Five Sufferings), and when the fifth was complete, the sun rose again. Later ages honored him as Astra, the First Martyr. His followers founded the Church of the Five Sufferings, and the five instruments of torment used on him became its highest relics (the Instruments of the Sufferings). In the main story's design, he is also the final boss.

The official history

  • When the first Long Night fell, people tried war, magic, sacrifice and prayer, and none of it truly stopped it.
  • Astra appeared and bore, one after another, the Crowning, the Scourging, the Starving, the Nailing and the Piercing.
  • At the moment the holy lance pierced his chest, the sun rose again, and the Long Night ended.
  • People believed that “Astra repaid the world's calamity with his own pain.”
  • Later, the Church divided the power of the five holy instruments among five Seal-bearers, who share Astra's pain.

Early in the game, every piece of evidence supports this account. Players and ordinary townsfolk alike naturally believe that Astra saved the world, and the player's mission is to repeat his miracle of long ago (§8).

In the game

Astra himself has not yet appeared in the game. The places in Chapter 1 where he can be seen:

  • Astra's altar: beside the sacred fire of Firstlight Village, carved with the line at the top of this page. After the sacred fire is taken, Sister Ilse goes every morning to sweep the cold altar.
  • Ilse's story: at dusk after Lina and Oskar are rescued, Ilse tells the villagers the story of the First Long Night and the Five Sufferings (see Chapter 1).
  • The prayer: in the quest The Pilgrim's Road, Ilse says: “The Church teaches that Astra bore the Five Sufferings so that the sun would rise again, and that five Seal-bearers carry them still.”
  • Stigmata: items of the highest quality “bear one of the First Martyr's five wounds”.
What the Grey Man says of Astra (Chapter 1) (click to show)

The Grey Man believes that what the seals hold is Astra, and that the First Night wears his face. This is the account of the first reversal, and the Grey Man believes it from the start (the team's decision). In Chapter 1 he does not say it aloud: on the East Road, the Grey Man stops the protagonist, says only that the knight beyond the thorns is a good man, and adds:

Only, when the crown comes off, something will come out of it. That, I will take with me.

The truth

What Astra really is (click to show)
  • The first reversal (§9): the protagonist finds stranger and stranger records: the five instruments of torment seem to have a real purpose other than commemorating Astra; underground there are many structures that carry power toward the Holy City; the pain the five Seal-bearers bear all ends up flowing to the same place; inside Astra's holy corpse lies a darkness of astonishing size; the nights began lengthening at exactly the same time the holy corpse showed signs of life again. The protagonist arrives at a new explanation: “Astra was not the one who ended the Long Night. He may be the Long Night itself.” The goal becomes stopping the First Martyr's return.
  • The second reversal (§10): Astra does carry the First Night, but not because he created it: it is because “he locked the First Night inside himself.” For centuries he has never truly died, nor truly lived; he has existed only as a vast vessel. The five instruments of torment hold him in place, the five Seal-bearers share his pain, and the Church of the Five Sufferings keeps the whole system running.
  • The nights are lengthening again not because Astra wants to come back to life, but because “Astra can barely hold any longer.” What is called “Astra's awakening” is really just “the cage is breaking.”
  • Why he bore the Five Sufferings (§3): Astra discovered that the First Night cannot be destroyed, but that it can be given a vessel. An ordinary person's body and soul could not withstand it, so he designed the Five Sufferings, to remake an ordinary person step by step into a being able to hold the First Night.
  • The most deeply hidden doctrine of the Church of the Five Sufferings is “There must always be an Astra” (§11). See Nightbearer.

As the final boss

The original lore text gives the cause of the final battle and its lines; the battle itself (skills, arena, phases) has not yet been designed, and it is not in the game yet.

The final battle (§14–§15) (click to show)
  • The protagonist finally carries the five Instruments of the Sufferings into the Most Holy Hall. At its center lies Astra's body. As the five holy instruments return to their places (the Crown of Thorns set on again, the scourge marks split open again, the Scales of Hunger rising once more, the holy nails fixing his body again, the Lance That Pierced the Heart entering his chest once more), Astra opens his eyes (§14).
  • Astra knows he can no longer go on bearing the First Night, but he cannot simply make the protagonist the new Nightbearer: if the new vessel cannot withstand the First Night, the world will only be destroyed sooner. So only one person is fit to succeed him: “one who can kill the present Nightbearer.” This battle is not meant to stop the player from saving the world; it is “the last trial” (§15).
  • What he says during the fight:

Early in the fight: “Leave this place.” As the fight goes on: “You do not know what you are asking for.” At last, he is satisfied the player is strong enough: “Good.” “At least this time...” “I can die.”

  • After he dies, the First Night sealed within him for centuries begins to run out of control. See Ending.
