Levels and roles

Every kind of monster has its own level (1–7, the same level as its trophy gear), and its health, attack and defense are all worked out from its level and its role along one curve: each level up adds 25% health, the same step by which gear grows stronger per level. So with gear of the same level as the monster, a level 7 monster takes about as long to fight as a level 1 one.

Role How it fights Examples
Swarm Little health, down in three or four hits Green slimes and the other slimes, bats
Fighter Five or six hits, with moves to dodge Wolves, frost wolves, zombies, skeletons, cave spiders
Caster Hits as hard as a fighter, falls sooner Ghosts, fire imps, storm wisps, dragon whelps
Brute Lots of health, high defense, a long fight Boars, stone golems, ice golems, giant crabs
Elite A fight of its own, three times a fighter's health Slime King, the barrow warden, the cyclops

The experience for felling one is still equal to its maximum health: the monsters are tougher, and every fight gives more experience too.

Temper

The same kind of monster can have a different temper in different places (for example the boar and the frost slime). Temper is data carried by each monster. See Factions and Temper.

Temper What it does Examples
Aggressive Chases and attacks anyone it sees Most monsters
Nocturnal Ignores people by day, attacks anyone it sees after nightfall The frost slime at The Ford
Wary Only chases and attacks whoever has hit it The fire slime at The Ford, the boar on The East Road
Docile Never attacks; when hit, runs away from whoever hit it Cows, chickens

Whatever its temper, whoever hits a monster becomes its foe. Each monster keeps near its own home, and goes back once a chase takes it a certain distance away. Someone knocked down is not attacked again.

The health bar overhead is red for those that will attack the player right now, and amber for the rest.

Fire

Except for those that carry fire themselves (the fire slime, fire imps, dragon whelps, the dragon of Cinder Peak), every monster fears fire: they will not come within 2 tiles of anything lit or burning, nor chase someone standing by a fire. So keeping watch beside a lit campfire at night is safe. See campfire, Day and Night.

Elements

A monster with an element attacks with that same element: fire sets people burning, ice freezes those who are wet, and lightning leaves people electrified. Fire monsters struck by fire and ice monsters struck by ice heal instead; fire monsters fear water, electric ones are immune to lightning, and golems take only half damage from lightning. See Elements.

Reaching out empty-handed

Press E on a wild monster with empty hands (“Reach out to the wolf”), and it snaps at your hand: “The wolf snaps at the outstretched hand!” It hits you once with its own attack (defense counted as usual), an angry emote appears over its head, and from then on it treats you as a foe. Wary monsters (which only chase whoever has hit them) and nocturnal monsters by day will also come after you if you touch them like this.

Animals (cows, chickens, calves, chicks) do not bite: pressing E on them empty-handed is something no rule covers (“Interact with the cow ?”); see The World Learns.

Handing things to creatures

Holding something that is not a weapon, right-click a monster or animal, and instead of attacking you hand it the thing (“Hand the stone to the green slime”). Rope is a tool, so a left click binds too. The creature takes it by its own rules:

What you hand it Result
Fiber: Rope, Cut Grass “Bind the green slime with the rope”: “The green slime is lashed down with the rope.” For 3 seconds it cannot move or attack. The rope or cut grass stays on it.
Something edible (bread, raw meat, berries, herbs...) “Feed the apple to the green slime”: “The green slime snaps up the apple.” A happy emote appears over its head. This does not heal it, nor change how it feels about you. What it swallows is gone.
Anything else The creature's own kind answers: no rule covers what happens (“Hand the stone to the green slime ?”); see The World Learns.
  • Bosses take nothing: a boss like The White Knight Alberic, and also the Slime King, barrow warden, cyclops and the dragon of Cinder Peak that carry the boss mark: whatever you hand them, no rule covers it.
  • Something already frozen or bound: handing it more fiber is something no rule covers.
  • Bound means frozen: binding uses the frozen status; on screen it is wrapped in a shell of ice, and its status reads “frozen”. So it is just like being frozen: blunt weapons or the earth element deal it × 1.5 damage and free it at once; fire also frees it (leaving it wet).
  • Animals can be bound too, and will swallow ordinary food, but only Animal Feed and Wheat count as feeding them (see feeding).
  • Things with their own use do that first: clothing and armor are put on it, a torch sets it alight, a container of water splashes it, and tools such as the pickaxe and hoe strike it.
  • Only the right click (and the left click with a tool) hands things over: pressing G on a creature does nothing (G only gives things to people).

Night creatures

After nightfall, zombies (six in ten) and bats (four in ten) come up on the outdoor maps of combat zones. They appear 6 to 10 tiles from the player, at most 4 on one map at once, and crumble to dust at daybreak. Peaceful zones, underground and indoor maps never have them. After the White Knight falls the nights grow longer, and the time they roam grows with them. See Night Creatures.

Monsters not currently in the game

The Slime King is already written into the game's data, but no map, respawn rule or story event puts it into the world, so you cannot meet it for now. It holds a slot on the monster page of the codex, so for now the codex cannot be completed.

After the fight

  • Felling a monster gives experience equal to its maximum health. See Levels and Experience.
  • Animals leave meat when they fall: a cow leaves 2 pieces of Raw Beef, a chicken 1 piece of Raw Chicken.
  • Every kind of monster, once felled, leaves the materials of its own body: hides, meat, bones, stones, and the Slime Gel, Venom Sacs and Ectoplasm only monsters have, lying where it fell; press E to pick them up. See Monster Drops. Those carrying things drop them too: the cyclops drops 2 Iron Ore, the barrow warden drops “Tomas's Belt of Vigil”; rope or cut grass you bound it with also counts as something it carries and drops (what you fed it has been eaten and does not drop again).
  • A Seal-bearer leaves 4 Iron Ore when it falls, and a Legendary Arm if the player has a profession. See The White Knight Alberic.
  • Things dropped on the ground lie still and do not fly into your bag by themselves; press E to pick them up.

History

  • 2026-09-30: The first monster types were added.
  • 2026-10-02: Two slimes were placed at the Ford; cows and chickens were added with survival play.
  • 2026-10-03: The night creatures, the barrow, and the monsters of the Emberfall line were added; the boss challenge added the Seven Sins bosses.
  • 2026-10-04: Monsters appear in the wild.
  • 2026-10-05: The Seven Sins setting was withdrawn, and the White Knight Alberic was added.
  • 2026-10-07: The barrow grew to three levels; monsters and animals were placed in the Mill Cellar, Dawn Hill, the Chapel of the First Dawn and the Held Ground.
  • 2026-10-09: Left-clicking a creature while holding something that is not a weapon hands it over: rope or cut grass binds it, edible things it swallows, bosses take nothing; reaching out empty-handed to a wild monster gets you bitten.
  • 2026-10-21: The controls settled on four inputs: using a held thing on something moved to the right click (the left click is the held thing's own use: a tool or weapon is swung, anything else is used on yourself), and picking up loose things moved to E (see Controls).
