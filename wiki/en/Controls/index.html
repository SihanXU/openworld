Controls

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

OpenWorld is played with keyboard and mouse, left hand on the keyboard and right hand on the mouse; there is no controller support. Keys can be changed in the Options (see rebinding keys); this page describes the default bindings. There are four inputs for doing things to the things in the world, each with one job:

  • Left click: the held thing's own use. A tool or weapon is swung (strike, chop, dig, hoe, draw water, light a fire); with an empty hand, your fist; anything else you hold (food, potions, clothes, letters) is used on yourself (eat, drink, put on, read).
  • Right click: the held thing used on what you point at (sowing, feeding a cow, unlocking, roasting meat, and every kind of experiment the world has never seen). Holding a wall, a bed or the like builds it there.
  • E: the hand. A thing lying loose on the ground is picked up; a thing set up is handled (talk, open a door, read a notice, sleep, pick a ripe crop).
  • G: put. Put down, put in or put on top what you hold, or hand it to someone; whatever you put down lies loose and is never built.

The other keys move, attack, cast skills, switch what you hold and open panels.

The eight buttons at the lower right of the screen (L B I K J C U Esc) do the same as the matching keys. Clicking the minimap at the upper left also opens the world map, and clicking a slot of the skill bar at the lower left also casts the skill.

Movement

  • Move with WASD or the arrow keys; when several directions are held at once, the last one pressed wins.
  • Hold Shift to walk slowly, taking a little more time per cell.
  • Walking into a person first turns you to face them; keep pushing against them for about 0.8 seconds and the two of you swap places.
  • When you are not moving, pointing the mouse at an adjacent cell turns your character to face it. Stand next to a person facing them and they pause their schedule for a while, so you can talk or hand them something.
  • Only the player can walk into the exits at a map's edge; walk off the edge of a map that has a place on the overworld and you are on the overworld (see World Map).
  • Height is crossed only by stairs and ramps; you can jump down the edge of a cliff one level high, down only, and a drop of two levels or more cannot be jumped. See Terrain.

Inputs

Input Name What it does Reach

E The hand Picks up loose things; touches set-up things by hand: talking to people, opening chests and doors, reading notices, waking a waypoint statue, sleeping by standing on a bed, entering a place on the overworld, warming yourself by a lit fire, picking ripe crops; pressed on terrain such as ground, water or trees, nothing happens; reach out to a wild monster and it bites Depends on the target: most things 1 cell; beds and places on the overworld 0 cells (you must stand on them); notice boards 2 cells; picking things up 1 cell
G Put Puts the held thing on the ground, on a surface, into an open container, into a fire, or hands it to a person; laying planks across a river is putting too Always 1 cell

Some key points:

  • Reach is counted in cells, and a diagonal counts as 1 cell too (a square centered on yourself). Every character is held to the same reach, NPCs included.
  • Ranged attacks (bows, staves, wands and spears, with a reach above 1) can only strike along your own row or column. A fishing rod is not a weapon, and casting is not bound by this: any water cell within 3 will do, diagonals included (see Fishing).
  • The Ranger's Archery and the Mage's Archmage add 1 to the reach of their profession's weapons.
  • Pointing at a person or a companion acts on that person, never on the ground under them. On a friendly person, the left click, the right click and G all ask first; see friendly people are asked about first. Right-clicking a person while holding a weapon “uses the weapon on them”; only with the weapon drawn does it cut them. When Space swings in front of you, a person standing in the way is hit too.
  • Another profession's own weapons cannot be swung; you get the message “Only X fight with Y”. See Professions.
  • Picking up with E and putting with G are physical acts with results that are always certain: if a thing can be taken it is taken, and if it can be put down it is put down. They never set off the world learning. The G prompt is shown only when it would change something.
  • When the right click (or E on a set-up thing) meets something no rule defines, that is where the world learns, and a “?” appears after the prompt. See action prompts and “?”.
  • An empty-handed left click on a patch of open ground (grass, dirt) that the world has never seen struck does nothing, so a careless click does not go off to ask the world. With a sword, a bow or another weapon that is not also a tool, a left click on such ground is a swing at the air that way (a creature in the swing's path is still struck), and asks the world nothing either. A left click on the ground with a tool in hand uses it there: what the world has never seen (a pickaxe digging grass, say) has a “?” in its prompt, and pressing goes to ask the world.
  • In a fight (an enemy is after you, or you struck someone or were struck, within the last five seconds; the battle music plays then too) a careless click never asks the world:
    • The left click strikes an enemy in the cell you point at or one beside it (a click one cell off still lands); with no enemy there it does what the world already knows (a tree still falls); failing that, it swings at the air that way.
    • Using something on yourself, or a right-click use, that the world has never seen is not done: the prompt says “Not mid-fight”, with no “?”.
    • To experiment mid-fight on purpose, choose the layer with Alt+left click / right click: that still asks the world.
    • No friendly fire: your blows pass through Lumi and the companions following you to the enemy behind them (arrows too); a click on one of them is a swing at the air that way, and “Attack Lumi?” is not asked. Out of a fight, a blow on someone friendly is still asked first.

Left click

The left click looks only at what you hold:

  • A weapon or tool in hand (sword, bow, axe, pickaxe, hoe, sickle, fishing rod, torch, bucket, watering can…): it is swung at what you point at and does its own job: a weapon strikes, an axe fells trees, a pickaxe mines, a hoe tills, a sickle reaps, a bucket draws and pours water, a torch lights fires, a fishing rod casts.
    • An axe, pickaxe or hammer striking a built thing (bed, workbench, chest, crate, smith's anvil, smith's forge, bee box, wall, door, floor, stairs): it comes loose whole, falls where it stood as a single loose thing, is drawn into your bag automatically when you come near, and can also be picked up with E. A chest spills its contents first. A locked chest or door cannot be knocked down; it only loses durability. A weapon such as a sword only damages it (for durability see Durability and Breaking). Things too heavy to move (tables, fences) are not knocked down.
  • An empty hand: you strike with the weapon equipped in your main hand, or with a punch if there is none.
  • Anything else in hand (bread, a potion, a helmet, a letter, a stone…): it is used on yourself, wherever you point: eat, drink, put on, read. A use the world has never seen (using a stone on yourself, say) first asks “Use Stone on yourself?”, and only after you confirm does it go to the world.
  • What gets struck: with an empty hand, first a person or creature in the cell you point at, then things too heavy to move (trees, chests, walls, boulders), never things lying loose on the ground; with a tool, first a person or companion you point at, then the topmost thing. Left-clicking a person with a weapon (an axe counts) strikes them; with a tool such as a torch it uses the tool on them (sets them alight). A friendly person is asked about first (“Attack so-and-so?”); with your weapon drawn (X) a blow is not asked about.
  • A swing is never aimed at yourself. To choose something stacked underneath, use Alt+left click.
  • It acts on the press and goes on while held: against monsters it strikes again and again at your attack speed; for work it keeps the same rhythm, and moving the mouse carries it from cell to cell (hoeing a row of ground, felling a stand of trees). Holding repeats only the same known act: things the world has never seen (with a “?”) and things that need confirming are not repeated, nor is what you just did undone (a full bucket is not poured out again); things used on yourself are used one per press.

Right click

The right click uses the held thing on what you point at, and chooses nothing else for you (on a friendly person it asks first; see friendly people are asked about first):

  • Using it on things: a seed on empty farmland sows it, ash or fertilizer on an unripe crop fertilizes it, a key on a locked chest unlocks it, raw meat on a burning fire roasts it, ore on a burning furnace smelts it, wheat on a cow feeds it, bait on a fishing rod baits it, a potion on someone gives it to them to drink.
  • Experiments: a use the world has never seen (an apple on a well, a stone on a slime) has a “?” after its prompt, and pressing it goes to ask the world (see The World Learns). The right click is the place for experiments.
  • Pointing at yourself: uses it on yourself, the same as a left click with it in hand (eat, drink, put on); a weapon pointed at yourself is equipped in your main hand.
  • Building: holding a wall, floor, door, stairs, bed, workbench, fence or the like, right-click open ground, a floor or a built thing to build it there; hold and drag to build a row, one per cell; holding a floor, press R to lay it as the ceiling overhead instead, or use Alt+right click and choose “Ceiling” from the list. Clicking water, a tree or a person is still a place for the world to learn. You can also use build mode (B).
  • An empty hand: the right click does nothing.

Friendly people are asked about first

Just as using something on yourself that the world has never seen asks first, striking a friendly person (or a companion), using something on them or handing them something asks first too, so that a slip of the hand does no harm:

Input Prompt Question
Left click (strike) Strike Hesper with Stone Axe (asks first) Attack Hesper?
Right click (use on them) Set fire to Hesper (asks first) Set fire to Hesper?
G (hand over) Give Apple to Hesper (asks first) Give Apple to Hesper?
  • “Yes” goes on as it always would; “No” does nothing. Holding the left button to keep swinging never swings at someone who would be asked about.
  • Friendly means neither of you would attack the other now: they do not count you as an enemy (you have not struck them, and their faction is not hostile to you), and you are not in a fight. Once you have struck them or they have struck you (within about 5 seconds), you are not asked again; nor about someone chasing you down.
  • With your weapon drawn (X), a left-click blow on a person is not asked about: drawing it means you mean to fight, and a fight is not interrupted. Using something on them with the right click and handing something over with G still ask.
  • Creatures (slimes, wolves, livestock) are never asked about.
  • Talking to someone with E never asks.

E: The Hand

E looks at the form of what you point at:

  • Loose things: items on the ground, things on a table, monster drops, and beds, walls and the like that were knocked down or put down with G. Press E to pick them up into your hand. Other people's things first ask “Steal …?”, and are taken only when you confirm; that is theft.
  • Set-up things: people, doors, chests, beds, notice boards, ripe crops, workbenches… Press E to touch them by hand: talk, open and close, open up, read, sleep, pick a ripe crop.
  • On a person while sneaking: go through their pockets (“Pick someone's pocket”); see Sneaking and Pickpocketing.
  • Pressing E on empty ground does nothing.

Even if the world learns “turning a stone over with an empty hand”, that does not change what E does to a stone: a loose stone is always picked up.

Two forms of things

As with blocks and dropped items in Minecraft, a thing that can be built has two forms on the ground:

Loose Set up
Which Every item; beds, walls, workbenches and chests knocked down or put down with G Things built with the right click or in build mode; crops, trees, rocks
Look Drawn as the item's small icon, not in the way Standing, in the way, can serve as a workstation or a container
E Picks it up Handles it
How it changes Built with the right click or in build mode → set up Struck with an axe, pickaxe or hammer by left click → comes loose whole, lying where it stood

When a chest is knocked down, its contents spill out first.

Pointing and targets

Every input acts on the cell the mouse points at.

  • By default, on the topmost layer. A cell may hold several things stacked: a person, things on a table, the table, a tree's crown, the ground. A short press acts on whatever is drawn on top (E first picks up what is on the table, and only once that is gone the table itself). When you stand on something and point at yourself, the left and right clicks act on yourself, and E and G act on the topmost thing in the cell under your feet (berries, for example).
  • You can point only at cells within reach. A cell can be chosen only if at least one input can do something there. A farther cell shows only the inputs that reach it, such as a single line “Left click Strike the wolf with the bow”.
  • When the mouse is beyond reach, the game looks back along the line from you to the mouse and chooses the farthest cell within reach.
  • When you are not using the mouse (your last move was walking with the keyboard), E and G act on the cell in front of you; the left and right clicks are mouse buttons and always act on the cell the mouse points at. E has one exception: when the cell in front of you has nothing defined for E (its prompt shows “?”), or is only empty ground or floor, and under your feet there is something that does something when pressed (a bed, a place on the overworld), E acts underfoot. When the cell in front is only open ground, E acts on what is underfoot even if pressing it changes nothing: standing on a place on the overworld that is not open yet, for example, pressing E tells you why you cannot enter (see places not yet open).
    • On a place on the overworld, you can press E facing any direction: the overworld's terrain is never “felt”.
    • Standing on a bed with empty ground or floor in front of you, E sleeps; with something else in front of you (a chest, a door, a person), E goes to it first, so to sleep, point the mouse at the bed.
  • Only on your own storey. When you are upstairs, you can act only on things on that storey. The floor of the storey above (the ceiling overhead, that is) can be acted on only when chosen specifically in the selection list or in build mode.
  • The chosen cell is outlined. A place on the overworld covers 3×3 cells and is outlined as a whole.

Choosing among stacked things

Hold E or G for about 0.4 seconds, or hold Alt and left- or right-click, and an “On which?” list pops up:

  • It lists everything stacked in that cell (that this input can reach), each line saying what pressing it will do. Lines no rule defines have a “?” after them. When choosing with the right click under your own feet, the first line is “Yourself”; with an empty hand, Alt+left click pointed at yourself lets you choose “Yourself” and punch yourself.
  • Press 1–9 or click a line to choose; press Esc or click elsewhere to close it.
  • With a floor in hand, Alt+right click has “Ceiling” as its last line: lay a floor overhead. With an axe, pickaxe or hammer, Alt+left click also has “Ceiling” as its last line when there is a floor overhead: knock it down, and it falls whole onto the storey where you stand. See Storeys.
  • The list includes only things on your own storey.
  • Time pauses while the list is open. An ordinary click never brings it up, so being slow on the buttons in a fight does not stop the game.

Action prompts and “?”

The cell to be acted on is marked on the map by four small corners: the frame slides to the next cell, is faint over open ground, and is orange for an act the world has never defined.

The cursor changes with E and the right click: a hand over things you can pick up, open or read, a speech bubble over a person, and a hand too when holding a wall or bed over a place it can be built; when the world has never seen the right click's use it carries an orange “?” (pressing goes to ask the world), when E would take someone else's thing it carries a red “!” (that is theft), and when out of reach the cursor fades (the act will land on the outlined cell). An act that asks first has “(asks first)” after it in the prompt.

The prompt box sits above the frame and says what each input will do when pressed. It appears only once your character stops and the frame has settled for a moment, so it does not jump about while you walk or sweep the mouse across:

  • Using the mouse: left click and right click (not repeated when the right click does the same as the left), plus E and G.
  • Not using the mouse: E and G for the cell in front of you. The line for an empty hand touching open ground the world has never seen is left out (it would come up at every step, and the frame's orange already says so).

For example, pointing at a stone with bread in hand:

  • Left click Eat Bread (asks first)
  • Right click Use Bread on Stone ?
  • E Pick up Stone

The wording of the prompts comes from the world's rules. The same thing is described differently in different situations: a helmet on yourself is “Put on Helmet”, on someone else “Put Helmet on Tobin”; a place on the overworld is “Enter the forest wild”.

“?” means the world has never defined the result of this act. The line is faded and followed by an orange “?”. Press it and the world learns it (when an AI is connected), or it is handled by default (usually nothing happens). When AI for this kind of thing is turned off in the Options, the “?” is no longer shown: pressing only applies the default, and nothing is asked. See The World Learns.

No prompt is shown while your character cannot act (during a cutscene, while down, while the world is learning), nor while a dialogue box or a yes/no question is open.

Attacking and dashing

  • Space: attacks once in the direction you face, striking the first thing in the way within reach. Hold to keep attacking, at least 0.5 seconds apart; bonuses to attack speed shorten the interval. When what you hold is not a weapon and no weapon is equipped in your main hand: a tool (torch, hoe, bucket…) is swung, and anything else (a stone, a rope, bread…) gives way to a punch; you never strike with odds and ends you picked up. You can fight with food in hand just by pressing Space.
  • A left click on a creature is an attack too, landing on the one you point at: with a weapon in hand you use the weapon, with an empty hand the main-hand weapon or your fist. To hand something to a creature, use the right click (with things it knows how to take, such as wheat for a cow); see handing things to creatures.
  • An attack with an empty hand is a punch, which can slowly break plants and wood; on stone it says “Too hard.” See Durability and Breaking.
  • Q: once you have learned Dash, you dash up to 3 cells in the direction you face; it cannot pass solid obstacles, and has a 4-second cooldown.
  • Shift+1–6: casts the active skill in that slot of the skill bar at the lower left; after casting you must wait out the cooldown. Nothing is cast while a panel is open; while the skill tree is open, these keys put the learned active skill under the mouse into that slot. See active skills and the skill bar.
  • For the detailed rules see Combat.

Sneaking and drawn weapons

  • V: start or stop sneaking. While sneaking you move slowly, and only people a short way in front of you can see you. While sneaking, press E on a person (“Pick someone's pocket”) to open their pockets, or G to slip what you hold quietly into their pocket. See Sneaking and Pickpocketing.
  • X: draw or put away your weapon. With your weapon drawn, a left click strikes a person without asking first (wounding someone is assault, killing them is murder); sheathed, a friendly person is asked about first (“Attack so-and-so?”, see friendly people are asked about first). Walk up to a guard and he tells you to put it away. When guards are chasing you, put your weapon away and stop fighting back, and they stop. See Guards and Bounties.
  • While sneaking or with your weapon drawn, a small tag at the upper left of the screen reminds you. When you are wanted, the bounty is shown there too.

What you hold and the toolbar

  • The toolbar is the first row of the bag, ten slots for 1–0. The chosen slot is what you hold in hand. What you hold is not drawn on your character; when a sword, bow or staff attacks, the swing, the drawn bowstring or the raised staff is drawn for that moment.
  • The mouse wheel jumps to the next (previous) slot that holds something. Clicking a toolbar slot chooses it too.
  • Shift+Tab brings the next row of the bag up to serve as the toolbar; rolling the wheel with Shift held does it too, and rolling back gives the previous row. The chosen column stays the same.
  • Drops drawn into your bag automatically go into empty slots, never into the chosen one: fight a monster with an empty hand, and your hand is still empty afterwards.
  • The single slot beside the toolbar is the off hand, for a shield and the like. See Equipment.

Panels and menus

  • Tab opens the bag. Along the top of the panel is a row of tabs: bag, crafting, skill tree, skills, quests, codex, world map; each tab shows its own key, and clicking one switches to it.
  • You can also press each one's own key directly: I bag, C crafting, K skill tree, U skill panel, M World Map, L quests, J codex. Press the same key again, press Esc, click ✕ or click the empty space outside the panel to close it.
  • The trading stall panel has no key: press E on the stall in The Held Ground to open it.
  • The trade panel and the pockets panel have no key either: talk to a merchant and choose “Let's trade.” to open trade; while sneaking, press E on a person to open their pockets (see Sneaking and Pickpocketing).
  • Esc closes, in order: build mode, the developer console, an open panel. With nothing open, it opens the Options.
  • Time pauses while a panel is open, except in build mode. See In-game Time.
  • For mouse actions in the bag and chest panels, see Inventory and Chests and Containers.

Build mode

Press B or click the build button at the lower right to enter.

Main article: Building
Action What it does
Left click or E Builds the chosen thing in the cell you point at
Hold left click and drag Builds one in every cell along the way, a row at a time
R Turns stairs; with a floor chosen, switches between “this storey” and “the ceiling”
B, Esc or right click Leaves build mode
WASD Move as usual

In build mode G, Space, the number keys and the wheel do nothing; Tab still opens the bag (and leaves build mode). Time flows as usual. Opening any panel leaves build mode automatically.

Dialogue, questions and cutscenes

Situation Keys
Dialogue box E, Esc or a click: the first shows all the text, the second turns to the next line
Options below the dialogue box (see Dialogue) Click, or press 1–9 for the matching line; Esc or “Goodbye.” ends the conversation
Stopped by a guard Only the watch's options (pay the fine, go to jail…), chosen with 1–9 or a click; Esc cannot close it
A remark someone makes in passing G dismisses it at once, and carries out this put as usual
Yes/no questions (such as “Eat Bread?” or “Steal Bread?”) Arrow keys or ADWS to switch, E to confirm, Esc for “No”; or click a button directly
Full-screen story cards E or a click to continue

Time pauses while dialogue, questions or story cards are open.

While the world is learning

When you press an act with a “?” and the world is learning its result, the middle of the screen shows “The world is learning this…” and the seconds waited. The world is paused meanwhile; press Esc to stop waiting, and the default result applies at once. See The World Learns.

When keys are not accepted

  • The title page takes only ↑ ↓ (W S), Enter (Space) and Esc; the load panel and the death screen accept only mouse clicks on their buttons. See Difficulty and Saving.
  • During a cutscene, your character cannot act and no action prompts are shown.
  • The first lessons of the tutorial lock the inputs they have not taught yet: pressing them does nothing, and the key icon on screen shakes.

The key table in the game

The Options have a short key table, made from your current bindings, which follows when you change keys. With the default bindings its contents match this page: the left click is the held thing's own use, the right click uses it on what you point at, E picks up or handles, G puts, Shift+1–6 casts skills, and the last line is “Esc · I · C · K · U · M · L · J: menu · bag · crafting · skill tree · skill panel · map · quests · codex (with the Threadbook)”. The development build has one more line, ` Mod Console.

Rebinding keys

In the Options, click “Change keys…” to open the keys page:

  • Each action has a row with two columns, key and alternate key; both keys work (moving up, for example, is W and ↑ by default). Click one, then press the new key; press Esc to cancel, or Backspace to clear that key.
  • Changes start as a draft: the top of the page says “Not saved yet”, and they take effect only when you click Save; click Discard changes to go back to how things were. Click “← Options” without saving and you are asked whether to save or discard.
  • If the new key is already taken by another action, you are asked first: “G is “…”'s key. Trade the two?”; click “Trade” to swap them, or “Cancel” to change nothing.
  • A row that differs from the default is marked, with “↺ Default E” on its right; click it to restore that row's default (and the action whose key it took also gets its own default back where possible). Restore all defaults at the top sets every key back to its default (which must also be saved).
  • Skills can be bound to Shift+a key; movement and slow walking cannot use Shift (Shift is the slow-walk key).
  • What cannot be changed: the left and right mouse buttons (the left and right clicks belong to the mouse alone, with no keyboard version), Esc, `, and the browser's own keys such as Ctrl, Alt, F5, F11 and F12 (Ctrl+W, for instance, closes the web page outright).
  • Once you change a key, everywhere the game names keys follows: the action prompts, the key table, the keys on the lower-right buttons and the toolbar, the skill bar, and “press X” in the tutorial and in quests.
  • Bindings are kept in this computer's browser, not in the save; switch browsers or clear the site's data and they return to the defaults.

Tips

  • When you are not sure what a press will do, look at the prompt bar above your character first. An act with a “?” is something this world has never seen, and just right for trying: right-click with something in hand.
  • To act on something stacked underneath (the table rather than the cup on it), hold E, or Alt+click.
  • To move a bed somewhere else: strike it once with an axe by left click, and it falls whole; pick it up, then right-click to build it elsewhere.
  • Ranged weapons cannot hit targets on a diagonal; first get into the same row or column.
