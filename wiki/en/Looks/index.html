Looks

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

A look is how a person appears. Everyone in the game (villagers, passers-by, the player, and in future the people the modding AI brings into the world) has a look record: one body, one part per slot, and one color per color key. The 24×36 pixel figure on the map, the anime-style bust portrait beside the dialogue box, the pictures in the codex and on the wiki, and the appearance tags that rules can recognize are all drawn and derived from this one record. When someone dyes their hair, it changes on the map, in the portrait and in the tags at the same time; the three can never disagree.

The look record

Part Contents
Body Woman, man, girl, boy. Decides the figure's build on the map and the portrait's bust. Adult men and women share the same head, so the parts worn on the head fit both bodies
Parts One per slot: hair, face, beard, age, mark, glasses, hat, cloak, armour, outfit. Hat, beard, mark and glasses can be left empty; cloak and armour appear only when something is worn
Colors Hair color, eye color, skin color, and the parts' own colors (an outfit's main color, its second color, its leather or metal color, a hat's color). Beard and eyebrows follow the hair color

One color colors both pictures at once: the map figure takes light, middle, dark and deepest shades from it; the portrait spreads it into a whole ramp from line art to highlight. So silver hair on the map and silver hair in the portrait are the same silver.

Three outputs

  • The map figure: a 24×36 pixel, two-cell-tall FFBE-style chibi figure (since 2026-10-17), with three drawn directions (front, back, and a 3/4 view facing left, facing right being its mirror image), each with four walking frames and one standing frame: the body sits one pixel lower mid-stride, arms and legs swing alternately forward and back, and a standing woman rests her weight on one leg. The body is a bald base figure (man, woman; a child is the woman's head on a child's body, so hairstyles, hats and other head parts fit everyone), on which clothes and the armour and cloak over them are put on frame by frame (sleeves swing with the arms, and a long dress shows only the tips of the toes when walking), and then the face, beard, hair, glasses and hat are drawn, the hat covering the hair beneath it. The bases and parts were first sketched by a local model and then set onto the pixel grid and cleaned up. Monsters are still 16×16.
  • The portrait: the bust standing on the left of the dialogue box (Interface). Its parts are layered anime-style pictures, recolored with the record's colors and stacked. The mood of a line (happy, sad, angry, surprised, shy) switches to the matching expression. When the speaker has a status, the portrait shows it too: being wet is a layer of blue from the top down, burning an orange glow from the bottom up, being frozen or electrified a wash of icy blue or bright yellow, just as on the map figure. The codex (J) shows the map figure; the “Portrait” section of a character page on the wiki has both.
  • Tags and description: every part states which tags it gives a person (look:bearded bearded, look:hooded hooded, look:grey-haired grey-haired…); these tags are derived from the record, never written by hand. Rules can recognize people by appearance, such as “people in armour”; the modding AI sees a description, such as “woman; hair: bun (silver); face: soft eyes (brown); outfit: long dress and shawl (plum).”

Portraits of their own

The story's key people can have a portrait of their own, shown in the dialogue instead of the bust the look system composes. It is drawn on its own, as a visual novel's character design: a picture for each mood (neutral, happy, sad, angry, surprised, shy), each with a blinking and a speaking frame, so the person blinks in the dialogue box (once soon after each line begins, then every three or four seconds) and their mouth opens and closes as they speak. It sits inside the box, cut by its frame at the shoulders, a little smaller than a look's bust, so the pixel world stays in front. The map figure and the tags still come from the look record; for someone with a portrait of their own, the look record follows the design where it can (the hair, for one).

People with a portrait of their own so far: Nessa Quill and Lumi (2026-10-24), Lina, Sister Ilse, The Grey Man and Tobin (2026-10-25), Hesper (2026-10-28). Lumi has no look record; her portrait is her design. Every portrait of one's own faces right, toward the line in the dialogue box.

Where each person's look comes from

  • Villagers: each person's record holds the parts the author chose (Wren's auburn curls and herbalist's robe), and the generator fills in the rest by who they are: age decides the chance of grey hair and wrinkles, occupation decides the outfit (a smith wears a leather apron, a guard a tabard), and sex only weights the choice, never rules anything out. The generator is seeded by the person's id, so the same person looks the same every time.
  • The player: when starting a new game you make your own in “Your look”: choose a body, flip through the parts one by one, click a swatch to change a color, or roll the dice for a random look. The portrait on the right can switch expressions to preview them, and the little figure walks round in a circle. The default is a traveler with a ponytail in adventurer's gear.
  • People without a record: if a person made by a rule or the modding AI is given no look, one is generated from their id, and they are no longer shown as a slime.

What is worn

Helmets, hats, circlets, the Crown of Thorns, breastplates, pauldrons and cloaks are drawn on whoever wears them: each thing states which look part it becomes when worn (a helmet is the helmet of the hat slot, a breastplate the “armour” slot, cloaks and pauldrons the “cloak” slot), and its color is taken from its material. The look others see is “their own look plus what they wear”; the map figure, the portrait and the appearance tags are all drawn and derived from that; take it off, and the look goes back to their own. The person's own look record doesn't change because of it. These parts only appear when something is worn, and can't be chosen in the character creator.

Changing a look

A look is a person's own state. The rule effect restyle can only change its host's own look: swap a part, change a color. A changed look counts as the person's own, and is not overwritten by the world's settings when a save is loaded. There are no ready-made rules for dyeing or cutting hair in the game yet; using a dye or scissors on someone is undefined behavior, which is exactly the place for the world learns.

Technical notes

  • The portrait parts were drawn by a locally run open-source image model, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (Apache-2.0): each part is one edit made on the same bald base model; a program cuts out the part that changed and turns it into light and shade values, and the game recolors it with the chosen colors. The prompts and the pipeline used to generate them are in the repository (games/openworld/tools/src/art/looks/portrait/).
  • The map figure's bases and parts are pixel grids (bases.ts, baseoutfit.ts, basehead.ts and basehair.ts in packages/runtime/src/assets/figure/): the same model first sketches on the bald base, a program reads which piece of cloth and which color lies on each cell of the body, and lays it onto every frame by body part; the method and tools are in games/openworld/tools/src/art/looks/sprite/.
  • How a portrait of one's own is made (the cut-out, the expressions, the blinking and speaking frames): games/openworld/tools/src/art/portraits/, design document docs/character-art.md.
