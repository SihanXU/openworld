Player Character

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

The player character is the person the player controls. In the game this person's name is simply “you”, and it cannot be changed; you make their look yourself when you start a new game (Looks). They wake in the ruined outpost on Cloudtop with only the fairy Lumi beside them. Once they come down the mountain, the people of Firstlight Village call them the traveler, and later the Pilgrim.

Base stats

At the start of a new game:

Stat Value Notes
Health 40 / 40 Ten hearts, 4 points each
Attack 2 The power of a bare-handed punch; with a weapon, the weapon's power is added
Defense 0 Subtracted from every hit you take, to a minimum of 1
Movement 0.2 seconds per tile Hold Shift to walk slowly
Satiety 12 / 20 You start a little hungry
Level 1 0 experience, 0 skill points
Sight 7 tiles Halved away from firelight after dark; see Day and Night
Inventory 30 slots See Inventory

You start with nothing on you. Skills, equipment, professions and Boons change these numbers.

Looks

When you start a new game, the top of the “New game” panel is “Your look”: choose a body (woman or man), flip through hairstyles, facial features, beards, marks, glasses, hats and outfits one by one, click the color swatches to change hair color, eye color, skin color and the colors of the clothes, or roll the dice for a random look. The little figure on the map, the portrait beside the dialogue box and the tags that describe how you look all come from this one look record; see Looks. The default is a traveler with a ponytail in adventurer's clothes. What you hold in your hands is not drawn on the body: a sword, bow or staff is drawn only in the moment you strike with it. Equipment such as helmets, breastplates and cloaks is drawn on the body once worn.

The player in the rules

OpenWorld's rules never ask “is this the player?”. In the rules the player character is an ordinary “person”, following the same rules as the villagers:

  • They belong to the folk faction, and the wild attacks them just as it would attack any villager.
  • They carry the property “does not die”: when their health reaches zero they collapse instead of vanishing. A villager who collapses gets up again after a while; when the player collapses, the death screen appears; see Difficulty.
  • Eating, wearing clothes, sleeping, getting hurt in a fall, catching fire, getting soaked in the rain: the rules treat them exactly as they treat anyone else. They can also hold a piece of clothing and right-click someone else to dress them in it.
  • For now only the player character has satiety. Villagers and animals have none, so there is no ready rule for feeding them ordinary food; it is something the world has to learn. Animals only take Animal Feed and Wheat; see Feeding.
  • What they do is seen by the people nearby. Take someone's things in front of the owner and the owner says “Hey — that is mine!”, and the story goes round the village for two days.
  • The engine checks the reach of every action, the same for every character. See Inputs.

Only the input knows which character the keyboard is moving.

Growth

  • Levels: defeating creatures earns experience, and every level gained gives 1 skill point.
  • Skills: spend skill points on the skill tree (K) to learn skills that raise health, attack, defense or speed, or slow hunger.
  • Professions: from level 5 you can see Bram in Firstlight Village and choose Assassin, Warrior, Ranger or Mage.
  • Equipment: ten slots plus the off hand, affecting attack, defense and other stats. The higher the quality, the higher the numbers.
  • Boons: after you defeat a Seal-bearer, Lumi catches part of their power, and you can choose one kind to carry with you.

Setting

The protagonist is sent onto the Pilgrim's Road by the Church of the Five Sufferings to find the five Seal-bearers and bring back the Instruments of the Sufferings. See Main Story.

The real reason the protagonist was chosen (click to show)

According to the original lore document, the Church of the Five Sufferings did not choose the protagonist for courage alone. To restart the Great Rite of the Five Sufferings, it needs not only the five Instruments of the Sufferings but also a new Bearer of the Sufferings: someone able to defeat the five Seal-bearers and bear the power of the five Seals. In the intended ending, the protagonist inherits the Five Sufferings and becomes the new Nightbearer. See Ending.

History

  • 2026-10-02: The player's figure became a ninja in a dog mask; the inventory, satiety, levels and experience were added.
  • 2026-10-02: The rules stopped asking “is this the player”: faction, temper and collapsing became data each character carries.
  • 2026-10-03: The skill tree and professions were added.
  • 2026-10-05: The off-hand slot for equipment and Lumi's Boons were added.
  • 2026-10-21: The controls were set as four inputs: using a held thing on something moved to the right click (the left click is the held thing's own use: a tool or weapon is swung, anything else is used on yourself), and picking up loose things moved to E (see Controls).
