The White Knight Alberic is the first Seal-bearer and the boss of Chapter 1. The Crown of Thorns has grown into his helm, and the crown has worn away most of his self; all he remembers is that he must “hold”. He keeps a field at the end of The East Road as his own kingdom, fenced all round with thorns, and he tore down the little bridge at The Ford so that nothing which will not kneel can get past.

Defeat him, and Chapter 1 draws to its close: he takes off the Crown of Thorns and hands it to the player, the power that spills from the crown flows into Lumi, and after that the nights grow longer. For his human form after he takes off the crown, see Alberic.

Spawning

  • Campaign: The Kingdom of Thorns
  • Boss Challenge
  • The East Road: a field ringed with thorns at the east end of the road, with “kneeling stones” carved as kneeling pilgrims standing in it. He is inside, and has been from the start of the game. The first time you walk into the ring of thorns, a scene plays in which he speaks: “None cross that do not kneel.”
  • The boss challenge and the boss test arena: the fight takes place on a trampled bare clearing beside the East Road, with the challenge's numbers. There is no story there, no other characters, and no Lumi.
  • The Mod Console of the development build has a “Summon the White Knight” button, which calls one up at his old place on the East Road.

Every move “winds up” first: colored marks on the ground show every tile the move will land on; once locked, the marks no longer follow the player, and the move lands only when the wind-up ends. Step off the marked tiles and it cannot hit you. All his damage is a multiple of his attack: attack 10 in the campaign, 24 in the boss challenge.

Briar sprouts do not block the way, but “none cross that do not kneel”: step into one and you are cut and kneel for a second. They wither after 12 seconds, and can also be cut down or burned away (the thorns catch fire, and fire runs along the line of thorns). He himself walks through the thorns unharmed. After being pinned once, you are steadied for a while, and during that time you cannot be pinned kneeling again.

The two phases:

  • Phase 1 (100%–50%): Arrow of Conquest → White-Feather Volley → Thorns Bend the Knee → Arrow of Conquest → None Cross Unkneeling, on a loop. When you are right beside him, he first pushes you off with Hilt Rebuff (without breaking the order).
  • Health falls to half: he plants his sword in the ground, says “Hold.”, and grows a hedge in a ring 6 tiles from him, shrinking the arena. This move is steady: a stun only delays it, never interrupts it.
  • Phase 2 (50%–0%): Arrow of Conquest (two arrows in a row) → White-Feather Volley → None Cross Unkneeling → Thorns Bend the Knee (inner ring then outer ring), on a loop; wind-ups 15% shorter, recovery 30% shorter, damage 25% higher.

Behavior

  • Two sets of numbers. In Chapter 1 he is weaker than in the boss challenge: 300 health, 1 defense, 10 attack. In the boss challenge he has 1200 health, 2 defense and 24 attack.
  • His body takes up 3×2 tiles. Collisions and hits count for the whole body.
  • How he pursues. He spots opponents within 12 tiles; for the moves centered on himself (the thorn crowns), he first walks closer if the opponent is out of range; the moves aimed at the opponent (the arrows, the volley, the line of thorns) he can use as long as he can see them. He never goes too far from his field (12 tiles).
  • Skills only. He has no ordinary attack; all his damage comes from his skills.
  • Camera and music. Once the fight starts, the camera pulls back and holds over the battlefield, and the boss music starts; both return to normal when he falls or the player goes far away.

Strategy

  • Prepare first. There is a waypoint statue in front of the ring of thorns: wake it first, and when you go down you get up again here (on normal difficulty); you can also change Lumi's Boons beside it.
  • Clear the wolves first. There are two wolves inside the ring of thorns as well; best deal with them first.
  • Watch the marks on the ground. Fennick's words are the key: “Stand inside the ring, or well outside it, but never on it. And when he draws his bow, don't be in front of him.” The volley lands only on five points: when you see the marks, step off the tile you are on, and do not dodge onto the four outer landing points.
  • Do not grind away right next to him. Standing beside him all the time earns you Hilt Rebuffs. Hit once, step back once.
  • Do not force your way through the line of thorns. None Cross Unkneeling cuts the arena in two: go round it, cut open a tile, or set it on fire. Force your way through and you cross on your knees.
  • In phase 2, in first, then out. For the thorn crowns that go inner ring then outer ring: first stand in the band 3 to 4 tiles out; the outer ring only comes after the inner one lands.
  • The second arrow. In phase 2 the Arrow of Conquest comes twice, the second aimed afresh as the first lands: after dodging the first, do not step straight back onto your old line.
  • Strike in the gaps. After each move he pauses for a while; that is when to hit him.
  • By design, Chapter 1's numbers let a player of any profession, around level 5, who knows how to dodge the marks, win in 20 to 40 seconds.

  • 4 Iron Ore.
  • If the player has already chosen a profession, he also drops one Legendary Arm of that profession: two for each profession, and he drops the first that has not yet appeared in the world.

After he falls (none of this happens in the boss challenge):

  • In a story scene, he kneels, takes off the crown with his own hands and holds it out to the player, who receives the Crown of Thorns.
  • The cold light spilling from the crown flows into Lumi, and she gains her first Boon, “Crown's Insight” (double the experience you gain).
  • The Grey Man appears, gathers the rest of the light into his lantern, and leaves eastward.
  • The nights grow longer: nightfall moves forward from 21:00 to 20:00, and daybreak is put back to 05:30. See Day and Night.
  • The road to Emberfall opens.
  • In the briar where he fell, one stem is still green: the player gets the Oath-Thorn, with which you can enter The Held Ground.

Lore

Alberic is modeled on the rider of the white horse at the First Seal of Revelation, bearing for Astra the share of suffering that is the Crown of Thorns (see Seal-bearers, Instruments of the Sufferings). On its face, the Crown of Thorns means “giving up human pride and will”; what it really does is wear down the bearer's sense of self. After three hundred years (a proposal of the Chapter 1 design), all Alberic remembers is that he must “hold”: he keeps the end of the East Road as his kingdom and tore down the little bridge at the Ford, so that nothing which will not kneel can get past. He is not cruel; he simply no longer knows what he is holding back.

In life he was Knight-Commander of the Order of the Thorn, a man who would halt a whole column to kneel on one knee and talk to a child. The roll in the The Ossuary of the Thorn reads: “Alberic, Knight-Commander, who knelt to children. Taken up by the Holy City to bear the Crown. He holds.” Fennick carried his lance for six years as a young man; by then he had worn the crown for two hundred and fifty years, and still he would halt the column to talk to a child. On the altar of the The Warden's Sanctum his vow is carved: “I will hold until I am relieved.”

What defeating him means (click to show)

The five Seal-bearers are the five fulcrums holding up Astra, the “Vessel”. Defeating the White Knight both ends his suffering and takes away one fulcrum, which is why the nights really do grow longer. The Grey Man tells the player: “Every seal you break makes the night longer. Ask your Church why.” See Main Story.

As he falls he says: “Take it. It was never mine to hold.” He dies as a man, not as a monster. Afterwards he keeps his vow turned the other way round, becoming the keeper of the Held Ground; see Alberic.

Art

His look comes from the team's concept art: a sealed ivory great helm with the Crown of Thorns grown right through it; a ragged white surcoat with gold trim and old bloodstains; a longsword held point down; a bow and a quiver of white-fletched arrows on his back. In the game he is a top-down pixel sprite, 128 pixels per cell, displayed at 1.25× scale, drawn facing down, right and up (left is right mirrored).

Seven actions: idle, walk, planting the sword (Thorns Bend the Knee), level shot (Arrow of Conquest), high shot (White-Feather Volley), hit, and fall. The fall goes: he kneels, takes off the crown, holds it out, and collapses, the crown coming to rest beside his gauntlet. The actions play at 20 frames per second. The images were generated with image tools from the team's prompts; on export they were only cropped and aligned, never redrawn. See Art.

Who he was (clues on the Pilgrim's Road)

  • The statue in the courtyard of the Abbey of the Thorn: the young Alberic kneeling on one knee before a child, holding out a mended wooden horse — the day he was knighted.
  • His diary (the abbey hall): mending a kite for the miller's daughter; being chosen to wear the Crown of Thorns, “Forty years, and then I am to be relieved. I can hold for forty years”; in the forty-first year no one came, “Today I could not remember Hugh's face” (Hugh was his brother by blood; Tomas the Warden was his sworn brother in the Order); at the end only “Hold. Hold.”
  • The Order's roll in the The Chapter Hall: forty-one names, the last “Alberic, Knight-Commander — Bearer of the Crown”, and “No knight was made after him”.
  • The Writ of Relief: it says another shall be sent to relieve him after forty years, but it was never sent — “No one could be found who would bear it. Let him go on holding.”
  • Father Caius, as you take your vow: “The Seal-bearer has held that road for three hundred years. ...He was due to be relieved, once. That is not a pilgrim's concern.”
  • The kneeling stones of The Kneeling Field: the pilgrims who would not kneel to him before, each stone carved with a name and the year of his bearing the Crown.
  • The white flags along The Pilgrim's Road grow more ragged the farther east you go, and the last is only rags with a needle still stuck through one, its thread wound round and round it; Brother Ezra once saw him mending flags on the Kneeling Field, and when he saw someone his hands stopped and he stood a long while holding the needle.
  • Wynn's nursery rhyme: “he kneels to the small and he stands for the weak”; it ends “and when they come, he kneels again.”

History

  • 2026-10-03: The boss challenge started out with seven bosses from the Seven Sins setting.
  • 2026-10-05: The Seven Sins setting was withdrawn; the White Knight was added as the first Seal-bearer, with his skills and top-down art finished.
  • 2026-10-06: Placed in the kingdom of thorns on the East Road, with weaker numbers for Chapter 1 (300 health, skill damage at 40%); the art grew into a library of 32 frames per action, from which 8 to 12 frames are picked for playback.
  • 2026-10-07: He leaves the Oath-Thorn when he falls, and his human form becomes the keeper of the Held Ground.
  • 2026-10-12: The Pilgrim's Road was added: to reach his kingdom you first pass through the Pilgrims' Rest, the Pilgrim's Road and the Kneeling Field; the abbey holds his statue, his diary and the Writ of Relief.
  • 2026-10-13: He has held for three hundred years; Fennick was his squire when he had worn the crown two hundred and fifty years; the brother in his diary was renamed Hugh.
  • 2026-10-15: Redesigned: two phases, the new moves None Cross Unkneeling (briar sprouts), Hilt Rebuff and Hold., the double Arrow of Conquest, and the inner and outer rings; a hit from the thorn crowns now leaves you kneeling; the campaign's numbers are now worked out from attack (attack 10).
