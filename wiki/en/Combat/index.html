Combat

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay

Combat is in real time: hold Space to strike what is in front of you, or click an enemy you point at with a weapon in hand. There are no turns and no skill bar; the combat skills you learn all work by themselves (except the dash on Q). Felling an enemy gives as much experience as its maximum health (see Levels and Experience).

For who attacks whom, see Factions and Temper; for where monsters rise at night, Peaceful and Combat Zones and Night Creatures; for boss fights, Bosses.

How to fight

  • Space: strike once the way you face. Hold it down to keep striking.
  • Left click: swing what is in your hand. With a weapon, clicking a creature strikes it, and a tool is swung the same way; with an empty hand you strike with the weapon in your main hand, or with a fist if there is none, and an empty hand does not strike loose things on the ground. Hold it to keep striking. With anything else in hand (a stone, food…), the left click uses it on yourself and never strikes with it. To hand something to a creature, hold it and right-click the creature: a rope or hay binds it for 3 seconds, something edible it swallows, and anything else is answered by that kind of creature itself (see Handing things to creatures).
  • A left click on a friendly person first asks “Attack so-and-so?”, and strikes only on “Yes”; once you draw your weapon with X, or once a fight is under way, it no longer asks (see Friendly people are asked about first). Right-clicking a person with a weapon in hand uses the weapon on them (asked first too), which no rule defines: something the world has not seen; with an empty hand the right click does nothing.
  • What Space swings need not be a weapon: when what you hold is not a weapon and no weapon is equipped in the main hand, Space swings whatever is in your hand, as a blunt blow, with the force of your attack plus its power (most things have no power).
  • Ranged weapons (bows, staves, wands) shoot along the row or column you face, hitting the first person or thing in the way. When you click a distant target with the left button, it must be in the same row or column as you.

Attack

The force of a blow = attack + the power of the weapon in hand.

  • The player starts with attack 2, defense 0 and health 40.
  • Attack and defense also come from what you wear (see Equipment), the skills you learn (see Skills) and Lumi's powers.
  • A weapon's power = the weapon itself × its material multiplier × its quality multiplier. For example, an Iron Sword 6, an Iron Dagger 3, a Wooden Bow 2 (see Substances, Quality).
  • Bare-handed you strike with your body, power 0, only your attack.

Cooldown

  • The player's blows are 0.5 seconds apart.
  • Attack speed bonuses (Quick Hands, Battle Tempo, Quick Draw, and those some weapons carry) shorten the gap, by at most 50%.
  • Monsters strike every 1.4 seconds in melee, every 2.2 seconds at range.

Range

Range depends on what is in your hand:

In hand Range
Empty, most weapons and tools 1 cell
Spear, halberd 2 cells
Staff 4 cells
Wand 5 cells
Bow 6 cells

The Ranger's Archery gives bows +1 range; the Mage's Archmage gives staves +1 range. Legendary weapons reach farther; see Legendary Arms.

How damage is worked out

A blow goes through these steps in turn:

  1. Force: attack + weapon power, plus the bonus of profession training (+1 for an Assassin with a dagger, for example).
  2. Situational bonuses: the percentage bonuses of the various skills are added together (First Strike, Backstab, the chains, Executioner…), then multiplied in.
  3. Critical: a critical multiplies by 1.5; by 2 with Deadly Focus learned; Lethal Edge and Brutal each add another 0.25. The critical is counted before defense.
  4. Efficiency: the weapon's efficiency against the target's material. A creature's body is flesh: blades, blunt weapons, bows and spells are all 1, hoes and shovels only 0.25 (see Durability and Breaking).
  5. Elemental resistance: damage dealt by an element is reduced by resistance.
  6. Defense: the target's defense is subtracted, leaving at least 1.
  7. The target's response: a dodge (the whole blow misses, “Dodged!”), a shield block (“Blocked!”), then the various reductions multiplied in (Evasive Step, Defiance, Bulwark, Mana Shield…), leaving at least 1. Dodge and block are each at most 50%.

Last come a few lifesavers: Last Stand leaves 1 health after a fatal blow, and Phoenix, once a day, leaves 30% health after fatal damage.

Critical chance comes from skills (Keen Eye, Precision…), weapons and what you wear. Someone with no critical chance never lands a critical.

Elements

When a weapon carries an element, whoever it hits gets the element, which reacts by the rules of Elements: fire sets it burning, ice freezes what is wet, lightning shocks what is wet, wind blows it back 2 cells. A frozen enemy hit by a blunt weapon or the earth element takes damage × 1.5; an enemy bound with rope or hay counts as frozen too.

Wind is one of the few things in combat that can push someone back; the other is bosses' skills. Ordinary blows have no knockback.

Profession weapons

Dagger, Sword (and sabres), Shield, Bow and Staff are profession weapons:

  • Only someone of the matching profession can fight with one: anyone else who strikes with it is told only “Only a(n) X can fight with Y.”, and the blow does not land. Only a Warrior can put on a shield.
  • A profession's talents work only with its own profession's weapons.
  • Anyone can hold them, give them away or put them in a chest.

Axes, clubs, maces, warhammers, spears, halberds and wands are common weapons: anyone can use them, but they take no profession talent. Before you choose a profession you can fight only with common weapons and tools; the Stone Axe (power 5) is the best weapon you can make at the start. See Professions.

On screen

  • Health bars: a thin health bar shows over a creature's head once it has been struck, or when it is hostile to the player. Red: will attack the player; green: people and companions; yellow: everything else (animals that do not attack people, struck trees, walls and other such things). Bosses have a large health bar of their own; see Bosses.
  • Floating numbers: every loss of health floats a “-N” over the head, colored by element: fire orange, ice pale blue, lightning yellow, water blue, wind pale green, earth brown, plain blows white. Healing floats a green “+N”.

After a fall

  • Monsters: when one falls, “X is defeated.” is shown; it leaves half of what it is made of (most monsters have no makeup and leave nothing), and what it carried drops where it fell. What it leaves lies still; press E to take it. Whoever felled it gains experience.
  • People, Lumi, the player: never die, only go down. A resident who is down wakes after 30 seconds with half health; no one strikes someone who is down. When the player falls, the death screen appears; see Difficulty.

Tips

  • The best weapon at the start is the Stone Axe: power 5, and it fells trees too.
  • Check whether an enemy has a red health bar: yellow ones will not attack the player of their own accord.
  • Lure enemies onto wet ground, or douse them with water, then use lightning, for the best effect.
  • When chased at night, run beside a lit campfire: monsters never come within 2 cells of a fire (except fire creatures).

History

  • 2026-10-03: Combat skills (the three branches Swiftness, Survival and Damage, and the Assassin) work by themselves; attack speed, criticals, dodge, Executioner and other bonuses went live.
  • 2026-10-04: Creatures take blows by their material: weapon power = weapon × material × quality; elements got resistances; armor can be worn (W8).
  • 2026-10-05: Profession weapons for their own profession only, profession talents count only their own weapons; Legendary weapons.
  • 2026-10-06: Health bars colored by hostility.
  • 2026-10-09: Anything swung with Space counts as a blunt blow; left-clicking a creature while holding something that is not a weapon hands it over, and rope or hay can bind it.
  • 2026-10-21: The controls settled on four inputs: using a held thing on something moved to the right click (the left click is the held thing's own use: a tool or weapon swung, anything else used on oneself), and picking up loose things moved to E (see Controls).
