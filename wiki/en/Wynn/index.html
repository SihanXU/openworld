“The White Knight rides, the White Knight bends, he mends your kite and he minds your friends, he kneels to the small and he stands for the weak—”

— Wynn

Wynn is Maud's daughter, 8 years old, and she will ask anything. She sings the White Knight's rhyme her grandmother taught her, about the days when the White Knight still knelt to children. Her grandmother never sang it to the end, saying “the end went wrong”.

By day she plays by the well at the Rest, and at night she stays in the hostel. After the White Knight falls, she says how the rhyme ends: “and when they come, he kneels again.”
