Building

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay

Building is putting a thing into a cell of the world: a wall, a floor, a bed, a campfire. The usual way is Building mode: press B to open the building panel (its title reads “BUILD MODE”), choose a thing, point at a cell beside you, and press the left button or E to put it there. You can build a house without Building mode too: hold a wall you have made and right-click a cell to build it in that cell, or hold the right button and drag to build a row. This is the same thing that Building mode puts down. Pressing G only puts it on the ground: a wall or bed put down that way lies loose there like any ordinary thing and is not built (press E to pick it up again). See Placing from your hand.

For houses of several storeys, how walls stack, ceilings and stairs, see Storeys. For when a house counts as a room, see Rooms.

Placing from your hand

Walls, floors, doors, stairs, beds, workbenches and the like are in your bag once made, and you can place them straight from your hand:

  • Right-click an empty cell beside you, a floor, or something already built (a wall on a wall, a floor on a wall), and it is built there.
  • Hold the right button and drag, and one is built in every cell the mouse passes; it does nothing where it passes trees, water or people.
  • A translucent preview under the mouse shows exactly where it will be drawn once built, and how big.
  • Holding a floor, press R and the right click lays a ceiling instead; press it again to switch back. You can also Alt+right-click and choose “Ceiling” among the levels listed.
  • Things with a direction (stairs, fences) are built facing the player: stairs climb away from the player, and a fence runs across in front of the player. The arrow on the preview is the way it faces.
  • Right-click the cell you are standing on, and things that can share a cell with a person, such as beds and floors, are built under your feet; anything else is used on yourself.
  • G does not build: a wall or bed put down with G only lies loose in that cell, and E picks it up again.
  • Holding one and right-clicking water, a tree or a person is something for the world to learn; the prompt ends with “?”.

Building mode

B opens the panel; B again, Esc or the right button closes it. While it is open:

  • The world's time runs as usual; it does not pause the way the menu does.
  • WASD walk as usual. Space does not attack; the left button places things, and the right button leaves the panel.
  • Only your own storey is drawn; the storeys above are hidden for now (see Storeys).
  • The panel closes by itself when a story scene starts, a question pops up, or a chest is opened.

The panel has two pages:

  • Structures: first the things to “put down” — Wall, Floor, Stairs, Door, the wooden bed, the wooden workbench, and anything else you carry that can be put down; then the things to “build up” — Campfire, Crate, Wooden Chest, Fence, Sign.
  • Farming: Till Soil, sow (one entry each for carrot, turnip, tomato and pumpkin), Water, Refill Water, Harvest. The rules of planting are in Farming.

Things put down

Walls, floors, stairs, doors, beds and workbenches are things you can hold in your hand.

  • If you carry one, that one is built, the same as right-clicking with it in hand. The panel says “N on you”.
  • If you carry none, one is first made from the materials you carry and then put down (“Make one from what you carry, then put it down”). The list of materials is at the bottom of the panel; any you are short of turn red.
  • Once built, knock it with an axe, pickaxe or hammer (left click) and it comes loose whole where it stood; press E to pick it up (close by, it is drawn into your bag by itself), with no materials lost.

Things built up

Campfires, crates, chests, fences and signs are built up step by step on an empty cell. Building mode does these steps for the player:

Thing Materials Tool to carry Use
campfire 2 stones, 2 sticks None Lit, it cooks things, and at night monsters dare not come near
crate 2 planks Hammer A 6-slot storage box
chest 4 planks Hammer A 12-slot storage box
fence 2 sticks Hammer Blocks the way; placed in a run, it fences in a garden
sign 1 stick, 1 plank Hammer A blank sign
  • The hammer means the Smith Hammer; in the crafting panel, 2 stones and 1 stick make a stone smith hammer. The hammer is not used up.
  • Only the materials in your bag and hands count; those in a chest beside you do not.
  • Before building, the game tries every step in an unseen copy of the world first; if any step fails, not a single material is taken (“The world's rules as they stand do not support this recipe; no materials were taken.”).
  • A chest you have built is opened with E after leaving Building mode.

How to place

  • You can only place what you can reach. Only within the ring of cells around you (1 cell). Solid things such as walls and doors cannot go on the cell you stand on; a floor can be laid under your feet.
  • Left button or E places one.
  • Hold the left button and drag, and one goes in every cell the mouse passes: a row of walls or of floor is laid in one stroke.
  • R: with something directional chosen, turns it: the arrow of stairs points the way up, and a fence stands upright when its arrow points left or right and runs across when it points up or down; with a floor chosen, switches between “lay on this storey” and “lay as a ceiling”; see Ceilings.
  • When the mouse points at nothing, it goes in the cell in front of you.

Preview

The cell the mouse points at shows a translucent preview, drawn where the thing will be drawn once placed:

  • “Blocks” such as walls, doors and stairs are drawn two cells high: the lower cell is the front, the upper the top.
  • A floor lies flat on its cell.
  • A ceiling is drawn one cell higher, right under the mouse.
  • Green if it can be placed, red if it cannot. The bottom line of the panel gives the reason.

Things that are “built up”, such as campfires and chests, can be built on empty ground on your own storey, or on a floor already laid.

Taking down

  • Knocking loose: knock a wall, floor, stairs, door, bed or workbench with an axe, pickaxe or hammer (left click), and it comes loose whole and falls where it stood (anything kept in it spills out); close by it is drawn into your bag by itself, or press E to pick it up, and the whole piece is back with you. An upstairs floor with something on it (a wall, a bed, a person) cannot be knocked loose; take what is on top away first. Take away a wall that holds up a floor, and the floor that has lost its support falls in (see Storeys).
  • Breaking: hitting it with a weapon only wears down its durability, and when that reaches 0 only half of what it was made of comes back (see Durability and Breaking).
  • Chests: knock an unlocked crate or wooden chest with an axe, pickaxe or hammer (left click), and what is inside scatters out, and the box comes loose whole where it stood; press E to pick it up (close by, it is drawn into your bag by itself), then right-click to build it somewhere else. The rules have locks and the Key: a key can lock and open a chest, and a locked chest cannot be knocked loose, only worn down. But the game has no key lying anywhere and no locked chest, so this does not come up yet.
  • Campfires: a campfire is stone; bare hands get “Too hard”; use a pickaxe.

Building by hand

Everything Building mode “builds up” is really materials put together one by one and then nailed with a hammer. You can do it step by step yourself; every step is a G to put down or a left-click knock with a hammer in hand:

Put down first Then put You get Knock with a hammer
Plank A plank on it Wooden frame (2 planks) 2 planks: a crate
Wooden frame More planks, up to 4 A frame of 3 or 4 planks 4 planks: a chest; 3 planks: “One plank short.”
Stick A stick on it Stakes A fence
Stone A stone on it Stone ring — (put 2 sticks in it and it becomes a campfire)
Stick A plank on it Sign post A blank sign
  • Press E at a frame and it tells you how many planks it has and what it can still be made into.
  • Unfinished things can be taken apart again with nothing lost: an axe takes apart a wooden frame, stakes and a sign post, a pickaxe a stone ring.
  • The rule for a blank sign: carrying an Ink Pot, write on it with a Quill, and from then on it reads “Handmade.” But the game has no quill or ink pot lying anywhere, so nothing can be written yet.
  • A plank put on shallow water lays a stretch of wooden bridge.

Tips

  • The order for your first house: a ring of walls and a door → a bed inside → in Building mode choose a floor, press R to switch to ceiling, and lay the roof. The sixth lesson of the tutorial teaches exactly this.
  • When dragging to lay floor or ceiling, walk along with it: every cell has to be within 1 cell of you.
  • To have both a floor and a bed on one cell: placing the bed first and the floor after works too; the floor is laid under the bed.

History

  • 2026-09-30: First version of building: planks, sticks and stones put together make frames, stakes, stone rings and posts, which a hammer nails into chests, fences, campfires and signs.
  • 2026-10-03: Building mode (B) added, with campfires, crates, wooden chests, fences, signs and a planting page.
  • 2026-10-04: Walls, floors and stairs became things you can hold and put down; upper floors need support (W6).
  • 2026-10-05: Building mode can place walls, floors, doors, beds and workbenches, making them on the spot when you carry none; hold the left button and drag to place a row. R turns stairs. Later each storey builds only its own storey, and a ceiling has to be chosen with R; where you point is where it is built and where it is drawn.
  • 2026-10-07: Campfires, chests and the like can be built on a floor already laid; a bed put down is drawn whole.
  • 2026-10-08: Held in your hand, a left click places it, and holding and dragging places a row; holding a floor, R lays a ceiling.
  • 2026-10-08: Fences can be turned with R; stairs and fences placed outside Building mode face the player.
  • 2026-10-21: The controls settled into four inputs: using a held thing on something moved to the right click (the left click is the held thing's own use: a tool or weapon swings, anything else is used on yourself), and picking up loose things moved to E (see Controls).
