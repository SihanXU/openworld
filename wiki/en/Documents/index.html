Documents

From the OpenWorld Wiki · List
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

Documents are items with writing on them: the Book, the Scroll, the Letter, the Map, and Chapter 1's Proclamation of the Pilgrimage and Almanac of the First Night. They are made of paper and can burn.

Much of the world's writing is not on items but on things that stand: signs, notice boards, gravestones, altars, chalk drawings on the ground. Walk up to one and press E to read it; a sign can be read even from 2 cells away. What you read appears on the screen.

Hold a document with writing on it and left-click to use it on yourself (label “Read …”), and its words appear in the messages at the bottom left. Those with writing at present are the Proclamation of the Pilgrimage (full text at the text of the proclamation), the Almanac of the First Night, the Writ of Relief, and Alberic's four journal pages in the Chapter Hall (full text at the Chapter Hall). Other books, scrolls, letters and maps hold nothing written, and the rules do not define reading them; an undefined act on a document is left to the world learns.

Things to read in the world

Below are the texts that can be read with E around Chapter 1 (the game's own words).

Around Firstlight Village

  • Astra's altar (Firstlight Village, the altar beside the flame): “ASTRA, THE FIRST MARTYR. He bore the Five, and the sun rose. Suffering is not God's abandonment. It is the proof the debt is being paid.”
  • The sign by the hot spring (Firstlight Village): “Warm spring — sit a while, and it mends you.”
  • Millbrook's sign (Millbrook): “MILLBROOK — flour and lamp oil for Firstlight.”
  • The Dawnstone (the five-wound shrine stone on Dawn Hill): “Cut deep, and old: HERE THE SUN ROSE AGAIN. Below, newer: five small wounds, and a flame.”
  • The first hearth (the altar of the Chapel of the First Dawn): “The chapel's first hearth, a ring of stone, cold for centuries. Old soot is still packed deep in its cracks.”

Pell's chalk drawings

Four chalk drawings of the grey man, the things the quest Pell's Drawings has you find. They are drawn on the ground; you have to look down.

Drawing Where What it shows
chalk drawing of a stone Mountain Foot, by the shrine stone “The shrine stone, five marks on it, the first scribbled over in black. A grey figure with its hand on it.”
chalk drawing of a door At the door of the Old Barrow “A door, shut, a hand-mark on it. Behind it, something with a crown of bones. In front, two small people.”
chalk drawing of a bridge The Ford, by the little bridge “A bridge with its middle gone. A tall figure in white beside it, and a smaller grey one watching from the trees.”
chalk drawing of a grey man Firstlight Village, beside the flame “A hooded man with a lantern, beside a fire. Underneath, in a child's letters: HE WAS HERE FIRST.”

The three levels of the Old Barrow

  • The carved standing stones (Thornwood Hollow, the gravestones on either side of the Old Barrow's entrance): “Worn carving: a knight with a crown of thorns, and before him a line of small figures, kneeling. Beneath: THE ORDER OF THE THORN HOLDS.”
  • The Order's niches (The Ossuary of the Thorn, four of them):
    • “BROTHER ODO OF THE THORN. He held the gate at Varn three days. He did not kneel.”
    • “SISTER MAREN OF THE THORN, who carried water to both armies.”
    • “BROTHER TOMAS OF THE THORN, the Commander's brother. Warden of this house until the night comes back.”
    • “Carved, and left empty: FENNICK, SQUIRE TO THE COMMANDER. The stone is clean. Someone keeps it swept.”
  • The Order's roll (the altar of the Ossuary of the Thorn): “THE ORDER OF THE THORN. Forty-one names, the oldest worn smooth. The last, cut deeper than the rest: ALBERIC, KNIGHT-COMMANDER, WHO KNELT TO CHILDREN. TAKEN UP BY THE HOLY CITY TO BEAR THE CROWN. HE HOLDS.”
  • The knight-commander's vow (the altar of the The Warden's Sanctum): “Cut into the altar, in a knight's plain hand: "I WILL HOLD UNTIL I AM RELIEVED. — ALBERIC." Below it, newer, pressed into the soot with a thumb: a mark of ash.”

The camp in the Forest

  • The proclamation nailed to a tree (the southeast of the Forest, a notice board): “BY ORDER OF THE HOLY CITY. The Seal-bearers weaken. Let the pilgrimage be called. Brother Cassian of Firstlight is named pilgrim. — In the 380th year of the Five. (The paper is grey with age. The wording is almost the same as the courier's.)”
  • A cairn of stones (a gravestone in the same place): “Stones piled over something long. A pilgrim's staff leans on it, its tip worn flat by many miles. No name.”

Cloudtop and the East Road

  • The watchman's log (a table on Cloudtop): see Story Items.
  • The kneeling stones (The East Road, in the knight's kingdom, five of them): “A stone carved as a pilgrim kneeling. No name. A scallop shell, the pilgrim's mark, worn almost away.” They are carved of those who would not kneel to the knight.

The Pilgrim's Road

What can be read at the Pilgrims' Rest, on the Pilgrim's Road, in the Abbey of the Thorn and on the Kneeling Field: the Holy See's notice at The Pilgrims' Rest, the five-wound altar and the litany of the Five Sufferings in the Chapel of the Five, the pilgrims' register in The Pilgrims' Hostel, the verses of the wayside shrines, the statue and the memorial stones of the Abbey of the Thorn, the Order's roll in the The Chapter Hall, the abbots' niches in the The Abbey Crypt, the kneeling stones and the scarecrows of the The Kneeling Field. The texts are on each page.

The Held Ground

  • The planted sword (the middle of The Held Ground): “Alberic's longsword, driven point down into the earth, a little crooked. Its grip is wrapped in new cloth.”
  • The boundary stones (the four sides of the Held Ground): “A boundary stone. Its carving is worn: a ring of thorns, and nothing inside the ring.”

For what is written on signs and notice boards, see Sign and Notice Board.
