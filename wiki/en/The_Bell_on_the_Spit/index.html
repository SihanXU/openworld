The Bell on the Spit is a Chapter 1 side quest given by Hilde, the fishwife of The Reed Bend. Nine winters ago her husband Rolf took the boat out into the fog; the boat came back on its own, and he did not. The boat's bell lies on the spit where the boat ran aground, held for nine years by water slimes. She does not go there, and asks you to.

The quest has no prerequisites.

“Rolf's Boat Bell” is a Bell; Hilde keeps it when you hand it in (she says she will hang it on the door).

Walkthrough

  1. Take the quest: go to the Reed Bend and find Hilde, who stands by the jetty by day (5:00–22:00); press E on her and choose “Accept ‘The Bell on the Spit’” in the dialogue box.
  2. Onto the spit: the spit is south of the jetty (rows 20–22), a strip of sand reaching out into the river. There are 3 water slimes on the spit, which attack on sight; once defeated, another may come onto the sand every 1800 seconds.
  3. Pick up the bell: the bell is at the very end of the spit; press E to pick it up.
  4. Hand in: go back to Hilde and press E to hand in.

Rewards

  • Experience +35 (Levels and Experience)
  • Grilled Fish×2
  • Rolf's Net Ring (Fine): Ring, attack 2, level 2, luck 0.05
  • Regard Hilde +20 (Friendship)
  • Karma +3 (Karma and Faith)

“Rolf's Net Ring” gives +5% luck. After you hand in, Hilde has one more line she says only once.

Dialogue

“The spit, south of the pier. Bronze, the size of your fist. Mind the slimes; you will come back soaked.”
On handing in
“It rings the same. I thought it would sound different. ...This was his ring. He mended nets with it on. You keep it. I can't wear it, and I won't sell it.”
The same sound (click to show)

When you hand in, Hilde says: “It still sounds the same. I thought it would have changed.” She gives you the ring Rolf wore when mending nets — it only works on a hand that goes places. Afterwards she says she has hung the bell on the door “so he knows which house is ours”. In the catch book on the table in her cabin, the year nine winters ago has only one line: “The boat came back. He didn't. Fog.”
