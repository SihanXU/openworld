The Long Night

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Lore

The Long Night is the calamity in which the sun gradually vanishes and the world sinks into an endless dark. The first Long Night, several hundred years ago, was ended by Astra; the people in the game call that one “the First Long Night”. As the game begins, the days are growing shorter again, and the Church has declared that the Long Night is coming back.

The first Long Night (the First Long Night)

Long ago the sun gradually vanished, and the days grew shorter and shorter until the whole world sank into an endless dark. Crops withered, beasts turned strange, the dead found no rest, and human civilization was all but destroyed.

People tried nearly everything: war, magic, sacrifice and prayer, and none of it truly stopped it. At the worst of the Long Night, the saint Astra appeared and willingly bore five holy torments (the Five Sufferings). When the fifth torment was complete, the sun rose again and the Long Night ended.

In the game, Sister Ilse of Firstlight Village tells the story like this:

Long ago the days grew shorter and shorter, until one morning the sun did not rise at all. The crops died. The beasts changed. The dead found no rest. That was the First Night.

The morning the First Long Night ended has left several place names and relics in the game:

  • Firstlight Village takes its name from the first dawn after the First Long Night. The village's fire (the First Fire) is said to have been taken from that day's first ray of dawn.
  • The dawnstone on Dawn Hill is carved “HERE THE SUN ROSE AGAIN”.
  • The Chapel of the First Dawn holds “the first fire altar”: the First Fire was lit here from the dawn light and then carried down the hill. The bell rung that morning still hangs in the bell tower.
  • The Almanac of the First Night is an old almanac from before the First Long Night.

The nature of the Long Night

The original lore text says the Long Night is not a disaster made by some demon lord. It is the result of the First Night seeping back into reality, closer to an unavoidable, cyclical breakdown of the world itself. The signs come in this order: dusk comes earlier, dawn later, the days shorten, the noon sunlight grows pale, plants and animals behave strangely, the dead find no rest, and creatures that do not belong to reality appear at night.

The Long Night is coming back

When the game begins, several hundred years have passed since the first Long Night. Lately the world has turned strange again:

  • dusk comes ever earlier;
  • dawn comes ever later;
  • the days grow shorter every month;
  • some remote regions go days on end without seeing the sun;
  • the number of monsters at night has begun to grow;
  • there are signs that the dead cannot rest;
  • some creatures known only from the records of the first Long Night have appeared again.

People gradually realize that the Long Night is coming back. The Church of the Five Sufferings declares that the five Seal-bearers can no longer go on bearing the Five Sufferings; once the five Seals fail completely, the Second Long Night will come. So the Church calls the pilgrimage.

The Proclamation of the Pilgrimage the Church Courier reads out in the game is:

By order of the Holy City: the Seal-bearers weaken, and the Long Night returns. Let the pilgrimage be called.

In the game

  • The sun sets earlier and earlier: Nessa Quill keeps a table of sunset times and says the sun sets earlier every month; the log of the Church's Sunwatch Post on Cloudtop records years of sunset times, each year earlier than the last (see The Watchman's Log).
  • The nights really do grow longer: this is a rule the team decided: each Seal-bearer who falls makes the night longer. So far the game has only the first Seal-bearer. After you defeat Alberic, the White Knight, night begins at 20:00 instead of 21:00, and day breaks at 05:30 instead of 05:00. When you return to Firstlight Village, Nessa says, “The sun set four minutes early tonight.” See Day and Night.
  • At night zombies and bats appear in the wilds; see Night Creatures.
Why the nights are lengthening (click to show)
  • In the first reversal, the protagonist comes to think the nights are lengthening because the sealed Astra is coming back to life. The Grey Man says only half of it in Chapter 1: “Every seal you break makes the night longer. Ask your Church why.”
  • The second reversal reveals that the nights are lengthening because Astra can barely hold any longer: the cage that he is is breaking (§10).
  • The Seal-bearers are the five Fulcrums of the vessel that is Astra. In defeating them, the protagonist is also dismantling the Fulcrums, so the further the journey goes, the worse the night may become (§12).
  • In the preview ending, the protagonist takes Astra's place as the new Nightbearer. The sun rises over the world again, the ever-lengthening nights end, and people celebrate that “the Second Long Night was stopped” (§16).
