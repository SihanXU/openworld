Bosses

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

A boss is a strong enemy with a set of moves of its own. Its moves are the same thing as monster skills: the cells to be struck are marked on the ground first (the wind-up), and the blow lands a moment later; stand outside the marks and it cannot hit you. The only difference is how a move is picked: minor monsters use moves as they come, by distance and cooldown, while a boss takes them in turn, in the order its “phase” sets; the lower its health, the later the phase, and the faster and harder its moves.

Two bosses are in the campaign so far: Alberic, the White Knight of Chapter 1, and the dragon of Cinder Peak in the Dragon's Lair. The other three can be fought only in the Boss Challenge.

The Velvet Lure, the Face Thief and the Crown Without a King are three bosses left from the earlier “seven deadly sins” setting, not yet put into the campaign; how the second to fifth Seal-bearers play is to be designed.

Moves: the marked cells

  • The boss walks toward the nearest enemy (one of “the people” faction; see Factions and Temper), begins a move once it can reach, and calls out the move's name over its head.
  • Wind-up: every cell the move will strike is marked on the ground in the boss's own color, the marks growing brighter. Below the boss's large health bar it says “Wind-up / Evade”, and the blow lands when the progress bar fills.
  • Landing: every enemy inside the marks is struck once (a move counts once per person, however many cells their body takes up). Cells outside the marks are entirely safe.
  • Recovery: after the blow lands the boss rests a while (recovery), then uses its next move.
  • Where it aims is fixed as the wind-up begins: a move aimed at a target strikes where the target stood when the wind-up began; walk away and you dodge it.
  • Chains: some moves follow straight on with another as they land (a second arrow, a second wave of fire); the follow-up aims afresh at where you stand when its own wind-up begins. Having dodged the first, do not hurry back.

How a boss picks its moves

  • Rotation: each phase has a string of moves, used one after another in order, starting over when done.
  • Opening move: on entering a new phase, the boss first uses that phase's opening move (the White Knight's “Hold.”, the dragon's “Call the Brood” and “Ashfall”), only once per fight. Opening moves are “steady”: stunned or frozen, they are not interrupted, only delayed.
  • Answering moves: between two moves, if where you stand happens to meet one move's conditions (right beside it, for example), the boss uses that move first (the White Knight's “Hilt Rebuff”, the dragon's “Tail Sweep” and “Bite”); this does not upset the rotation. Such moves are picked the way minor monsters pick theirs: by distance and cooldown.
  • Interrupting: a rotation or answering move is wasted if the boss is stunned, frozen or bound during its wind-up; the boss pauses, then uses its next move.
  • What stays on the ground: some moves leave things on the cells as they land: the White Knight's briar sprouts, the dragon's embers. They act by their own rules and vanish by themselves after a while.

The shapes of moves:

Shape Where it strikes How to dodge
Line A line three cells wide in front of the boss Step to the side
Cone An area widening in front of the boss Go round to the side or the back
Dots The target's cell, plus four cells a few cells off it up, down, left and right Stand in a gap between the marks
Cross Arms reaching a few cells up, down, left and right from the target's cell Step away diagonally
Ring A ring; inside the ring is safe Step inside the ring, or outside it
Circle A solid area Step out

Some moves also pull people in, push them back a few cells, or leave them Mired (slow for 2.5 seconds), Entranced or kneeling (unable to move for about a second). While held fast you cannot dodge the next move, so step away during the wind-up. Once held fast, a person is steadied for 4.5 seconds: during that time they cannot be held fast again by Entranced, kneeling or stunned, so they cannot be locked down in a chain.

Phases

A boss is divided by health into two or three phases: the large health bar says “I / Arrived”, “II / Awakened”, “III / Fury”. Each phase has its own rotation, and its wind-ups, recoveries and damage can each grow faster or heavier (at least 0.8 seconds of wind-up and 0.6 seconds of recovery). Phases only move forward: a boss that regains health does not go back to an earlier phase. Each boss's phases are on its own page.

The three bosses only for the Boss Challenge (the Velvet Lure, the Face Thief, the Crown Without a King) have only two phases, entering the second at half health:

Phase one Phase two
Wind-up time As is × 0.8 (at least 0.8 seconds)
Recovery time As is × 0.65 (at least 0.6 seconds)
Damage As is × 1.35

The boss's health bar

A boss has no ordinary small health bar but a large one at the top of the screen: it shows “Boss”, the boss's name and current phase, its health in numbers, a trail as health drops, a mark where each phase begins, and the wind-up's progress bar. A Seal-bearer's bar also carries five seals, the lit one being the Seal he bears. When no wind-up is under way, it says below “Watch the wind-up · Strike during recovery”. It appears within 12 cells of the boss.

Camera and music

  • When the boss enters combat (winding up, using a move, being struck) within 12 cells of the player, the camera stops following the player, pulls back and settles on the middle of the battlefield, and the boss music plays.
  • When the boss falls, or the player runs more than 18 cells away, the camera goes back to the player and the map's own music returns.

The boss does not leave the battlefield

A boss keeps to its own place: sight 12 cells, chasing at most 12 cells. If its target runs far off, it gives up and goes back; it never chases into a town or blocks a map's exit.

After it falls

When a boss's health reaches 0, it is down for 2 seconds (its death animation), then vanishes. It leaves:

  • Experience for whoever felled it, equal to its maximum health.
  • 4 Iron Ore, and whatever it carried.
  • If whoever felled it has a profession: of that profession's two Legendary weapons, the first that has not yet appeared in the world. If both have appeared, or there is no profession, nothing.
  • The story records that this boss has fallen.

A boss's every move deals a multiple of its attack (written in its own rules), so in the campaign, with his lower attack, every move is lighter too. For each move's numbers, see The White Knight Alberic.

After the White Knight falls (click to show)

The White Knight kneels and takes off the Crown of Thorns with his own hands to give to the player; Lumi takes up part of the First Seal's power (Crown's Insight). The road to Emberfall opens with it. From then on, nights grow longer: dark comes earlier, at 20:00, and dawn later, at 05:30 (see Day and Night).

Other strong enemies

A few kinds of monster are strong too but fight no boss fight; they are fought like ordinary monsters: barrow warden, cyclops, Slime King. See their own pages.

Tips

  • Watch the ground, not the boss: when the marks appear, step away; strike during the recovery.
  • Inside a ring is safe. When the White Knight plants his sword, walking in toward him is shorter than running out.
  • In phase two the wind-up is only eight-tenths as long, so move early.
  • A boss chases only 12 cells: if you cannot win, run off, eat something and wait for your health to come back before going in again. A boss never heals by itself.
