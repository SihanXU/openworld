The First Night

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Lore
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

The First Night (原夜) is, in the Church of the Five Sufferings setting, a darkness older than the world. The sun keeps light and the First Night apart; when the First Night seeps back into reality, the days grow shorter until at last the sun vanishes, and that is the Long Night.

The Church's theology of creation

The later theology of creation of the Church of the Five Sufferings holds:

God did not truly “create” light. God only separated light from the First Night.

On this account, the sun is not the First Night's opposite but a boundary that keeps the separation in place. That boundary is not stable forever.

The First Night seeps into reality

Over long ages, the First Night seeps back into reality. The signs the original lore text lists are:

  • dusk comes earlier;
  • dawn comes later;
  • the days grow gradually shorter;
  • the noon sunlight grows paler and paler;
  • plants and animals begin to behave strangely;
  • the dead cannot fully rest;
  • creatures that do not belong to the real world appear at night.

If the erosion goes on, the sun at last vanishes altogether, and that is the Long Night. So the Long Night is not a disaster caused by some demon lord; it is closer to an unavoidable, cyclical breakdown of the world itself.

In the game

The game's text does not yet use the word “First Night” for this darkness. The signs visible in Chapter 1 match the list above:

Sign In the game
Dusk comes earlier, the days grow shorter Hesper and Sister Ilse say it gets dark earlier every week; Nessa Quill keeps a table of sunset times; the watchman's log on Cloudtop records years of sunset times, each year earlier than the last.
Pale noon sunlight The first time you walk onto the East Road, Lumi says the sun is lower than it was this morning: “It's only just past noon.”
Plants and animals behave strangely Edda's wheat died because the days were too short; Tam at Millbrook says the brook comes off the hills, “and the hills get less sun.”
The dead find no rest The dead in the Old Barrow and the Ossuary of the Thorn “can't lie down”; when the barrow warden falls it says, “The night... is coming back. I could not... lie down.”
Creatures that appear at night The zombies and bats that roam the wilds at night (see Night Creatures).

Note: the game's English text calls the first Long Night “the First Night”; its Chinese text calls it 初夜. That is not the same thing as the First Night (原夜) of the original lore text, the primordial darkness. On this wiki, “the First Night” means only that primordial darkness, and “the First Long Night” means the first Long Night.

The First Night and Astra (click to show)
  • During the first Long Night, people tried war, magic, sacrifice and prayer, and none of it stopped the First Night. Astra discovered that “the First Night cannot be destroyed,” but that it can be “given a vessel.” If a living being strong enough carries the whole of the First Night within itself, the First Night can no longer invade the world. The Five Sufferings are the rite that remakes an ordinary person into such a vessel (§3).
  • The final truth: Astra locked the First Night inside himself. For centuries the five holy instruments have held him in place and the five Seal-bearers have shared his pain. Now he can barely hold any longer, and the First Night is seeping back into the world through the cracks (§10).
  • “The First Night cannot be killed.” It can only be carried. If the one who carries it dies with no one to take their place, the First Night is released all at once, and the true Long Night swallows the world in a short time (§11). See Nightbearer.
  • In the preview ending, after Astra dies, the First Night within him runs out of control and finally enters the protagonist entirely (§16).
  • The true ending may reinterpret where the First Night really comes from; this is not yet decided (§18). See Ending.
