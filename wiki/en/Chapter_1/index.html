Chapter 1

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Story
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

“The White Knight knelt, and gave up the Crown. The grey man took the light east. And the night came a little sooner.”

— Chapter 1's end card

Chapter 1, “The First Seal: the Crown of Thorns”, is the main story's first chapter, and the one chapter fully built in the game so far. The days grow shorter by the day. The protagonist comes down from the mountain with Lumi and arrives at Firstlight Village, a village named for the first dawn after the First Long Night, whose sacred fire is said to have been taken from that day's first ray of dawn. The footbridge to the east has been torn apart plank by plank by a rider in white wearing a crown of thorns. The protagonist helps the village rescue two people from the barrow; on the night of thanks, a man in grey takes the sacred fire; the next morning, the Church's courier brings the Proclamation of the Pilgrimage. The protagonist crosses the river, takes the Pilgrim's Vow at the Church's Pilgrims' Rest, lights the five shrines along the Pilgrim's Road, reads the knight's past in the abandoned Abbey of the Thorn, crosses the Kneeling Field, finds the first Seal-bearer, Alberic, the White Knight, on the East Road, defeats him, and wins the Crown of Thorns. That evening, the sun sets early.

The game's current version is Part One: it runs until the hero crosses the river and stands on the far bank of the Ford, when the chapter card “Part One · The Ford” appears. The second half of the chapter, from the Pilgrims' Rest to the White Knight, belongs to Part Two; the team has built it, and it waits for Part Two to open (see Part One).

This chapter tells only the first layer of understanding: it never questions the official history and never lies (see Main Story). For a step-by-step walkthrough, see Main Quest Walkthrough.

Walkthrough

Below, the chapter is written section by section in the order actually built in the game. The trigger conditions are in parentheses.

Down from the Mountain

A new game begins on Cloudtop. Go down to the Mountain Foot, past the five-wound shrine stone with its first wound cracked, and on down to the Ford. See Prologue.

The Ford

The first time you reach the Ford, the ferrywoman Hesper comes to meet you:

The storm took my ferry rope. The bridge was a rider. He was all in white, with a crown of thorns grown through his helm. He pulled the planks up with his hands and laid the nails out on my pier in a row, like a toll.

She says there is an old axe by the stumps and three planks would mend the bridge; but two people from Firstlight Village to the west have gone missing, and the bridge can wait, so help them first. She gives the player a piece of bread. Thorns are driven into the two broken stumps of the footbridge (“Thorns driven into the stump”).

Firstlight Village

The first time you enter the village, Sister Ilse welcomes the player by the sacred fire: “the flame was lit from the first dawn after the First Long Night.” The boy Pell wants to draw Lumi; the village head, Rowan, says two people have gone missing and asks you to come to him once you have warmed up. Rowan's quest Lost in the Barrow then begins.

In the village you can see: the sacred fire and Astra's altar beside it; Pell's chalk drawing by the sacred fire, “HE WAS HERE FIRST”; Nessa's table where she records sunset times; the waypoint statue at the edge of the square.

To the barrow

The barrow is south of the village; on the way you pass through, in order:

  • The Grassland: on your first visit, Lumi notices the cows are all bunched up at the north end, away from the trees. At dusk (17:00–20:00), the whole herd kneels, all facing east. The herder Brin says: “Every dusk, ever since the bridge went down.” After dark the dead come up out of the trees (her quest Night on the Grassland).
  • The Forest: no birds sing. Deep in the forest's southeast is a camp long unused: a rotted tent, a cold fire ring, and a cairn with a pilgrim's staff leaning on it; nailed to a tree is an old Proclamation of the Pilgrimage, worded almost exactly like the one the protagonist later receives, only with a different name. Beside the cairn lies a Pilgrim's Scallop. Come back after dark (20:00–04:00), and a pale shade kneels by the fire ring and, without looking up, says: “Do not kneel to him.”
  • Thornwood Hollow: Lumi senses that “the dead are sleeping badly” down below. On the carved standing stones on either side of the barrow mouth is a knight wearing a crown of thorns with a row of small figures kneeling before him: “THE ORDER OF THE THORN HOLDS”. There is a waypoint statue beside the entrance.

The barrow's three levels

  • The Old Barrow: find Lina in the small cell to the west. She says it was not the dead who shut them in, but a man in grey, who pulled the inner door to from outside and then pushed a loaf of bread through the gap, with a grey thumbprint on the bread. Oskar went down the stairs in the east cell. This “Loaf marked with ash” lies in the cell. On the ground at the barrow's door is Pell's drawing of the door.
  • The Ossuary of the Thorn: a whole order of knights is buried here, every niche carved with a name, and the dead are not resting either. There is an empty niche carved “Fennick, squire to the Commander”, swept clean. The roll on the altar ends with Alberic's name. Find Oskar in the southeast cell; he heard something below walking all night, back and forth, “like a guard on a wall.”
  • The Warden's Sanctum: the barrow warden stands before an altar and does not come for you; Lumi asks: “Is it keeping watch?” When you defeat it, it says: “The night... is coming back. I could not... lie down.” The altar is carved: “I will hold until I am relieved. — Alberic.” Below it, pressed into the soot, is a grey thumbprint.

Return to the village and hand the quest in to Rowan.

The Vigil

Once Lina and Oskar are home, Ilse wants to keep a vigil at the sacred fire for those who came back (the quest The Vigil):

  • Millbrook (west of the village): the waterwheel turns for a while and then stops. The miller, Tam, says something has got into the cellar.
  • The Mill Cellar: slimes and spiders, and two flasks of vigil oil on the shelves.
  • Dawn Hill (north of the village): this is said to be where the sun rose again after the First Long Night, and the flowers all face east. There is the dawnstone carved “HERE THE SUN ROSE AGAIN”, and dawnbloom grows all around. On the hill are wolves and a waypoint statue.
  • The Chapel of the First Dawn (on top of Dawn Hill): roofless and cold. “The first fire altar” is where the First Fire was lit. Step into the bell tower in the northeast corner and ring the old bell, and its sound rolls all the way to Firstlight Village.

The tale at dusk

(After the two are rescued and the vigil is ready, in Firstlight Village between 17:00 and 21:00) Ilse tells the villagers the story of the First Long Night and the Five Sufferings. When she reaches the spear going through Astra's heart, Lumi is shivering; she says she is only cold. Ilse recites: “Suffering is not God's abandonment. It is the proof the debt is being paid.”

The night of thanks

(Same conditions, in Firstlight Village between 21:00 and 24:00) Rowan leads the whole village in giving thanks. The screen goes dark and comes up again: a man in a grey cloak stands before the sacred fire and gathers the flame into a hooded lantern. The sacred fire goes out. Holt shouts “thief”; the grey man says only “...Not tonight.” and walks away east. Holt says he went east over the river: the water froze under his feet, and he walked straight across.

After this night, Ilse goes every morning to sweep the cold altar. If you go to the Ford now, Hesper says she too saw the man who walked across on the ice, and far off to the east a light bobbed along the road and went out.

The letter

(The first morning after the night of thanks, in Firstlight Village) The Church Courier arrives:

By order of the Holy City: the Seal-bearers weaken, and the Long Night returns. Let the pilgrimage be called. One has come down from the mountain with a fairy at their side. The Church names this one pilgrim.

The courier stares at Lumi for a long time, says nothing, and hands the protagonist a Proclamation of the Pilgrimage. Ilse says: “The flame taken, and the Church's letter come, in the same night. ...When you are ready, pilgrim, come to me.”

The Pilgrim's Road (side quest)

Ilse's side quest The Pilgrim's Road sends the protagonist to ask three people:

  • Fennick explains how the White Knight fights: when he drives his sword into the ground, thorns grow in a ring, so stand inside the ring or keep well away, but never on it; when he draws his bow, don't stand straight in front of him. Afterward Fennick remembers: as a young man he carried Alberic's lance for six years, when Alberic had already worn the crown for two hundred and fifty years and would still halt the whole column to kneel on one knee and speak to a child; but the crown kept taking, and one morning he asked Fennick his name.
  • Joss explains the bridge: the gap is three planks wide, “Or find some other way across.”
  • Hesper tells of the rider; come to the Ford again afterward and she talks about the thorns in the stumps: where the planks were, he drove thorns into the wood, “like a man marking what is his.”

Crossing the river

The goal of the objective “Mend the broken bridge” is a state: standing on the far bank. The ways built are:

  • Mend the bridge: fell a tree with an axe, split it into planks, and press G to lay three planks on the water.
  • The ferry: fit the rope from Hesper's chest to the ferry winch, then ask her to take you across.
  • The dead tree: fell the tree at the narrows, dead for ten years, so that it lies across the river.
  • Freeze it: trade food to Tobin for the Frost Wand on his cart, freeze the river and walk across.
  • Any way of getting someone across the river that the world learns counts too.

You can cross before the courier comes, but the main story waits for the pilgrimage to begin before it moves on. Once you are on the far bank, Hesper says the road east comes first to the Church's Rest; the rider went on far past it, beyond the burned farm: “If you find him — mind his bow.”

Part One ends here: standing on the far bank once the pilgrimage has begun, Lumi looks down the road east and says “The road goes on east from here, all the way to the Rest, and the rider, and wherever the grey man took our flame,” and the chapter card “Part One · The Ford” appears. The great road east is overgrown with the rider's thorns and cannot be walked until Part Two.

The Pilgrim's Road

The east exit on the far side of the Ford does not lead straight to the East Road. Heading east, there are four places in turn, all objectives of The First Seal, but none of them blocks the way.

  • The Pilgrims' Rest: the Church's stopping place for pilgrims. On the notice board is the Holy See's notice: “BY THE HOLY SEE OF THE FIVE. The Seal-bearers, saints who bear what Astra bore, can bear it no longer. Pilgrims of the Five: seek out the five Seal-bearers, overcome them, and bring the five instruments home to the Holy City for the Great Rite. Before you go on, take the oath at the five wounds.” (After the letter) Father Caius comes to meet you: “You must be the pilgrim the letter named, the one with the fairy.” He asks the protagonist to take the oath in the chapel first.
  • The oath: before the altar of the five wounds in the Chapel of the Five, the priest leads the protagonist through the Five Sufferings as the Church reads them (the Nails: “upon the holy wood he accepted death”), and finally says: “The Seal-bearer has held that road for three hundred years. The Holy See asks you to overcome him and bring home the Crown, the first of the five instruments. ...He was due to be relieved, once. That is not a pilgrim's concern.” The protagonist receives a Sworn Scallop. The Litany of the Five in the chapel reads: “As long as one bears the Five, the Long Night shall not return.” In the book at the Pilgrims' Hostel, beside many names is written “Went east. Did not come back.” The pilgrim Maud and her daughter Wynn have stopped at the Rest, and Wynn sings the White Knight's nursery rhyme: “he kneels to the small and he stands for the weak—”
  • The Pilgrim's Road: five wayside shrines, one for each Suffering, each of which lights only when what its Suffering asks for is laid on it: something worn on the head, a cord, something to eat, a piece of metal, a light. The signposts are wound with thorns: “EAST: THE KINGDOM. NONE CROSS THAT DO NOT KNEEL.” The white pennants along the road grow more tattered the further east they are, and the last is only rags: “A needle is still stuck through one of them, its thread wound round and round it.” The penitent Brother Ezra whips himself to share the Seal-bearers' pain; he once got as far as the Kneeling Field and saw the knight mending a pennant, whose hands stopped when he saw him: “he stood a long while holding the needle. Then he told me to kneel. I knelt, and I came back.” When all five shrines are lit, the light of the middle one points to the path leading north to the abbey.
  • The Abbey of the Thorn: the order's abandoned abbey, where dead brothers still keep the hours of prayer. The statue in the courtyard shows Alberic on the day he was knighted, kneeling on one knee before a child and holding out a mended wooden horse. In the courtyard stand memorials to the order's dead (who are buried in the Ossuary of the Thorn beneath Thornwood Hollow), one of them to the warden, Brother Tomas: “sworn brother to the Commander, sent down to ward the Order's dead under the hollow.” In the Chapter Hall are the order's roll (forty-one names, the last being “ALBERIC, KNIGHT-COMMANDER — BEARER OF THE CROWN”, and “No knight was made after him”), his journal (mending a kite; “My brother Hugh says it is a sentence”; “Forty years, and then I am to be relieved”; in the forty-first year, “Today I could not remember Hugh's face”; at the end only “Hold.”), and the Writ of Relief that was never sent: “No one could be found who would bear it. Let him go on holding.” In the Abbey Crypt, Prior Wendel, who held back the writ, guards the Order's Seal: “We hold, that others need not.” If you see Father Caius again after finding the writ, he admits: “Yes. I knew.”
  • The Kneeling Field: pilgrims kneel here to pray and then go on their way. Rows of stones carved in kneeling figures bear the names of earlier pilgrims and the year of the Crown-holder, each one carved with their refusal to kneel; the oldest is from the 212th year of the Crown, and the newest has no name carved on it yet. Between the stones, scarecrows in pilgrims' coats are bound kneeling on the ground, all facing east. Lumi jumps: “Oh! That straw one moved — no. It was only the wind.”

The East Road

  • The first time you walk onto the East Road, Lumi says the sun is lower than it was this morning, and that someone has planted the thorns along the road in a line.

  • The burned farmstead: everything has burned except the house. In the ashes are claw marks longer than an arm (a clue for the older storyline Emberfall and the Dragon).

  • The barn: Edda is hiding inside. The days were too short and the wheat died; the things of the night came; a knight in white took everything that shone, including her late husband's ring that she had hung on her doorpost. On the night the barn burned, a man in grey pulled her out of the smoke: “Don't tell them in the village.”

  • The Grey Man's warning (after the courier has come, on the stretch of road before the thorns): the Grey Man stops the protagonist: “Pilgrim, go back. There is nothing at the end of this road that you are looking for.” He says the knight beyond the thorns has held this road for three hundred years, “He was a good man, and he still is”; the Church wants you to take his crown, and he will not stop you: “Only, when the crown comes off, something will come out of it. That, I will take with me.” He looks at Lumi for a long time, says nothing, and walks away. There is no fight.

  • The waypoint statue before the thorns: Lumi says you can rest here, and later, once there is more than one power, change it here too; past the thorns it is even colder than at the shrine stone.

  • The knight's kingdom: a ring of thorns encloses a field, and in the field stand kneeling stones carved as kneeling pilgrims. Also in the kingdom are the Ninth Nail, Edda's Wedding Band, a pilgrim's earring by a kneeling stone, and the gauntlets of one of the White Knight's retainers. When you step into the ring of thorns, the White Knight speaks:

    None cross that do not kneel. Another pilgrim. They always send another. I hold. I... hold. I no longer remember what.

The White Knight

Main article: The White Knight Alberic

The White Knight uses three skills: Arrow of Conquest (a straight line ahead of him), White-Feather Volley (five spots around the target) and Thorns Bend the Knee (a ring around him, with safety both inside and outside the ring; anyone hit kneels for a moment). Before each attack, the cells it will land on are marked first; when his health drops to half, he enters the second phase.

The White Knight in Chapter 1 is weaker than the one in the Boss Challenge: 300 health (1200 in the challenge), 1 armor, and his skills deal 40% of the challenge's damage. When he falls he drops 4 Iron Ore; if the player has a Profession, also one of that profession's Legendary Arms. Only after he falls does the road to Emberfall open.

The Crown of Thorns

(After the White Knight falls) The knight kneels and, for the first time in a very long time, takes the Crown of Thorns off his head with his own hands and holds it out: “Take it. It was never mine to hold.” The protagonist receives the Crown of Thorns.

A cold light comes loose from the crown and runs to Lumi “like water finding an old riverbed.” She receives her first Boon, “Crown's Insight”. Lumi says: “It came to me! ...It's cold. Am I allowed to keep it?”

The Grey Man appears. He opens his lantern, and the remaining light flows into the stolen sacred fire; his hands are shaking.

Every seal you break makes the night longer. Ask your Church why.

He tells the protagonist to keep what Lumi caught, to go home and tell Rowan “the light is safer with me than with any of you,” and walks away east.

From this point the nights grow longer (see below). In the briars where the knight fell there is still one green thorn; the protagonist receives the Oath-Thorn, with which they can enter the Held Ground.

Return

(Back in Firstlight Village with the Crown of Thorns) Rowan says: “The Crown of Thorns, here in Firstlight. ...And that heretic still has our flame.” Ilse says: “He always did carry things that were too heavy for him.” Nessa says: “Four minutes. The sun set four minutes early tonight. I am sure it means nothing.”

The chapter's end card appears on screen:

Chapter One · The First Seal

The White Knight knelt, and gave up the Crown. The grey man took the light east. And the night came a little sooner.

Afterward

  • The main quest The Dragon Road begins (the first stage's log: “I took the road east, toward Emberfall.”), continuing into the older storyline Emberfall and the Dragon. By the setting, Chapter 2 should be the chapter of the second Seal; see Chapter 2.
  • Edda moves to Firstlight Village. Brin's cows no longer kneel; they just stand and look east.
  • Alberic appears as steward of the Held Ground. See Alberic.

Maps

The twenty-four maps on the way to the White Knight: Cloudtop, Mountain Foot, the Ford, Firstlight Village, Firstlight Chapel, the Grassland, the Forest, Thornwood Hollow, the Old Barrow, the Ossuary of the Thorn, the Warden's Sanctum, Millbrook, the Mill Cellar, Dawn Hill, the Chapel of the First Dawn, the Pilgrims' Rest, the Chapel of the Five, the Pilgrims' Hostel, the Pilgrim's Road, the Abbey of the Thorn, the Chapter Hall, the Abbey Crypt, the Kneeling Field, the East Road (not counting small interiors such as houses, shops, farmhouses and tents). After the White Knight falls there is also the Held Ground. On either side of the forest are two more places for side quests: the Charcoal Dell (with Orrin's Hut) and the Reed Bend (with Hilde's Cabin).

The chapter's waypoint statues are at the Mountain Foot, Firstlight Village, the Ford, Thornwood Hollow (beside the barrow mouth), Dawn Hill and the East Road (before the thorn kingdom).

People

  • New in this chapter: Sister Ilse, Joss, Pell, Nessa Quill, Fennick, Edda, Brin, Tam, Orrin, Hilde; along the Pilgrim's Road, Father Caius, Maud, Wynn and Brother Ezra.
  • Appear only in scenes: the Grey Man, the Church Courier. Ordinarily they are on no map the player can reach.
  • Existing characters with new lines: Hesper, Rowan, Holt, Tobin, Lina, Oskar, Marta.
  • After the White Knight falls: Alberic (steward of the Held Ground).

The nights grow longer

After you defeat the White Knight, the nights really do grow longer: from then on night falls at 20:00 and day breaks at 05:30 (previously 21:00 and 05:00). The hours when night creatures appear lengthen with them. In the story, Nessa says the sun set four minutes early. See Day and Night.

Skipping and safety nets

  • Each step's goal is a state, not a path.
  • The player can ignore everything before and go straight to fight the White Knight. Once he falls, the earlier main quests (the barrow, the vigil, The First Seal) and The Pilgrim's Road all count as complete, and Lina and Oskar are home too; the tale at dusk, the night of thanks and the letter no longer play, and the welcome and the oath at the Pilgrims' Rest no longer happen.
  • The oath, the shrines, the abbey and the Kneeling Field along the Pilgrim's Road never block the way; you can walk straight past them. The shrines do not take holy relics or important things, so the Crown of Thorns and the Order's Seal cannot be offered up by mistake.
  • When every way across at the Ford is cut off, Hesper takes the player across the river herself.

Clues

This chapter tells its story with things. The table below lists the traces the Grey Man leaves and how the villagers understand them.

What is seen How the villagers understand it
Pell's drawing of the shrine stone: the first wound blacked out, a grey figure with its hand on it The heretic is defiling the holy stone
Lina: “A man in grey pulled the inner door shut from the outside.” He trapped people in the barrow
Pell's stone that grows warm at night An uncanny thing from a stranger
He takes the First Fire and walks east on the ice Theft of a relic; sorcery
He gathers the knight's scattered power into his lantern The heretic is devouring the Seal
“Every seal you break makes the night longer. Ask your Church why.” Heresy

Other clues: Hesper and Ilse saying “it gets dark earlier every week”; Nessa's sunset table and the Almanac of the First Night; the kneeling herd; the Proclamation of the Pilgrimage in the forest, thirty-one years old; the kneeling stones in the kingdom and on the Kneeling Field; the roll in the ossuary and the oath in the sanctum; the journal in the abbey and the Writ of Relief that was never sent.

Lore

The truth behind each of the Grey Man's acts (click to show)
What is seen The truth
The grey figure by the shrine stone He was checking how far the First Seal had failed.
He shut the door from outside He was shutting the restless warden in, and he left the trapped people bread, pressed with the Ashen Watch's sign.
Pell's warm stone An ember left from a fire taken from the dawn: the things of the night stay away from Pell's window because of it.
He takes the First Fire He needs this dawn flame to hold what each broken seal leaves behind, so he takes it before the pilgrimage begins. How exactly he uses the flame is not yet decided.
He gathers the knight's power He does not want it to reach the Holy City; he believes that sending it there will wake Astra. The flame flares gold, and his hands are shaking.
“The night grows longer” It is true: the Seal-bearers are the Fulcrums of the vessel that is Astra (§12).

The Grey Man's rules: he never harms anyone and never explains a word more than he must; each of his acts also protects someone. The player never fights him.

What the characters leave unsaid (click to show)
  • Sister Ilse raised the Grey Man (Severin). On the night of thanks she recognized his walk but told no one.
  • Fennick left the escort the night Alberic did not know his name. He also knew Severin.
  • Joss: the one who paid for the bridge twenty years ago was a man in grey, a boy at his heel carrying the purse, with the Ashen Watch's old coins, wanting “a road out of here a cart could take in a hurry.” Joss tells this after Chapter 1 ends.
  • Nessa Quill: the last pages of the old almanac were written as the First Long Night fell, in a hand begging someone to “stop him”; she cannot make sense of it. This is the first misreading behind the setting's first reversal. Once you have the Almanac of the First Night, hold it and left-click to use it on yourself to read these pages.
  • Edda owes this heretic her life but dares not say so.
  • The amulet of frost Tobin sold to the Grey Man is how the Grey Man walked across the river on ice.
