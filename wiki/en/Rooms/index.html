Rooms

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay

The game recognizes rooms by itself: a space closed in by walls on every side and covered overhead is a room. You need not place anything special to “designate” it. What is inside decides what kind of room it is: one with a bed is a bedroom, one with a fire a kitchen.

What counts as a room

A set of cells is a room when all of these hold:

  • On the same storey, every cell can be stood on (there is a floor or ground underfoot, and nothing solid such as a wall in its space slot).
  • Every cell is covered overhead: the same cell on the storey above has a floor or a wall (see Storeys). The cell with the stairs counts as covered: that is the way up.
  • Closed in on every side: walking out from any cell inside, you meet a wall or a door; you cannot reach an uncovered place, nor the edge of the map.
  • No more than 256 cells. Anything larger counts as outdoors.

A door, open or shut, is an edge just like a wall, so a house is a room without the door being shut. Nor does it need a door at all: cells walled in on four sides that no one can walk into count as a room too.

On maps that are not outdoors, such as caves and chapels, everywhere counts as covered.

Room uses

The things inside give a room its uses. A room can have several uses at once.

  • Bedroom: the room has a bed. Sleep in a bedroom and you wake with all your missing health restored; on a bed anywhere else, only half (see Sleeping). This is the only use with a real effect so far.
  • Kitchen: the room has a cooking fire, such as a campfire (lit or not), a brazier, a fireplace, a forge or a cauldron.
  • Workshop: the room has a workstation, such as a workbench, an anvil, a forge, a cauldron or a loom.
  • Storeroom: the room has at least 3 containers, such as chest, crate, barrels or clay pots.

Kitchens, workshops and storerooms are only marks on the room for now, with no effect. New rules the world learns (see The World Learns) can read these marks, for example “cooking in a kitchen”.

What a room gives you

  • Shelter from rain: rain does not reach cells covered overhead, so people there do not get wet, and burning things are not put out by rain.
  • A safe night: shut the door, and monsters outside cannot come in; the night creatures that come out at night can only prowl outside.
  • A clear view inside: standing inside, the storeys above are not drawn and the south wall turns translucent (see What you see indoors).

Crops do not grow indoors: crops need open sky to grow (see Farming).

Tutorial

Lesson six of the Tutorial, “Build a house with a bed”: put up walls, fit a door, place a bed, and lay the ceiling last. Once the house becomes a bedroom, the lesson is done. That night, sleep in this bed.

Tips

  • If a single cell is missing its ceiling, the whole house does not count as a room. When you have finished, step inside and see whether the storeys above disappear.
  • A large house cannot exceed 256 cells; if you need more space, divide it into several rooms with walls.
  • For the bedroom's effect, the bed has to be inside the house, not outside the door.

History

  • 2026-10-04: The game began recognizing rooms by itself: closed in on every side and covered overhead; the things inside give a room the use of bedroom, kitchen, workshop or storeroom; sleeping in a bedroom restores full health; covered places keep the rain off (W5).
  • 2026-10-05: A cell with stairs leading up counts as covered too, so a stairwell does not break a room.
  • 2026-10-08: A brazier counts as a cooking fire too; a room with a brazier is a kitchen.
