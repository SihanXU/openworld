Dialogue

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay

Walk up to a person and press E to talk to them. They first say whatever is most fitting at the moment: handing in a quest you have finished for them, telling you what they want to ask of you, or pressing you about their errand; only when there is none of these do you hear an ordinary line (for the order, see what people say first). Then the things you can say to them are listed below the dialogue box. Click one, or press the matching number key 1–9. Esc or “Goodbye.” ends the conversation. Time stands still while a conversation is open.

The small tag next to their name is their opinion of you: hostile, cold, neutral, friendly, close. If they remember something bad you did, it is written in red after their name (for example “You picked Marta's pocket (Bread)”); if you are wanted here, the bounty is shown too.

Things you can say

Option When it appears Result
Accept “quest name” They have a side quest or commission for you You take the quest, and they may say one more line. See taking quests
Decline “quest name” As above You do not take it. A side quest stays open and can be taken later; a commission that arose in the village is withdrawn for good
Quest topics A quest you have under way needs you to say this to them For example, in What Was Taken, saying “Give back what you took.” to the one who took the thing moves the quest on
What news? Always They tell you whatever news they know; if they remember a wrong you did, they say “I know what you did”; if there is nothing, they say “Nothing much has happened”
Let's talk a while. Always They say a random bit of small talk: everyone has at least two lines of their own, plus one where they let slip their favorite gift (said only once; after that the gift panel names it). See gifts
I have something for you. Always Opens the gift panel: at the top, the thing they like best (“?” if you have not heard them say), below, what in your bag you could give, sorted by how much they would like it; click “Give” to give it. See gifts
Let's trade. They are a merchant Opens the Trading panel
Could you do something with my look? The person is the dresser (Odette) Opens the restyle panel, see Looks
About professions… They are a profession trainer (Bram) Opens the Professions panel
Give back what I took. They remember your theft Everything you carry that is not yours goes back to its owners, the matter is dropped, and their opinion of you rises by 8
I'm sorry. They remember a wrong you did A roll (based on their opinion of you and your level, plus 15%): on a success they forgive you; on a failure, opinion −2
I want to settle what I owe the watch They are a guard and you are wanted See Guards and Bounties
Come with me. They are not following you People the story needs cannot leave; at an opinion of “friendly” (25) or above, or if they are a hired hand, they come with you. See Followers and Hiring
Wait here. / Let's go on. / Go home. They are following you They wait where they are, keep following, or go back to their own lives
Bribe (10) You carry 10 Dawnmark You give them 10: opinion +12, and the wrongs they remember are wiped clean
Rob: Your money or your life. They are an adult and you have not robbed them today No roll; it compares strength: you succeed only if your strength is at least twice theirs. Success: they hand over half their money and the two most valuable things they carry, fear you, and their opinion drops by 40. Failure: they beat you down to 1 health, and their opinion drops by 60
Steal: (Slip a hand into their pocket.) They are an adult and you have not been in their pocket today A roll, comparing finesse: on a success they notice nothing, and you take a third of their money and the most valuable thing they carry; on a failure, they knock off half your health, and their opinion drops by 40
Spread atheism: There are no gods watching, you know. They are an adult and you have not said it to them today See Karma and Faith

The options are laid out in a grid of two equal columns, ordered by kind as below, the numbers running on, with “Goodbye.” in the last cell (across the whole last row when it would sit there alone):

  1. Quests: accept, decline, a quest's topics (always first)
  2. Small talk: what news, a chat, a gift
  3. Dealings: trade, professions, the Held Ground, a new look, an invitation to move in
  4. Company: come with me / wait / let's go on / go home
  5. Amends: give back, apologise, settle a bounty
  6. The underhand: bribe, rob, steal, spread unbelief

Stopped by a guard, only the watch's options show (pay the fine, go to jail, talk your way out, bribe, resist).

Robbery and theft

Strength is worked out from level, attack (including the power of the weapon in hand), defense and build (maximum health): level ×2 + attack ×2 + defense + maximum health ÷10. People who can fight (guards, smiths) stand their ground: their strength counts one and a half times. Your strength must reach twice the other person's for a robbery to succeed. It is not a roll but a straight comparison: a level 1 traveler with bare hands cannot rob anyone; after a few levels and a decent weapon you can rob villagers, and guards are harder still.

Finesse is worked out from sneaking, dodge, movement speed and level, and set against the other person's perception: a base of 20%, plus 10% for every 10 points of finesse, minus 10% for every 10 points by which their perception is higher, plus 10% while sneaking (V). The minimum is 5% and the maximum 95%.

The money and things you get from either are yours outright, not stolen goods. The beating you take on a failure is not fatal (you are left with at least 1 health), but a failed robbery leaves you at just 1, so be careful afterwards. You can rob each person once a day and steal from each once a day.

Minors (under 18; a person's page gives their age) cannot be robbed, stolen from, or told there are no gods: those options do not appear. The village's children are Mia, Pell and Wynn.

To steal without talking, there is another way: sneak and press E on a person to go through their pockets directly; see Crime.

Say something the world does not know (for example, something said in your own words in a later version), and the world that learns decides how they answer (see The World Learns).

History

  • 2026-10-09: Added the dialogue panel. Before this, pressing E only brought one line.
  • 2026-10-24: The dialogue options are laid out in a two-column grid of equal cells, ordered by kind (quests, small talk, dealings, company, amends, the underhand), with “Goodbye.” last.
  • 2026-10-09: Quest options were added to the dialogue box: accepting or declining an offer, and the topics a quest under way brings, listed first.
  • 2026-10-18: “Persuade” and “Intimidate” were removed, replaced by “Rob” (comparing strength) and “Steal” (comparing finesse); people now have ages, and minors cannot be robbed, stolen from, or told there are no gods. Persuasion survives only as “Talk your way out” when a guard stops you.
  • 2026-10-18: Added “Let's talk a while.” and “I have something for you.”: everyone has random small talk and one favorite gift they let slip while chatting; gifts got their own panel.
