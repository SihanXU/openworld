World Map

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

There are three things in the game to do with maps:

  • The world map (M): the whole continent with its six regions and its places; switch to “Nearby” for a patchwork of the maps you have visited and how they lie to each other. You can click a waypoint statue to travel.
  • The minimap: in the top-left corner of the screen, showing the map you are on.
  • The overworld: the Lands of the Five Seals, a continent surrounded by the sea. It is itself a map you can walk, and every cell of the continent leads to a map.

The world map (M)

Open it with M or by clicking the minimap in the top-left corner; press it again or press Esc to close it. Time stops while it is open.

  • It shows the whole continent, the Lands of the Five Seals, painted as a terrain chart: the grounds meet along winding edges, the mountains are shaded by their relief with snow on the ridges, forests are clumps of tree crowns, fields are patchwork, the sea deepens away from a foam-lined shore, roads are curving ochre paths, and rivers have bridges. Each region is tinted in its own color with a dashed border of the same color and its name written in the middle; regions that have no story yet are dimmed, with “no tale here yet” written under their names.
  • It opens zoomed in, near where you are; scrolling out stops where the whole continent just fits the view, and the map never leaves the window as you zoom.
  • Places are drawn as small round dots: capitals and towns are larger gold dots and always named, places you have visited show their names at a middling zoom, and the rest only when you zoom right in; places you have been to have a bright ring; sealed places are dark. The marks and names keep their size on the screen as you zoom.
  • A gold badge with a small figure on it, ringed by a pulsing glow, shows where you are now: on the overworld it is the cell you stand on; inside a map it is that map's cell (for an interior or an underground level, the cell of the map it belongs to). Click ◎ You to zoom in and come back to yourself.
  • Click “Nearby” for another kind of drawing: every map you have visited, pieced together by where it lies in the world, drawn in the colors of its terrain and labeled with its name; click “The continent” to switch back.
  • Hold the mouse button and drag to move the view; scroll the wheel to zoom.
  • The map marks every waypoint statue in the maps you have visited as a crystal. Woken statues are a glowing bright cyan; hovering over one says “click to travel,” and a click takes you there, onto the cell just below the statue, facing it. Statues not yet woken are a dim teal and say “not woken yet,” and clicking them does nothing.
  • The top of the panel says “Drag · wheel to zoom · click a glowing statue to travel.”
  • Where a tracked quest wants you to go, a yellow ★ is drawn; hovering over it shows which objective it is. See Tracking and markers.

“Nearby” draws only maps that you have visited and that are joined to each other by exits at their edges. It does not draw: interiors entered through a door, such as houses and chapels; the deeper levels beneath the Old Barrow; the Held Ground; the overworld itself; and the wild on the overworld. While you stand in these places, the world map shows no marker for you.

You cannot travel during a cutscene, while a yes/no question is open, or while the world is learning from your action.

The minimap

A small square in the top-left corner of the screen shows the terrain of the current map, with M marked in its lower left. Click it to open the world map.

Color What it is
White dot (black outline) You
Yellow dot Lumi
Blue dot People
Beige dot Animals
Red dot Monsters
Bright cyan A woken waypoint statue
Dark cyan A waypoint statue not yet woken
Yellow diamond The person or place a tracked quest wants you to go to

Changes to the ground (a felled tree, planks laid down) are drawn on it too. Below the minimap it says whether the current map is a “Peaceful zone” (green) or a “Combat zone · monsters at night” (red). See Peaceful and Combat Zones.

The overworld: the Lands of the Five Seals

Main article: The Lands of the Five Seals

The Lands of the Five Seals are a continent surrounded by the sea, 64×48 cells, divided into six regions. Each cell of the continent is a place that leads to a map (the overworld has its own style, with an icon for each story place): the story maps (The Ford, Firstlight Village, The Forest…) sit on the cells that match the exits between them; the other five regions hold some two hundred places not yet open; and every remaining land cell is wild. A river and a mountain range enclose the west bank of the Ford, which can be crossed only by going through the Ford's own map.

How to get onto the overworld

Walk off the edge of a map where there is no exit to another map, and you come out on the overworld, standing on that map's own cell; from there you choose where to go: step onto a neighbouring cell, or press E right where you stand to go back in. The cells of the Ford and The Reed Bend are river and cannot be stood on: walking out of them puts you on the cell beside them in the direction you walked (out of the Ford, only on the same bank).

  • This works only for maps that have a place on the overworld. Interiors and underground maps do not.
  • Most story maps are ringed with trees and rocks. There are only two places where you can walk out without clearing a way: the one cell on the south side of the Ford's west bank right next to the river, and the one cell beside the stone steps on the south side of Mountain Foot (the far bank of the Ford is woods on its north, east and south sides but for the road east, and in Part One that road's exit is sealed); see the Lands of the Five Seals for details. Elsewhere you must first fell trees (axe) or break rock (pickaxe) to open a way, and then walk off the edge. See Woodcutting, Mining.
  • The four edges of a wilderness map are open: walk out of any side and you are back on the overworld.

Traveling the overworld

The overworld is a peaceful zone with no monsters. Every step on it takes time, and the clock jumps ahead each time:

Terrain Per step
Road 15 game minutes
Plains 30 game minutes
Forest, hills, snowfield 45 game minutes
Sea, mountains, river Impassable

In rain and storms each step takes half again as long. If you are hurt on the way you stop, with the notice “Hurt, you stop on the way.” See In-game Time.

Entering a place

  • Stand on a place and press E; the prompt reads “Enter X,” and in you go, whichever way you face.
  • The cell under your feet is outlined, with the place's name written on it, such as “The wild forest” or “Emberfall.”
  • You enter the map from the side you came onto the cell from: walk in from the east, and you enter from the map's east side.
  • Just out of a map by its edge, press E before taking a step, and you go back in where you came out.
  • The Ford's cell is river and cannot be stood on: stand on the cell beside it, face it and press E, and you enter by the crossing between its two maps.
  • Any other input on a place (left click, right click, G) says “Out of reach — go in first (E).”
  • The place you have just walked out of is drawn with a blinking gold frame and a small marker bobbing above it, so you can find your way back.

Places not yet open

Places the story has not reached yet are drawn on the overworld with their icons, but locked:

  • How they look: the icon is greyed out, with a small padlock in its upper right corner.
  • You cannot step onto it: a locked place's cell is like a wall, neither walked onto nor crossed; step toward it and you bump into it, and Lumi says to come back another time (when she is not with you, the lower left says why; at most once a minute for the same place). Going off a map by a way that leads to a locked place, or off the east edge on the far side of the Ford, is the same. Others travelling the overworld go round it too.
  • You cannot enter: standing on one (in an older save) and pressing E, the prompt reads “Sealed: place name”; pressing it only shows a line saying why, and the place does not open (see Aim and target).
  • Locked in Part One: the road east of the Ford: the Pilgrims' Rest, the Pilgrim's Road, the Abbey of the Thorn, the Kneeling Field, the East Road. Bumping into one says “The road east of the Ford is not open yet: its tale is told in Part Two.” They all open together when Part Two opens (the fact world.part2); so does the exit east from the Ford's far bank, which cannot be passed before then (see Part One).
  • Locked behind the White Knight: what lies east of the East Road, held by the White Knight Alberic: Emberfall, the Old Mine, Cinder Pass, the Dragon's Lair. Bumping into one says “The White Knight still holds the East Road. There is no way past him yet.” They all open when the White Knight falls. The crossing on the east side of the East Road that leads to Emberfall is the same: you cannot pass it before then.

The wild

Main article: The Wild

Every land cell on the overworld without a story map is wild. Each patch of wild is decided by the world's seed and the cell's coordinates; within the same world it is the same every time you go in. A wilderness map is 40×30 cells, a combat zone, with night creatures after dark.

Overworld terrain Cell name Map name inside What is there
Plains The wild plains Open Plains Grass, flowers, scattered trees and rocks; green slimes, chickens; berries, sticks, stones
Forest The wild forest Deep Woods Dense woods; boars, green slimes; mushrooms, sticks
Hills The wild hills Rolling Hills Dry grass, gravel, rocks; wolves, green slimes; stones, herbs
Snowfield The wild snowfield Snowfield Snow, ice, pine trees; frost wolves, frost slimes; stones, sticks
Road The wild road The Road A road running from west to east; green slimes; sticks

Tips

  • Whenever you reach a new place, first find its waypoint statue and wake it; afterwards one click on the world map brings you back.
  • On the overworld, following the roads is fastest.
  • If you want to forage in the wild, first open a gap in the edge of a map near home, and use it to go out to and come back from the overworld.

History

  • 2026-10-26: Bumping into a locked place, or going off a map by a way that leads to one, Lumi says to come back and explore it another time; when she is not with you, the lower left says why.

  • 2026-10-23: The continent on the world map is painted as a terrain chart (winding edges, shaded mountains with snowy ridges, tree crowns, patchwork fields, foam along the coast, curving roads); it opens zoomed in beside you and zooms out no further than the whole continent; you are a figure on a gold badge, waypoint statues are crystals, and the marks keep their size as you zoom.
  • 2026-10-21: The controls were fixed as four inputs: using a held object on something moved to the right click (the left click is the held thing's own use: a tool or weapon is swung, anything else is used on yourself), and picking up loose things moved to E (see Controls).
