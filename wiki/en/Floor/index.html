Floor

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Item
Floor
CategoryStructures
Durability8
MadeBy hand (Crafting)
Made ofPlank×2
Chinese name地板
IDstructure/floor

A floor is a wooden floor piece for building houses, made from 2 Plank on the spot. Laid on the ground, it covers the terrain that was there; laid upstairs, it is a floorboard and needs walls on the storey below to hold it. A storey's ceiling is the floor of the storey above: the same piece of floor is a ceiling seen from below and a floor seen from above. For the detailed rules see Storeys and ceilings; for how to place it see Building.

Source

  • Crafting: Plank×2
  • Crafting: press C and choose “Floor”: 2 planks, no tool needed.
  • Build mode: press B and choose “Floor”. If you have none on you, it is made from planks on the spot. Press R to switch between “Lay on this storey” and “Lay as ceiling”. Drag to lay a row.
  • Holding a floor: right-click a tile to lay it (hold and drag to lay a row). Pressing G only puts it down loose on the ground, not laid, and E picks it up again.
  • The last step of lesson six of the tutorial: choose the floor in build mode, press R to switch to ceiling, and lay the roof on.

Uses

The game data records no set use for it. Right-click something else while holding it, and the world may learn a new use (see The World Learns).

Laid on the ground

  • It covers the terrain that was there and becomes the surface of the tile; laid on flowers or dry grass, it also covers the flowers and tall grass drawn on the ground. Take the floor away and the terrain and plants show again.
  • It can only be laid on ground you can stand on: not on a tile of water, lava, tree or rock (“You can't lay a floor on this ground.”).
  • A tile can have only one floor per storey (“There is a floor here already.”). If a bed, stairs or a chest already stands on the tile, the floor is laid underneath them.

Laid upstairs, laid as a ceiling

  • A floor upstairs needs a wall or door on the storey below, within 2 tiles of the tile, to hold it (“Nothing could hold it up.”).
  • To lay a ceiling over your own storey you have to choose it on purpose: press R in build mode; holding a floor, press R to switch the right click to laying a ceiling, or Alt+right-click and choose “Ceiling”.
  • No ceiling can be laid directly above stairs (“The space above the stairs must stay open.”).
  • A tile with a floor overhead stays out of the rain; a house walled all round and fully ceilinged becomes a room. Crops do not grow under a ceiling.

Taking it up

  • Strike a floor on your storey once with an axe, a pickaxe or a hammer and the whole piece comes loose and lies on the spot; press E to pick it up (close by, it is drawn into your bag by itself).
  • To take a ceiling, hold an axe, a pickaxe or a hammer, Alt+left-click the tile and choose “Ceiling” to knock it loose.
  • A floor upstairs with something on it (a wall, a bed, a person standing there) cannot be knocked loose; first take away what is on it.

Falling in

When the walls holding a floor upstairs are taken away or broken and no other wall nearby holds it, the floor falls in: “The floor gives way.” It breaks into 1 plank, and the people and things on it fall to the first storey below that has a floor. Falling 1 storey does no harm; each storey more costs 6 health (see falling).

Breaking and fire

  • Durability 8, wood. Broken, it leaves 1 plank (half of 2).
  • It can catch fire: while burning it has a half chance every half second to lose 1 durability, and a 10% chance to spread the fire to something burnable within one tile. When the fire goes out the floor remains, with less durability; when its durability burns away, it burns to a portion of Ash, leaving no plank.

History

  • 2026-10-04: The floor became something you can hold in your hand, lay down and take up; a floor upstairs needs walls to hold it, and falls in when the walls are gone (W6).
  • 2026-10-05: A ceiling has to be chosen on purpose with R or a long press; no ceiling can be laid above stairs.
  • 2026-10-07: A ceiling taken down comes back to your hand whole, instead of breaking into a plank.
  • 2026-10-08: A burning floor spreads the fire beside it; burnt away, it turns to ash instead of falling apart into planks.
  • 2026-10-08: A floor laid on the ground covers the plants on that tile; flowers and grass no longer poke up through the floor.
  • 2026-10-21: The controls were set as four inputs: using a held object on something moved to the right click (the left click is the held thing's own use: a tool or weapon swings, anything else is used on yourself), and picking up loose things moved to E (see Controls).
