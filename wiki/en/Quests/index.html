Quests

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

A quest is something the world wants you to do. Main quests begin by themselves as the story goes; side quests and commissions need someone to entrust them to you: a person with a yellow “!” over their head has something to ask of you. Press E on them, they explain what they want, and below the dialogue box “Accept: quest name” and “Not now (quest name)” are listed; only choosing to accept takes it on. Every quest is divided into several stages: when things in the world change, the quest moves forward a step and writes a line in its journal. Press L to open the quest journal; the tracker in the top right of the screen, the minimap, the world map and the markers at the screen's edge point to where the quest you are tracking wants you to go.

Four kinds of quest

Kind Where it comes from How it begins Examples
Main The story itself By itself, without talking to anyone: when one main quest ends, the next takes over Down from the Mountain, The First Seal
Side Someone's own business Talk to them and accept in the dialogue box Mia's Ring, The Old Templar
Commission Something happening in the village right now, or a job there is every day Talk to the person asking and accept, or take it at the commission board A Bite to Eat, Wood for the Fire
Guide Lumi teaching you a way to play She speaks up herself: it begins when the moment comes A Road of Your Own, The First Cast

In the quest journal, every quest name has a small tag in front saying which kind it is.

Taking on quests

Main quests: they begin by themselves

Main quests don't need to be taken. As soon as their condition is met (the previous main quest ends, you reach a certain place, the story reaches a certain step), they begin, and the lower left says “Quest taken: X.” If you haven't chosen another quest to track in the journal, the newly begun main quest is the one tracked. Main quests follow one another, and there is always one under way. See Main Quest Walkthrough.

Guides: Lumi speaks up herself

Guides are Lumi's teaching quests. She waits for the right moment and says a line herself, and the quest begins, with the notice “New guide: X” in the lower left: when you have unspent skill points it is The First Skill Point; when you reach level 5 without a profession, A Road of Your Own; the first time you reach the pond in Firstlight Village, The First Cast; the first time you walk into the vegetable garden, A Lesson in the Garden. They don't take the main quest's place: the main quest is still tracked by default; press E on Lumi, and her “The Road” page lists the guides under way beside the main quest, each with her hint. Every objective is a state (a fishing rod in your bag, having talked to Bram, a sprout standing in the clearing), and however you reach it counts. There are no guides in the tutorial: its lessons are in the stages of Waking.

Side quests and commissions: accepted in conversation

  1. A person with a yellow “!” over their head has something to entrust to you. Walk up to them and press E, and they first explain what they want.
  2. At the top of the list below the dialogue box is each thing they are asking of you, with two choices each: Accept: quest name and Not now (quest name). Click one or press its number key.
  3. Choose accept: the quest begins, the lower left says “Quest taken: X,” and they may say another line.
  4. Choose not now:
    • A side quest: they don't take offense, and the matter stays open. The “!” over their head doesn't go away; come back and take it whenever you have made up your mind.
    • A commission the village gave rise to (A Bite to Eat, What Was Taken, A Letter to Carry, The Night-born): declining withdraws it, and this commission won't come to you again.

Some side quests have prerequisites: a certain level, the story reaching a certain step, finishing another quest. Until the conditions are met there is no “!” over their head, and they won't bring it up. Side quests the story has already passed won't be brought up again either (for example, once the White Knight has fallen, no one will entrust The Pilgrim's Road (quest) to you any more).

What people say first when you talk to them

Press E on someone, and they pick one thing to say in this order:

  1. Words that move a quest forward: a quest you are to hand in to them (with everything done), or a quest that sends you to talk to them. They say that stage's line, and the quest moves on. This comes before everything else.
  2. Entrusting: if they have something to ask of you, they explain it, and the dialogue box lists accept and not now.
  3. Words while they wait: if you have taken their quest and not finished it, they give you a nudge, such as Marta's “Three cuts of beef and two eggs. The pot is on already.”
  4. Only if there is none of these do they say their everyday line.

After that line, the things you can say to them are listed below the dialogue box. Choices to do with quests come first: commissions to accept or decline, and the topics a quest under way adds for this person (for example, in What Was Taken, saying “Give back what you took.” to the one who took the thing). For the other choices see Dialogue.

Markers over heads

Marker Meaning
Yellow “?” Talking to them now will move one of your quests forward: handing in, or a quest that sends you to them
Yellow “!” They have something to entrust to you (a side quest or commission)
“?” (quest under way) One of your quests' objectives is to talk to them, even if it isn't time yet (for example, you have everything but left it in a chest)

When someone has several of these at once, only one is shown, taken from the top of the table down. Markers are drawn above everything in the world, so you can see them even when the person stands behind a house. See What appears over heads.

Objectives

Every quest has several objectives, listed in the journal and the tracker:

Objective How it counts as done
Bring You carry a number of a certain kind of thing. The count looks at how many you carry now: drop them or use them, and the count goes back down
Defeat After taking on the quest, defeat a number of a certain kind of creature (some only on a certain map). Those defeated before you took the quest don't count
Talk Go to someone and press E, and they say a particular line
Give Hold something and press G on someone, and confirm, to put it into their hands
Reach, achieve Something holds true in the world: you reach a place, ring a bell, read a journal, wake a number of waypoint statues, the story reaches a certain step
  • Objectives look at states, not routes: however you do it counts. Some objectives already hold before you take the quest (for example, you woke three statues long ago), and are done the moment you take it.
  • Some objectives are released stage by stage: the later ones only appear in the journal after the earlier ones are done.
  • Objectives marked “optional” don't affect whether the quest is completed.
  • An objective that can no longer be done (for example, because the story has moved past it) is struck out in the journal.

Stages and the journal

Every quest is made up of several stages. A stage waits on a state of the world: a thing coming into your hands, someone falling, you entering a place, a step of the story happening. As soon as the state holds, however it came about, the quest enters that stage:

  • a line is written in the journal, such as “All of it is done. Back to Marta.”;
  • new objectives are released, or objectives are ticked or struck out;
  • sometimes someone says a line, takes things from you, or hands out a reward;
  • sometimes the quest is completed or failed then and there.

Quests only move forward, never back. Each quest page's “Stages and journal” section lists all its stages, what each writes in the journal, and when it is entered.

Handing in

Side quests and most commissions must be handed back to the person who asked, once done:

  1. When all the objectives are done, the journal writes “All of it is done. Back to X.”, an objective “Back to X” appears, and a yellow “?” appears over their head.
  2. Press E on them: they say their hand-in line, take what they asked for from you (a few things they let you keep, such as the Pilgrim's Scallop), the reward is yours, and the lower left says “Quest complete: X.”
  3. At hand-in the objectives are checked once more: things for a “Bring” objective must be on you. If you put them down after gathering them all, you have to bring them along again to hand in.

Some commissions don't need you to go back: in A Bite to Eat, What Was Taken and A Letter to Carry you put the thing into the other person's hands with G, and the quest is completed on the spot. Most main quests complete by themselves when the last objective is achieved; Lost in the Barrow has you go back to Rowan, and The Vigil has you go back to Sister Ilse.

Rewards

Rewards can be:

  • Experience, multiplied by your experience bonus. See Levels and Experience.
  • Items, put straight into your backpack. Some are named gear of a given quality, such as Rowan's old mail. See Equipment, Quality.
  • Money: What Was Taken gives 10 Dawnmarks, A Letter to Carry 6, The Night-born 12. Other quests give no money. (The money for expanding the Held Ground is not a quest reward; it is paid when a project of expanding the Held Ground is finished.) See Money.
  • Regard: the person who asked (and sometimes others) thinks better of you. See Friendship.
  • The story moving forward.

Each quest page's “Rewards” section lists all its rewards.

Quests the story has passed

The main story never breaks off and never leaves you stuck. If you jump ahead some other way, the quests passed over come to an end instead of hanging in the journal forever. At present only one thing passes over other quests: the White Knight Alberic falling.

Quest If the White Knight falls first
Lost in the Barrow It ends, and Lina and Oskar make their own way back to the village. If by then both have been found and the warden has fallen, it is completed and rewarded as usual; otherwise there is no reward
The Vigil It ends. If by then you carry the lamp oil and the dawn flowers and have rung the bell, Ilse accepts them all the same and the reward is given; otherwise there is no reward
The First Seal Completed, rewarded as usual
The Pilgrim's Road (quest) It ends. If by then all three people have been asked, the reward is given as usual; otherwise there is no reward. If you never took it, no one will entrust it to you any more

Quests that end this way all go on the journal's “Done” page, with the last journal line reading “I went on east before the vigil.”, or, when everything was done, “I did all of it on my way east.”

Commissions

Commissions arise from what is happening in the village right now: someone is hungry, someone has watched their things being taken, there is a letter to carry, the things of the night are growing in number again. They come and go, and the people and things in each are the ones really there at the time.

Commission When it appears Who asks What to do Deadline
A Bite to Eat Someone is hungry, has no money and can't find food The hungry person Give them something to eat (G) 8 hours
What Was Taken Someone has seen a person they know take something of theirs (not food) The one who lost it Get it back and return it to them (G) 24 hours
A Letter to Carry Some mornings (6:00) someone in Firstlight Village has a letter to send The one who wrote the letter Give the letter to its recipient (G) 12 hours
The Night-born Some evenings (20:00) a guard thinks there are too many things about in the night A guard Defeat 3 night creatures and go back to them 10 hours
Wood for the Fire Every day Gus Bring back 4 logs and hand them to him None
  • Someone with a commission has a “!” over their head, and it is also listed on the commission board in Firstlight Village.
  • Deadlines run in game time: once a commission is offered, if no one takes it within the deadline it is withdrawn; once taken, if it isn't done within the deadline it fails. Commissions without a deadline never fail.
  • There are at most two of the same kind of commission at once (one each of A Letter to Carry and The Night-born); the same person or the same thing never gives rise to two of the same commission at once.
  • Wood for the Fire can be taken once a day; after handing it in you have to wait until the next day. Gus asks it of you in person, so it isn't on the commission board.

The commission board

Main article: commission board

On the square of Firstlight Village, beside the notice board, stands a commission board. Stand within 2 cells and press E (“Look at the commission board”) to open the commission board panel: every commission in the village that no one has taken yet, each with its title and description and a “Take it on” button beside it. Click “Take it on” and it is yours, with no need to go and find the person asking; handing in and the outcome are the same as taking it on face to face. When there are no commissions it says “Nobody wants anything done just now. Come back later.”

Failure

A quest fails when:

  • a commission passes its deadline without being done;
  • a person the quest can't do without dies, or the thing it needs is gone: the hungry person in A Bite to Eat dies, the recipient in A Letter to Carry dies or the letter is gone, the thing in What Was Taken is destroyed.

On failure the lower left says “Quest failed: X” (in red), and the quest moves to the journal's “Failed” page, with its last line giving the reason for the failure, or “It came to nothing.”

Protected people and quest items

  • People the story cannot do without (people who speak in cutscenes, people who give quests, profession trainers) don't die when their health reaches zero; they only fall down. Besides, while a quest is under way, the people it names have the same protection, such as the guard who asks in The Night-born. See Death of Characters.
  • Quest items: while a quest is under way, the thing it needs you to bring back or deliver (the thing in What Was Taken, the letter in A Letter to Carry) is a quest item, and merchants won't take it. See Trading. Once the quest is over it is an ordinary thing.

The quest journal (L)

Open it with L or by clicking the quest button in the lower right; time stops while it is open.

  • At the top are three tabs: Under way, Done and Failed, each showing how many quests it holds.
  • On the left are this page's quests, newest first; each has its kind's tag in front (Main, Side, Favour), and the tracked one has a ★ in front.
  • On the right are the details of the selected quest:
    • the title, with “✓ Done” for completed quests and “✗ Failed” for failed ones;
    • From: who asked (left out for main quests with no one asking);
    • the description;
    • the objectives: unfinished ones first, with their progress (such as “Log 2/4”), “optional,” and “→ map name” when the objective is on another map; finished ones ticked ✓, struck-out ones marked ✗;
    • the Journal: one line for each stage reached, in the order they happened.
  • A quest under way has a “☆ Track” button in its upper right; click it and it becomes “★ Tracked,” and this quest is the one tracked. Click again to cancel and return to the default.
  • When you haven't taken any quest, “Under way” says: “No quests taken on. Look for a yellow "!" over someone's head, and press E.” When you have taken quests but they are all done for now, it says: “Nothing under way. Look for a yellow "!" over someone's head for more.” Other empty pages say “None.”
  • The number on the quest button in the lower right is the number of quests under way. It has a red background when someone in sight has a “?” over their head, and a brown one otherwise. The button flashes when a quest enters a new stage, and stops when you open the journal.

Tracking and markers

Only one quest is tracked at a time: the one you chose in the journal; if you haven't chosen one, the most recently begun main quest still under way (or, with no main quest, the most recently begun quest).

  • The tracker: in the top right of the screen, below the clock, it shows the tracked quest's name and its unfinished objectives (up to four), with their progress; when an objective is on another map it says “→ map name.”
  • Quest markers: the people or places that the tracked quest's unfinished objectives point to:
    • a yellow diamond on the minimap;
    • a yellow ★ on the world map (M); hovering over it shows the objective;
    • for one on the current map but off the screen, an arrow at the screen's edge pointing its way; once it is on the screen, the arrow turns into a diamond floating above it;
    • when the objective is a person, the marker follows them; when it is a place, the marker sits in the middle of that place; when it is something to fetch from a place (the dawnbloom on Dawn Hill), the marker sits on the nearest one lying there.
    • when every objective is on another map, one more marker sits on the way off this map toward it (a door or the map's edge): the arrow at the screen's edge and the minimap's diamond point there.
  • Both can be turned off in Options: “Quest tracker” and “Quest markers (map and screen edge).”

Lumi

Press E on Lumi, and she tells you the tracked quest: “Now: quest name.” Then come the unfinished objectives and their progress, plus this stage's hint, such as how to do it and where to look. When no quest is under way, she says “Nothing presses now. Go where you like!” In the tutorial, what she says is the hint for each stage of the main quest Waking.

Stand five seconds doing nothing and she flies to where the tracked quest goes next herself, calls out over it, and a ring of light shows the spot; farther off she leads the way, and on another map she shows the way off it. See Showing the way.

Tips

  • When you reach a village, first look for people with a “!” over their heads. Firstlight Village has several side quests, and there are often commissions on the commission board.
  • Hand-ins usually take away the things for “Bring” objectives. Think it over before handing in something important you have only one of.
  • Commissions have deadlines. After taking one, check its description in the journal, and don't set off only after the deadline has passed.
  • If you want to know where to find an objective, track that quest: the tracker says which map to go to, and the maps carry markers.
  • Wood for the Fire can be done every day, a steady source of experience and bread.
  • For each quest's detailed walkthrough see its quest page and Main Quest Walkthrough.

History

  • 2026-10-03: The quest system was added: Firstlight Village's commissions, the quest journal (L), the “!” and “?” over heads, daily repeating quests.
  • 2026-10-06: Chapter 1's main and side quests were added; main quests are taken up automatically and complete by themselves, and quests the story passes over close automatically.
  • 2026-10-07: Chapter 1's main quest runs through to the White Knight, and quest rewards include named gear.
  • 2026-10-08: The quests for expanding the Held Ground reward Dawnmarks. Quests passed over by the story whose objectives were all achieved (such as Down from the Mountain and The First Seal) give their rewards as usual.
  • 2026-10-28: An objective to fetch something puts its marker on the nearest one lying on the ground, not the middle of the place; when every objective is on another map, the way off this map gets a marker too. Lumi shows the way when you stand five seconds doing nothing.
  • 2026-10-09: The quest system was rebuilt to be driven by events: side quests and commissions are accepted or declined in the dialogue box; quests are divided into stages, with the journal recording each stage; the quest journal has three pages, Under way, Done and Failed, and one quest can be tracked; the tracker in the top right and quest markers on the minimap, the world map and the screen's edge were added; what happens in the village gives rise to commissions (A Bite to Eat, What Was Taken, A Letter to Carry, The Night-born), and Firstlight Village put up a commission board; commissions have deadlines and fail when they expire; people a quest names are protected, and merchants don't take quest items; the “repeatable” kind became “commission,” and Wood for the Fire was filed under commissions; the stages of the tutorial became the main quest Waking; the old main line from Emberfall to the dragon became the main quest The Dragon Road.
