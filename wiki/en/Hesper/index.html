“Thirty-one years I've kept this crossing. I know every snag between here and the narrows.”

— Hesper

Hesper is the ferrywoman of The Ford, 52 years old. She lives by the tent in the Ford's camp; by day she minds the ferry winch on the jetty and mends nets, and morning, noon and evening she goes back to tend the camp's campfire. When the player comes down the cliff steps from Mountain Foot to the Ford, she is the first person they meet: she tells of the storm that snapped the ferry rope and the rider in white who tore down the little bridge, gives the player a Bread, and points them west to Firstlight Village.

Once the ferry rope is mended, press E on her (“Ask Hesper to take you across”) and she takes you over the river on her raft.

Appearance

Long, wavy ash-blonde hair falling past her waist, the upper part tied back with a black ribbon; grey-blue eyes, fine lines at their corners and about her mouth. A large cream wool shawl embroidered with grey-blue flowers and hung with long fringes, over a dark slate dress with a wide sash; a cameo brooch on her high white lace collar, a gold pocket watch and a tassel on chains at her waist, a wedding ring on her hand.

She has a portrait of her own: when you talk to her, the dialogue box shows it. It has six expressions: neutral, happy (a faint smile, the eyes closed), sad, angry (stern), surprised and shy (a small smile, the eyes lowered). She blinks soon after each line begins, and her mouth opens and closes as she speaks (happy, her eyes are already closed, so she does not blink). Her sprite on the map follows: ash-blonde waves, a dark dress and a cream shawl. Her design book: Art.

On ordinary days she sleeps at the camp from 21:00 to 05:00; from 05:00 to 07:00, 12:00 to 13:00 and 18:00 to 21:00 she tends the campfire; from 07:00 to 12:00 she minds the winch on the jetty and watches the water; from 13:00 to 18:00 she mends nets on the jetty. Tending the fire needs the camp's campfire; if it has gone out, she cannot do it.

On stormy days she shelters in the mouth of her tent from 07:00 to 21:00. After the player has crossed the river, she keeps to the winch all day.

Relationships

Her husband Aldo built the Ford's winch and raft; the next spring the river took him, and she stayed. Twenty years ago, Aldo and Joss built the Ford's little bridge together; Joss was Aldo's friend.

The peddler Tobin still owes her the fare from last autumn. She never brings it up herself; Tobin is forever mentioning it.

Quests

  • The Pilgrim's Road (Side)

Hesper gives no quests herself. Sister Ilse's side quest The Pilgrim's Road (quest) sends the player to ask her what she saw; once you have asked, a scene plays when you return to the Ford, of her looking at the bridge piles: the riders drove thorns in where the planks used to lie. “Mend it. Then go and tell that knight whose bridge it is.”

Fallbacks for the crossing

There must always be a way across the river at the Ford.

  • If the Rope needed to mend the ferry is burned, she takes an old rope off the raft.
  • When the rope, the dead tree and any cold that could freeze the river are all gone, she says “The water's going down”, and poles the player across on the raft herself.

Lore

Hesper is the first in Chapter 1 to speak of the White Knight. She saw with her own eyes a rider white from head to foot, a crown of thorns grown into his helm, pull the little bridge's planks out one by one with his hands and lay the nails on her jetty, “like he was paying the toll”. See Chapter 1 and The White Knight Alberic.

On the night the holy flame was stolen, she saw a man in grey walk across the river, the water freezing under his feet as he went. See The Grey Man.

What she does not say (click to show)

Her hands have grown too stiff to splice the rope, but she will not say so outright. Nor does she ever press a claim, which is why Tobin still owes her that fare.

What she wears each has its story (the design): she tied the black ribbon the spring the river took Aldo, and has not taken it off in nineteen springs; Aldo brought the shawl over the river their first winter; the lace collar and the cameo she brought with her when she crossed the river to marry him, thirty-one years ago; the pocket watch at her waist is Aldo's, the ferry has always run by it, she winds it every evening, and it was by it she saw the evenings coming sooner every week.

Trivia

  • In the chest at her camp are a spare Rope and a loaf of bread. The rope belongs to no one; the bread is hers: taking the bread in front of her counts as theft.
  • She says Aldo always claimed the dead tree at the narrows would “fall the wrong way”. Once the player fells the dead tree and lays it across the river, she admits Aldo was wrong.
  • In thunder she warns the player: “Lightning goes for that dead tree.”
