Money

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

OpenWorld's money is the Dawnmark (Dawnmark), the Held Ground's own coin. It is an ordinary item in the inventory: many stack in one slot, it can be put in a chest, and it falls to the ground along with other things. The game keeps no separate “money count”: however many Dawnmarks you carry is how much money you have. Open the inventory (I); the title line of the pack on the right shows the money you carry: a Dawnmark icon, the total and the word “Dawnmarks” (see Pack). When you collapse on Normal difficulty, your money does not go into the Lost Belongings but scatters where you fell; walk back and pick it up.

Places to spend and earn money: the General Store in Firstlight Village (Greta's counter) and the skill-book shelf in Firstlight Chapel (Brother Anselm), the trading stall Alberic keeps in the Held Ground, and everyone: now every person is a merchant, selling things that suit who they are (see Trading). Villagers also carry a few Dawnmarks, topped up every morning: they use them to buy food, and you can take them from their pockets (see Sneaking and Pickpocketing). Fines from the watch and bribes in dialogue are paid in Dawnmarks too.

As he hands it over, Alberic says: “Take this, the coin of this ground. My stall keeps seeds, feed and bait, and buys back what you grow, raise and catch. There are fish in the pond.”

Other quests reward experience and items, not money (see Quests). The rewards for expanding the Held Ground are written in the lower left corner of each card in the Expanding the Held Ground panel (“Reward: N”).

The stall

Main article: trading stall

The stall stands in the middle of the Held Ground, southeast of the planted sword, next to where Alberic keeps watch by day; it is named “Alberic's stall”.

  1. Stand beside the stall and press E on it (“Trade at Alberic's stall”) to open the stall panel.
  2. Along the top of the panel are four tabs: Farming, Ranching, Fishing and Sell, with the number of Dawnmarks you carry shown in the top right.
  3. Each row says what you give and what you get. On the first three tabs the button is “Buy”, which buys one lot at a time (a lot of seeds is 3 or 5); on the “Sell” tab the button is “Sell → N”, which sells one at a time for N Dawnmarks.
  4. When something can be done several times over, an extra “All ×times” button appears beside it, which buys until your money runs out or sells until the things are gone. Holding Shift while clicking the button does the same.
  5. What you buy goes straight into your inventory; what you sell is taken from your inventory, and the Dawnmarks you get for it go into your inventory too.
  • You must stand within one tile of the stall (diagonals count) to trade. From farther away, the panel reads “Stand beside the trading stall to trade.” and the buttons are gray.
  • Prices are fixed: they do not change with buying and selling, there is no stock, and there is no haggling.
  • When you do not have enough money or things, the row turns gray and its button cannot be pressed.

How trading works

Buying and selling at the stall works the same way as crafting: the stall is a “station”, and every trade is like a recipe: what you give (Dawnmarks, or the thing you sell) is used up, and what you get goes into your inventory. That is why trading can only be done beside the stall, just as some things can only be made beside a workbench.

There is no shipping bin in the game: you carry things to the stall yourself to sell them. The team decided not to copy any one game's own gameplay loops (such as Stardew Valley's shipping bin); see Design Pillars.

What money is for

  • Farming: buying seeds, Fertilizer and a Shovel. The Held Ground's stall sells the seeds of 14 vegetables, plus wheat seeds, herb seeds, berry seeds and acorns, many of which cannot be had elsewhere. See Farming, Crops.
  • Raising animals: buying Animal Feed, a Bucket for milking, Crated Hen and Crated Calf. See Animals.
  • Fishing: buying a Fishing Rod and Bait. You can also make a fishing rod yourself. See Fishing.
  • Things in the village: in Firstlight Village, buy Bread from Marta, and a Flask of Oil, a Hood, a Lockpick and Firecracker from Tobin; in Emberfall, buy tools and weapons from Garrick and a Healing Potion from Wren. For what each person sells, see Trading.
  • The General Store: the General Store by the East Road in Firstlight Village; press E on Greta's counter to buy lamps, rope, a fire steel, a knife, fishing rods, bait, food and potions, and to sell it herbs, berries, mushrooms, feathers, leather and eggs (at half the thing's value).
  • Skill books: Brother Anselm's skill-book shelf, 60 for each ordinary skill book and 120 for a profession skill book. See Skills.
  • Smithing materials: buy smith hammers, coal, ore and ingots from Haldor and Alberic. See Smithing.
  • Settling trouble: paying a fine, bribing a guard (one and a half times the bounty), or giving someone 10 in dialogue to make them forget what you did.

Not yet

  • Coin pouches: the Coin Pouch item is marked as worth 20, but it is not Dawnmarks, and it can neither be opened nor spent.
  • Item value: every item carries a value, and the higher its quality, the more it is worth (a Crude one is worth half a Common one, a Masterwork one three and a half times). The village merchants set their prices by this number (see Trading); the Held Ground's stall ignores it, its prices set one by one.
  • Exchanges in the story: the peddler Tobin at the Ford is hungry; give him some food and he gives you the frost Wand from his cart. This is a story rule: it costs no money and is not a trade.

Tips

  • The 30 you get on first entering the Held Ground are enough for a fishing rod, or a few lots of seeds and some bait.
  • To save up quickly: grow vegetables worth a lot per plant, such as Cauliflower and Melon, or go fishing in the early morning for Dawnscale.
  • You can make a fishing rod yourself from two Sticks and two bunches of Cut Grass, rather than spending 25 on one.
  • Before selling a harvest, think about keeping seed: harvesting with a sickle brings back seeds, and as they multiply you no longer need to buy them.

History

  • 2026-10-11: The inventory shows the money you carry; when you collapse on Normal difficulty, your money scatters where you fell; money can be spent at Firstlight Village's General Store and church.
  • 2026-10-11: The three quests for expanding the Held Ground were dropped; the 20, 40 and 60 for lands I–III are now given when each is done in the Expanding the Held Ground panel.
  • 2026-10-12: The coin was renamed the Dawnmark, its design a gold coin stamped with the first light of dawn; the coin's name is written beside the money in the pack's title line. Building a residents' tent also gives a reward.
  • 2026-10-01: An early version modeled on Stardew Valley had shops and a shipping bin, but no gold.
  • 2026-10-02: The team decided not to copy Stardew Valley's gameplay systems, and the shipping bin and shops were removed with that version.
  • 2026-10-08: Dawnmarks and Alberic's stall were added to the Held Ground (three shelves, Farming, Ranching and Fishing, plus buying back); the quests for expanding the Held Ground were changed to reward Dawnmarks.
  • 2026-10-09: The village merchants began buying and selling (prices set by item value and regard, with Tobin taking stolen goods); villagers carry money; fines and bribes were added.
  • 2026-10-13: Firstlight Village's church was merged into Sister Ilse's chapel; the skill-book shelf moved into Firstlight Chapel on the square and was renamed the chapel bookshelf.
