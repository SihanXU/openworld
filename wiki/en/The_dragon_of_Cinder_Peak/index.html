The dragon of Cinder Peak is the great dragon lairing in the Dragon's Lair atop Cinder Peak, the final foe of the “Emberfall and the dragon” line. It comes down from the mountain once every spring, and this year it came down twice: the claw marks in the ashes of the burned farmstead on The East Road and the half of Emberfall's roofs that are newly laid are both its doing. Fighting it is a boss fight: it breathes fire, hurls fireballs, sweeps its tail and dives, leaves embers on the ground, and calls up dragon whelps at half health. Defeat it, and the line comes to an end.

The Emberfall and dragon line was written before the Five Sufferings setting and is placeholder content; it will later be replaced by the chapter of the Second Seal (to be designed). See Emberfall and the Dragon, Chapter 2.

Spawning

  • The Cinderpeak Lair: 1 at the start
  • The Dragon's Lair: 1, on the scorched rock deep in the lair. When you walk into the mouth of the lair there is a stretch of narration, and the main quest The Dragon Road shows its last objective, “Defeat the dragon of Cinder Peak”.

Behavior

  • Boss fight. Once you enter the lair and come near it, its large health bar appears at the top of the screen, the camera pulls back, and the boss music starts. See Bosses.
  • Its body takes up 2×2 tiles. Collisions and hits count for the whole body; the wings, body and tail trailing behind it are only drawn.
  • Sight 10 tiles, at most 12 tiles from its nest. If its target runs too far, it goes back.
  • Skills only. It has no ordinary attack; all its damage comes from its skills, each a multiple of its attack (7 in the campaign, 22 in the boss challenge).
  • Does not fear fire. It carries fire itself; fire-element hits instead restore 2 health to it, and water does it more damage.

Embers are fire on the ground: step in and you catch fire (burning); they go out after 4.5 seconds. The Fireproof Draught still helps.

The three phases:

  • Phase 1 (100%–50%): Cinder Breath → Threefold Fire → Cinder Breath → Threefold Fire, on a loop.
  • Health falls to half: a shriek, and Call the Brood. Then phase 2: Searing Dive → Cinder Breath → Threefold Fire → Searing Dive, wind-ups 10% shorter.
  • Health falls to a quarter: Ashfall. Then phase 3: Searing Dive → Cinder Breath (twice in a row, the second aimed afresh) → Threefold Fire, wind-ups 20% shorter, damage 20% higher.

Strategy

For the full walkthrough see Main Quest Walkthrough.

  • Get two things ready first. Garrick forges Dragonsbane from 5 Iron Ore and 3 Coal, dealing double damage to the dragon and its whelps; Wren brews two Fireproof Draughts from 3 Fireweed and 3 Frostcap.
  • Drink at the mouth of the lair. A Fireproof Draught leaves you fireproof for a while: fire damage drops to a quarter, and you cannot catch fire. Drink the second when the first is about to wear off.
  • Make good use of the four rock pillars. Cinder Breath cannot pass a pillar; a dive into a pillar stuns the dragon, and that is your best chance to strike.
  • Do not linger on embers. After Cinder Breath and the dive the ground is left burning; walk round it.
  • Once the whelps are out, you can clear them first, or focus on the dragon: once the dragon dies, the whelps scatter.
  • Halfway up the pass there is a lit campfire where you can rest.

Drops

  • Cindertear Earring (trophy, straight into the slayer's bag: always drops)
  • Experience 700 (its maximum health; see Levels and Experience)

It leaves nothing (it carries nothing on its body). Defeating it gives experience equal to its maximum health. When it falls, the ending scene plays with the closing card “The dragon of Cinder Peak”: “Everything the world learned from you is kept in the Threadbook (J).” See Threadbook.

Once the dragon is dead, Mara wants to carve the player's name on the well.

Lore

The dragon line is the old main plot from before the Five Sufferings setting was settled (“from the broken bridge to the dragon”), and for now it follows Chapter 1. According to the lore, the chapter of the Second Seal will replace it; its content is still to be designed. See Emberfall and the Dragon.
