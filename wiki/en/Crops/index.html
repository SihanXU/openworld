Crops

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay

Crops are planted in farmland, watered once per stage, and grow stage by stage until they are ripe for harvest. How to plant them is in Farming. The table below lists every crop in the game; seed prices and produce prices are those of the trading stall on The Held Ground (seed prices are written as “coins / seeds per packet”).

Every crop has three stages: sprout → young plant → ripe. Turnips, parsnips and radishes take 1 game hour per stage; pumpkins, cauliflowers, melons, corn and blueberries 4 hours per stage; the rest 2 hours per stage. Normally a crop is watered once per stage; irrigation makes one watering serve two stages, at the same speed. A ripe crop cut with a sickle gives 2 produce and 2 seeds; picked by hand with E, it gives 1 produce and 1 seed.

Vegetables

There are fourteen vegetables. All can be eaten as they are; they restore only Satiety and cannot be roasted.

  • Carrots, turnips, tomatoes, pumpkins: the vegetable garden in Firstlight Village grows these four at every stage, and the “garden toolbox” beside it holds 2 seeds of each (see Firstlight's vegetable garden). The stall sells them too.
  • Parsnips, potatoes, radishes, cauliflowers, melons: seeds are sold only at the stall. Once harvested they are gone.
  • Corn, blueberries, strawberries, hot peppers, eggplants: seeds are sold only at the stall. They fruit again after harvest (see below).

The last ten were added after the fields of Stardew Valley: root vegetables, cauliflower, melon, and five that fruit again after harvest.

Crops that fruit again

Tomatoes, corn, blueberries, strawberries, hot peppers, eggplants and berry bushes are perennial: after you cut or pick them, the plant does not disappear but goes back to the young-plant stage; water it once more and it ripens again after one stage of its kind's time. Corn and blueberries take 4 hours, the rest 2 hours. Fertilizer is used up by the harvest and must be applied again; water held over by irrigation is not kept across a harvest either.

A crop that fruits again needs its seeds bought only once; after that, every watering brings another harvest. A packet of seeds for the last five holds only 3, and each seed costs more than most annuals (see the table above).

Wheat, herbs and berry bushes

  • Wheat (Wheat Seeds): the produce, Wheat, is a material and also animal feed (see Feeding). Ripe wheat is dry and catches fire easily.
  • Herbs (Herb Seeds): the produce, Herb, can be eaten and also restores health.
  • Berry bushes (Berry Seeds): perennial; the produce is Berries.

The seeds of these three are bought on the “Farming” page of the stall; no map has them planted. Herbs and berries can also be picked up in the wild (see Foraging). They have no sowing option in Building mode; hold the seeds in your hand to sow them.

Selling the harvest

Take the harvest to the stall on the Held Ground and sell it on the “Sell” page; prices are in the “produce price” column of the table above, in Dawnmarks. Everything the stall buys and for how much is in What the stall buys.

Trees

An Acorn planted becomes an oak sapling and grows into a tree in two game days. It needs no farmland and no watering, only open sky and no one standing on it (see Woodcutting). Acorns drop when you chop trees, and the stall sells them too.

Tips

  • To stock up on seeds, cut with a sickle: 2 seeds every time.
  • A fertilized crop (with ash or fertilizer) gives 1 more produce when cut.
  • A crop that fruits again needs its seeds bought once; after that, one watering, one harvest.
  • To earn money, look at the “produce price” in the table: among annuals, a single cauliflower or melon is worth a dozen or twenty coins.
