Perspective

Top-down 3/4 view, 16 pixels to a cell. One storey of height equals one cell of depth: things on the second storey are drawn one cell higher (see Storeys).

Asset pack

The ground, buildings, most people, monsters and item icons come from Ninja Adventure (by Pixel-boy and AAA, CC0 license).

Art direction

On 2026-10-02 the team discussed switching to anime-style, higher-quality assets: no existing asset pack met all three of “anime style, high quality, releasable with open-source code”, so Ninja Adventure was kept at that time.

From 2026-10-09, people are drawn in anime style, with assets generated by the project itself (Looks):

  • Portrait parts are painted one by one by the open-source model Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (Apache-2.0), running locally and editing the same base figure; a program then splits them into layers and turns them into recolorable shading maps. The generation pipeline and the prompts are in the repository, and the output is released with the code.
  • People on the map became 16×32 anime-style chibi pixel figures, assembled from pixel parts in code, one cell taller than Ninja Adventure's 16×16 people. Monsters and terrain still use Ninja Adventure.
  • From 2026-10-17, the figures became 24×36 in FFBE style (the style and palette set after the Cloud pixel art the user provided): a local model draws the draft, which is set on the pixel grid and touched up by hand; walking swings the arms in turn, and standing has a frame of its own.
  • The portrait, the map figure and the appearance tags come from the same look record, so they always agree.

Portraits of their own and story illustrations

From 2026-10-24 the key people have portraits of their own, the first being Nessa Quill, the second Lumi, the third Lina, the fourth Sister Ilse, among the men The Grey Man and Tobin, and the seventh Hesper, the first person the player meets on coming down the mountain (the designs below).

  • The design: an online image model draws a turnaround, an expression sheet and the portrait from a brief, and the team chooses; the style follows how a visual novel's official character designs are drawn (the face's proportions, fine line, pale colour), and no artist or character is named in a prompt. From the second person on, the brief uploads Nessa's turnaround as the style reference, so that everyone looks drawn by the same hand; the local models were tried and did not reach it.
  • Made into the game's pictures (locally): cut out, the white taken from the hair's edge (a feather, the ornaments and the face kept apart), each expression's features redrawn by the model after the expression sheet (where the sheet's face moves the brows, the brows too, one brow left over each eye), and the blinking and speaking frames only the eyes or the mouth, so the frames line up to the pixel and never flicker; a speaking frame opens each expression's own mouth across its own width, so it opens and closes. The pipeline: games/openworld/tools/src/art/portraits/.
  • In the dialogue box: set inside the box and cut at the shoulders, 200 px, smaller than a look's bust, so it does not take over the scene; everyone faces right, toward the line, the head in the middle of what the box shows. A large portrait standing at the side of the screen or in its middle was tried and left: on a pixel map it takes too much room; the moments that want a large picture get a story illustration.
  • Story illustrations (CGs): for each, references found by keyword first, then a shot spec of the camera, how much of the frame the person takes and the light; a local model draws the whole picture, makes the person themself again, and refines it at the game's size. The pipeline: games/openworld/tools/src/art/cg/.
