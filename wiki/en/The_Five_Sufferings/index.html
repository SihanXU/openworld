The Five Sufferings

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Lore
The Chinese page has changed since this translation was made: some of this may be out of date.

The Five Sufferings are the five holy torments Astra willingly bore during the first Long Night. When the fifth torment was complete, the sun rose again. The Church of the Five Sufferings takes its name and its doctrine from these five. Each Suffering corresponds to an instrument of torment, one of the Instruments of the Sufferings, and later also to a Seal-bearer.

Order Torment Instrument of the Sufferings Seal-bearer The Church's reading
First Suffering the Crowning Crown of Thorns Alberic, the White Knight Giving up human pride and will
Second Suffering the Scourging the Pillar of Scourging Matthias, Giver of War Repaying everyone's sins with the flesh
Third Suffering the Starving the Scales of Hunger Ehle, Priest of the Black Scale Freedom from the desires of the flesh
Fourth Suffering the Nailing the Holy Wood of the Nails Nacht, Holder of Death's Reins Accepting death
Fifth Suffering the Piercing the Lance That Pierced the Heart The Thousand White Witnesses Beneath the Altar —

Each section first gives the Church's reading (the account everyone believes early in the game), then puts what the original lore text says each one really does inside a spoiler block.

When Sister Ilse tells the story of the Five Sufferings in the game, she says:

Then Astra came. He took the crown of thorns. He was scourged at the pillar. He starved upon the scales. He was nailed to the holy wood. And when the spear went through his heart, the sun rose.

The Crowning

The First Suffering. Instrument of the Sufferings: the Crown of Thorns.

A crown of thorns covered in barbs was set on Astra's head. The thorns pierced his skull, yet it was never taken off.

The Church's reading: giving up human pride and will.

What the Crowning really does (click to show)

It wears down the bearer's boundaries of self, so that his mind can withstand the intrusion of the First Night (§4).

In Chapter 1, this is exactly what the crown does to Alberic, the White Knight: after three hundred years (a proposal of Chapter 1's design), he remembers only that he must “hold”, not what he holds, nor anyone's name. Fennick was his squire in the two hundred and fiftieth year of his wearing the crown: “But the crown kept taking. Every year I was with him, there was a little less of him. One morning he looked at me and asked me my name.”

In the game: Chapter 1, “The First Seal: the Crown of Thorns”, is built entirely around this Suffering. The Shrine of the Crown on the Pilgrim's Road reads: “THE FIRST SUFFERING: THE CROWN. They pressed the thorns into his brow, and he did not lift his hand. Pilgrim, lay down what you wear upon your head, and your pride with it.” The Crown of Thorns is the only Instrument of the Sufferings that has appeared in the game so far, and it can be worn on the head; its experience penalty (see Crown of Thorns) and the Boon Lumi catches from it, “Crown's Insight” (experience +100%), both come from the same thing: the Crown of Thorns wears the self thin.

The Scourging

The Second Suffering. Instrument of the Sufferings: the Pillar of Scourging.

Astra was bound to a pillar and scourged at length. Legend says that even when his blood had all run out, he still did not fall.

The Church's reading: repaying everyone's sins with the flesh.

What the Scourging really does (click to show)

It lets the flesh gradually adapt to the constant pain and transformation the First Night brings (§4). The second Seal-bearer, Matthias, Giver of War, bears the bodily pain of the scourging (§12).

In the game: Ilse's story and the oath at the Pilgrims' Rest mention it. The Shrine of the Pillar on the Pilgrim's Road reads: “THE SECOND SUFFERING: THE SCOURGE. Bound to the pillar, he bled for every one of us. Pilgrim, lay down a cord: the scourge that was spared you.” The penitent Brother Ezra whips himself; at the Rest, he says, they taught him that “every stroke I take is one Astra need not.” The corresponding Chapter 2 has not been designed yet.

The Starving

The Third Suffering. Instrument of the Sufferings: the Scales of Hunger.

Astra gave up food and drink and let his body be weighed on the scales against grain, against sin and against life. Scripture preserves one line:

“His flesh at last weighed less than sin.”

The Church's reading: freedom from the desires of the flesh.

What the Starving really does (click to show)

It cuts the bearer's dependence on food, sleep and the ordinary needs of life (§4). The third Seal-bearer, Ehle, Priest of the Black Scale, bears the hunger and the wasting of life (§12).

In the game: Ilse's story and the oath at the Pilgrims' Rest mention it. The Shrine of the Scales on the Pilgrim's Road reads: “THE THIRD SUFFERING: HUNGER. He took no food, and was weighed upon the scales. His flesh at last weighed less than sin. Pilgrim, lay down a meal you will not eat.” The corresponding Chapter 3 has not been designed yet.

The Nailing

The Fourth Suffering. Instrument of the Sufferings: the Holy Wood of the Nails.

Astra's hands and feet were pierced by holy nails, and he was fixed to the holy wood.

The Church's reading: accepting death.

What the Nailing really does (click to show)

It fastens Astra firmly to the real world. Even as his soul begins to be eroded by the First Night, he does not slip out of reality entirely (§4). The fourth Seal-bearer, Nacht, Holder of Death's Reins, bears the erosion of death the holy nails stand for (§12).

In the game: Ilse's story and the oath at the Pilgrims' Rest mention it, both giving the Church's reading. During the oath Father Caius recites: “The fourth, the Nails: upon the holy wood he accepted death.” The Shrine of the Nails on the Pilgrim's Road reads: “THE FOURTH SUFFERING: THE NAILS. Nailed by his hands and feet to the holy wood, he accepted death. Pilgrim, lay down metal, as he laid down his life.” The corresponding Chapter 4 has not been designed yet.

The Piercing

The Fifth Suffering. Instrument of the Sufferings: the Lance That Pierced the Heart.

At last a holy lance pierced Astra's chest. At that very moment the sun rose again, and the Long Night ended.

The original lore text gives no separate Church reading for this Suffering.

What the Piercing really does (click to show)

When the blade went through his heart, Astra's body became, for the first time, an “entrance” joining reality and the First Night, and the First Night began to pour into his body. It was at exactly this moment that the sun rose again (§4). The fifth Seal-bearer, the Thousand White Witnesses Beneath the Altar, bears the wound to the soul the holy lance left (§12).

In the game, when Ilse reaches the lance piercing the heart, Lumi shivers. When Pell asks her about it, she says, “No, I'm not. It's just cold.” Lumi is the failed Second Vessel; see Nightbearer.

In the game: Ilse's story ends with it, and so does the oath at the Pilgrims' Rest. The Shrine of the Lance on the Pilgrim's Road reads: “THE FIFTH SUFFERING: THE LANCE. The lance pierced his heart, and the sun rose. Pilgrim, lay down a light, and the dawn will remember you.” The corresponding Chapter 5 has not been designed yet.

The Great Rite of the Five Sufferings

The Church wants the protagonist to recover the five holy instruments and finally, in the Holy City, “hold again the Great Rite of the Five Sufferings” (§7). From the protagonist's point of view, this repeats Astra's miracle of long ago.

What the Great Rite really needs (click to show)

The Great Rite of the Five Sufferings needs not only the five Instruments of the Sufferings but also a new Bearer of the Sufferings. Only one who can defeat the five Seal-bearers and bear the power of the five Seals is fit to become the new vessel (§13). In the Final Chapter the five holy instruments return to their places in the Most Holy Hall: the Crown of Thorns is set on again, the scourge marks split open again, the Scales of Hunger rise once more, the holy nails fix his body again, and the Lance That Pierced the Heart enters his chest once more (§14). In the preview ending, the same Five Sufferings fall on the protagonist one after another (§16). See Ending.

The five wounds

In the game the Five Sufferings are often drawn as five wounds:

  • Five-wound shrine stones: roadside holy marks of the Five Sufferings, carved with five wounds. On the one at the Mountain Foot the first wound (the crown) is cracked open, and when Lumi comes near she says, “It's cold near it.”
  • Below the dawnstone on Dawn Hill are carved five small wounds and a flame.
  • The altar of Firstlight Chapel, “the Five Holy Wounds”: on the stone wall around Astra's figure are carved the crown of thorns, the scourge, the scales, the holy nails and the holy lance.
  • The altar of the five wounds in the Chapel of the Five at the Pilgrims' Rest, where pilgrims take their oath; the Litany of the Five on the bookstand beside it reads: “Suffering is not God's abandonment. It is the proof the debt is being paid. As long as one bears the Five, the Long Night shall not return.”
  • The five wayside shrines on the Pilgrim's Road, one for each Suffering, each of which lights only when something is laid on it.
  • The “Amulet of the Five Wounds”, the reward of the quest The Pilgrim's Road.
  • Items of Stigmatic quality “bear one of the First Martyr's five wounds”.
