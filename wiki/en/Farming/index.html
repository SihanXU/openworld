Farming

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Gameplay

Farming is growing crops in farmland: hoe the ground into farmland, sow seeds, water once per stage, and harvest when ripe. The vegetables you harvest can be eaten and bring back seeds; a surplus can be sold at the trading stall on The Held Ground. What you can grow is in Crops. The first time you walk into the vegetable garden in Firstlight Village, Lumi begins the guide quest A Lesson in the Garden, which takes you through it all, from the toolbox to cutting, sowing and watering.

A plot from start to finish

  1. Hoe: hold a Hoe and left-click a cell of grass, dirt, sand or snow; it becomes farmland.
  2. Sow: hold seeds and right-click an empty cell of farmland (“Sow …”). The seed becomes a “… sprout”.
  3. Water: hold a container full of water and left-click the crop: “You water the ….” The container is emptied, and the crop gets watered.
  4. Wait: 1, 2 or 4 game hours depending on the kind, as the seed's and the crop's tooltips say. A crop grows one stage only if it is in the open and has water: sprout → young plant → ripe.
  5. Water again: an ordinary watering serves only one stage, so the young plant needs watering again. Once you have learned Irrigation, one watering serves two stages in a row, each at the same speed.
  6. Harvest: once it is ripe, hold a Sickle and left-click to cut it, or press E to pick it by hand.

From sowing to ripe, turnips, parsnips and radishes take as little as 2 game hours; pumpkins, cauliflowers, melons, corn and blueberries take 8 hours; the rest 4 hours. Two waterings normally, one with irrigation.

Rules

  • No water, no growth. An unwatered crop stays at its stage for good, and it does not wither either.
  • A watered crop cannot be watered again. Until the watered status ends, watering it again does nothing.
  • Rain does not water. Rain only wets people and creatures and puts out burning things; it does not count as watering.
  • It needs open sky. A crop with something over it (indoors, under a ceiling) does not grow: when its time comes it waits, looks again every game hour, and grows once it is in the open.
  • One plant per cell. You cannot sow on farmland that already has a crop.
  • It grows while you are away. When you leave the map it is frozen; when you return, it catches up on all the growing due in the meantime.
  • There are no seasons and no withering. Agriculture skills can increase the harvest and cut down on rewatering; Sprinkler gives water to the crops around too.

Harvest

How You get
Cut with a sickle 2 produce (3 if fertilized) + 2 seeds, dropped on the crop's cell
Picked by hand (E) 1 produce straight into your hands + 1 seed dropped on the ground
  • After cutting or picking, an annual crop is gone, leaving empty farmland that can be sown again at once.
  • A perennial crop goes back to the young-plant stage after harvest; water it once more and it ripens again, with no need to sow anew. The perennials are tomatoes, corn, blueberries, strawberries, hot peppers, eggplants and berry bushes. See Crops.

Fertilizing

There are two fertilizers, with the same effect:

Fertilizer Where from
Ash What is left when something burns: drop a stick into a lit campfire (hold it and press G at the fire), and when it burns out it is a heap of ash; see Elements
Fertilizer The “Farming” page of the trading stall on the Held Ground, 3 for 10 Dawnmarks

Hold ash or fertilizer and right-click a crop that is still growing (a sprout or young plant; the prompt is “Fertilize …”): “You work the ash into the soil.” (With fertilizer, “You work the fertilizer into the soil.”) One is used up, and the crop gets fertilized, giving 1 more produce when cut.

  • It can only go on a crop still growing (a sprout or young plant), once per plant.
  • A perennial crop's fertility is used up by the harvest and must be applied again.

Where water comes from

Watering takes a container full of water:

  • Filling: hold an empty container and left-click shallow water, deep water, a well, a fountain or a trough (“Fill …”).
  • You can make a bucket yourself: 3 planks and 1 cut grass (see Crafting).
  • One filling waters one plant. Once you have watered, the container is empty; go and fill it again.

Selling the harvest

The stall on the Held Ground buys wheat, the 14 vegetables, berries and herbs; prices are in What the stall buys. Stand beside the stall with your harvest, press E to open it, and sell on the “Sell” page.

Farming in Building mode

Press B to open Building mode and turn to the “Farming” page: Till Soil, sow (one entry for each of the 14 vegetables), Water, Refill Water, Harvest. Choose one, point at a cell beside you and press the left button or E; it is the same as doing it with the tool in hand, except that you need not hold the tool: the tools and seeds just have to be in your bag. Wheat Seeds, Herb Seeds and Berry Seeds have no sowing entry in Building mode; hold them to sow them.

Firstlight's vegetable garden

Firstlight Village has a vegetable garden ready to use:

  • Each of its four columns grows one crop (carrots, turnips, tomatoes, pumpkins), from top to bottom sprout, young plant and ripe; the bottom row is left empty for you to sow yourself.
  • The “garden toolbox” beside it holds 2 seeds of each crop, a hoe, a sickle, a bucket of water and a smith hammer, plus 4 planks, 4 sticks and 4 stones.
  • The ripe row can be harvested at once, and cutting brings extra seeds.
  • The garden grows only these four. The seeds of the other ten vegetables (parsnip, potato, radish, cauliflower, melon, corn, blueberry, strawberry, hot pepper, eggplant) are sold only at the stall on the Held Ground.

The Held Ground's farmland

On The Held Ground, southwest of the planted sword, there is a 5×3 patch of farmland, already tilled and ready to sow. The trading stall is on the other side of the sword, so buying seeds and fertilizer and selling the harvest are close by. The Held Ground has no well; for water, fill up at the fish pond north of the sword.

Oaks

An Acorn need not go in farmland: any ground that can be hoed (grass, dirt, sand, snow, farmland) will do. Planted, it becomes an oak sapling; it needs no watering, and in the open with no one standing on it, it grows into a tree in two game days. Acorns drop when you chop trees (see Woodcutting), and the stall on the Held Ground sells them too, at 4 Dawnmarks each.

Shovel

A Shovel digs a crop back up into one seed, and it can also dig out a stump. No map has a shovel lying about; buy one on the “Farming” page of the stall on the Held Ground (15 Dawnmarks).

Agriculture skills

The Agriculture branch already has twenty skills. Irrigation makes one watering serve two stages; with Sprinkler, the neighboring crops benefit from irrigation too, each timed by its own kind.

Tips

  • For your first farming, go straight to Firstlight's vegetable garden: the tools, seeds and water are all in the box.
  • Once you reach the Held Ground, the Farming shelf also sells hoes (12 coins), sickles (10 coins) and empty buckets (8 coins). The 30-coin welcome buys five turnip seeds and a bucket; pick five turnips by hand and sell them, and you are left with 36 coins, plus the bucket and five seeds to carry on with.
  • A bucket of water waters only one plant. If you grow a lot, break ground beside water, so the trips are short.
  • Tomatoes, corn, blueberries, strawberries, hot peppers and eggplants need no resowing after harvest; just water them.
  • Cutting gives one more produce and one more seed than picking. Your seeds keep multiplying: buy them once and you never need to again.
  • To earn money, look first at the “produce price” in the Crops table: a single cauliflower or melon is worth a dozen or twenty Dawnmarks.

Related skills

The twenty skills of the Agriculture branch: bigger harvests, more seeds, hoeing 3×3 at once, watering one plant to wet a patch, making your own compost, and mastery of each kind of crop (see Agriculture skills).
