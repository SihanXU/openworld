“I've seen the grey man. Three times! Nobody believes me. He gave me a stone that's warm at night.”

— Pell

Pell is Marta's nephew, 9 years old, and he loves drawing on walls in chalk. He says he has seen the Grey Man three times, and nobody believes him. The man gave him a stone that is warm at night; since he got it, the things of the night have not come near his window.

The first time the player comes into the village, he spots Lumi at once: “A fairy! Can I draw her? Can I? Don't move, either of you!” At dusk, when Sister Ilse tells the story of the First Long Night, he is the one who begged her to tell it, and the one who notices that Lumi is trembling.

Quests

  • Pell's Drawings (Side, giver)
  • Pell's Drawings: he drew a picture at every place he saw the Grey Man, four in all: beside the five-wound shrine stone at Mountain Foot, at the door of The Old Barrow, by the little bridge at The Ford, and by the holy flame in the village. Press E to look at each one. Once you have found them all, he gives the player another warm stone and the “Chalk-dusted Cloak”.

Portrait

Lore

Laid side by side, the four drawings say that the Grey Man always came first: each time on the night before something bad happened. Pell's conclusion is that “he didn't do the bad things. He just got there first.” See The Grey Man and Chapter 1.

What the stone is (click to show)

The warm stone is an ember taken from the dawn's holy flame. It is why the things of the night stay away from Pell's window.

Trivia

  • After the flame is stolen, he says: “Now they believe me, but nobody will talk to me. The grown-ups are all arguing.”
  • After Chapter 1 ends, he asks: “Is he a bad man? ...He looked tired.”
