Where the Birds Went is a Chapter 1 side quest given by Orrin, the charcoal burner of The Charcoal Dell. No birdsong is heard in the Forest; Orrin says the birds all settle every dusk on the old oak at the west end of his dell, without a sound. He asks you to ask Gus whether the Forest has always been this quiet (Gus will not talk to him about it), and to pick up 3 feathers from under the old oak for him: he wants to know what birds they are.

The quest has no prerequisites.

Objectives

  1. Ask Gus about the quiet
    Talk to Gus
  2. Feathers from under the roost
    Carry Feather×3
  3. Back to Orrin
    Talk to Orrin

The two objectives can be done in either order. The feathers are taken when you hand them in.

Walkthrough

  1. Take the quest: go to the Charcoal Dell and find Orrin, who keeps by the clamp by day (6:00–21:00); press E on him and choose “Accept ‘Where the Birds Went’” in the dialogue box.
  2. Ask Gus: in the morning (6:00–12:00) Gus is felling trees in the clearing on the north side of the Forest, and in the afternoon he is stacking wood by the wood pile on the west side of Firstlight Village. Press E on him and he says: “It went quiet the night the bridge came down. Every bird in the wood went up at once and flew west. I've not told anyone. Who wants to hear a woodcutter talk about birds?”
  3. Pick up feathers: the old oak is at the west end of the Charcoal Dell, ringed by trees, with a path leading to it. Four feathers lie scattered beneath it; press E to pick up 3.
  4. Hand in: go back to Orrin and press E to hand in.

Dialogue

When offering
“You came through Hushwood. Hear any birds? No. They are all in my oak, west of here, every dusk, and not a note out of them. Ask Gus if it was always like that; he will not tell me. And bring me three of their feathers. I want to know what kind they are.”
While unfinished
“Ask Gus about Hushwood. The feathers are under the oak at the west end of the dell; they drop them at dawn when they go.”
When talking to Gus
“Quiet? ...It went quiet the night the bridge came down. Every bird in it got up at once and flew west. I never told anyone. Nobody wants to hear a woodcutter go on about birds.”
On handing in
“Every kind. Thrush, finch, jay, birds that never roost together. And Gus says it began the night the bridge came down. I have burned charcoal under that oak for forty years. They never cared for it before.”
What the birds are waiting for (click to show)

When you hand in, Orrin says: every kind of bird is there — thrushes, goldfinches, jays, birds that never roost together; and Gus says it began the night the bridge went. “Then it isn't my oak they want. It's what can be seen from it.”

Go to the old oak at dusk (17:00–21:00) and a scene plays: hundreds of birds crowd the branches, not one making a sound, all of them facing east. Lumi says that, like the kneeling cattle on the Grassland, the birds know before people do that something has happened. See The Charcoal Dell.
