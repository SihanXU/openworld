Ending

From the OpenWorld Wiki · Story
Planned: what is written here is settled design, not yet in the game.

The main story has two endings. The content of the preview ending, its closing image and its last line are settled in the original lore text, and the current version makes only this ending; the game has not reached it yet. The true ending has not yet been designed; the original lore text leaves only the question it is to answer.

The preview ending

The New Nightbearer (§16) (click to show)
  • The player finally defeats Astra. As the First Martyr dies, the First Night, sealed within him for centuries, begins to run out of control, and darkness spreads swiftly from the hall. At last the player understands: “Killing Astra does not kill the Long Night.”
  • The five Instruments of the Sufferings appear before the protagonist. The protagonist makes a choice: “Inherit the Five Sufferings.”
  • The Crown of Thorns settles on the protagonist's head. The scourge's wounds appear on their body. The Scales of Hunger come back into balance. The holy nails fix their flesh. The Lance That Pierced the Heart enters their chest. The First Night begins to leave Astra's corpse and enters the protagonist entirely.
  • Outside, in the world: the sun rises again. The ever-lengthening nights end. People celebrate: “The Second Long Night was stopped.”
  • But in the depths of the Most Holy Hall, the player can no longer leave. The protagonist takes Astra's place, becoming the new Nightbearer and, in the religious history to come, “the Second First Martyr”.

The closing image

The closing image (§17) (click to show)

The game ends very quietly:

  • The scene cuts to the village where the game began. For the first time, the sun rises in full. The villagers open their windows. Children see a true morning for the first time. Bells ring in the distance.
  • Then the camera returns to the Most Holy Hall underground. No music. Only the protagonist sitting in the dark, the holy lance still in their chest, the darkness behind them slowly writhing.
  • Finally, one line appears:

“As long as someone bears the Five Sufferings, the dawn will not go out.”

  • The screen goes black.

The true ending

To be designed: this part is not designed yet; below is only what is settled. To make a proposal, click “Talk” at the top of the page.

It is not revealed in the preview version. The original lore text says a later full version could turn on one central question:

Can the First Night really only be borne forever by someone?

That is, whether there is a way to break the endless cycle “Astra → the protagonist → the next Nightbearer”. The true ending could reinterpret:

  • where the First Night really comes from;
  • why the Church of the Five Sufferings came into being;
  • whether Astra knew of other possibilities;
  • whether the system of Nightbearers is a law of nature, or a mistake kept up for far too long;
  • whether there is a third way that needs no one to be sacrificed.

In the original lore text's own words: at this stage, the specific answer is not yet decided.

The game's current ending

The last scene currently playable in the game is the end card of the older storyline Emberfall and the Dragon: after you defeat the dragon of Cinder Peak, it shows “The dragon of Cinder Peak. The dragon is dead. Emberfall will sleep tonight, and the road east is open. Everything the world learned from you is in the Threadbook (J).” That storyline predates this setting and is not the ending this page is about. The last scene of the main story actually finished is the end card of Chapter 1.
