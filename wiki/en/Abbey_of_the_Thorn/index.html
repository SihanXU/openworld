The Abbey of the Thorn stands on the hill north of the middle of the Pilgrim's Road. It is the old seat of the Order of the Thorn, and the place where Alberic was knighted. It lies abandoned now, yet the dead monks still keep the hours of prayer. The Order's knights are buried in The Ossuary of the Thorn beneath Thornwood Hollow; their memorial stones stand in the abbey's court.

It is a combat zone.

Areas

  • The Thorn Yard: the weed-grown court in the middle. In it stands the Statue in the Thorn Yard (an altar plinth); press E to read: “A young knight on one knee before a child, holding out a wooden horse he has mended. The face is worn smooth by hands. The plinth: ALBERIC, ON THE DAY HE WAS MADE A KNIGHT.” The first time you enter the court, Lumi says: “There's a statue in the middle of the yard. It's the only thing here nobody knocked down.”
  • Memorials and grave: a row of four stones on the south side of the court. Three are the Order's memorial stones, the people themselves buried in the Ossuary of the Thorn:
    • “BROTHER ODO OF THE THORN, who held the gate at Varn for three days. He lies with his brothers under Thornwood Hollow; his name is kept here.”
    • “SISTER MAREN OF THE THORN, who carried water to both armies. She lies with the Order under Thornwood Hollow; her name is kept here.”
    • “BROTHER TOMAS OF THE THORN, sworn brother to the Commander, sent down to ward the Order's dead under the hollow. No year of death is cut on the stone.” (He is the warden in The Warden's Sanctum.)
    • The fourth is the gardener's grave: “BROTHER GILES, GARDENER, buried in the yard he kept. Rose canes, long dead, are still tied to the stone.”
  • The Chapter Hall: the long house northwest of the court, its door facing south. See The Chapter Hall.
  • The abbey church: the small stone church northeast of the court, its door facing south, with The Abbey Crypt beneath it.

Lore

The Order and the Writ of Relief (click to show)

The Order's roll ends with Alberic: “after him, no one else was knighted”. His journal records that the Prior called the Crowning an honor, and his brother Hugh called it a sentence; “forty years, and then I am to be relieved”; then, “The forty-first year. No one came.” The Writ of Relief in the hall is the very writ that should have relieved him, its wax whole, never sent, and on its back is written: “No one could be found who would bear it. Let him go on holding.”

The one who held it back was the abbey's prior. Prior Wendel still keeps watch in the crypt after his death, guarding the Order's seal.
