The **fire slime** is a slime of the fire element. There is only one in the whole game, living beside the [[slime burrow|slime burrow]] at the foot of the cliff north of [[The Ford]]. Its temper is wary: if you do not hit it, it will not attack you.

## Spawning
{{habitat}}

- **The Ford**: 1. By day it stays near the slime burrow; at night it roams the dry grass in front of the burrow. Once felled, it does not come back.

## Behavior
- **Temper: wary.** Only those who have hit it are chased and attacked. See [[Factions and Temper]].
- **Speed: slow.** About 0.7 seconds per tile.
- **Element: fire.** Its attacks carry fire, and whoever it hits catches {{status|burning}} (someone {{status|wet}} is only steamed dry).
- **Does not fear fire.** It carries fire itself, and a campfire does not keep it back.
- **Sets fires.** When it touches or bumps into something dry and flammable, it sets it alight, for example the Ford's haystacks and dry grass.
- **Fire cannot hurt it.** Fire-element hits instead restore 2 health to it; water does it more damage and leaves it wet.

## Skills
{{monster skills}}

- **Hop**: at 2–3 tiles away, it lights red marks on the 3×3 tiles around your feet, then 0.8 seconds later leaps over and lands beside you, crushing whoever is in the marked area for attack × 1.5 damage, carrying its own element. When you see the marks, step out of those 9 tiles.

## Strategy
- Leave it alone and you will have no trouble; the Ford itself is a [[Peaceful and Combat Zones|peaceful zone]].
- If you want to fight it, do not use fire: use water (for example a [[Bucket|Bucket]] filled with water) or a weapon with no element.
- It sets hay alight, so fight it well away from the camp's hay and wood.
- If it sets you alight, rain, walking into mud or being splashed with water all put the fire out. See [[Elements]].

## Drops
{{drops}}

Once felled, it leaves the materials listed above lying where it fell; press {{key|E}} to pick them up (see [[Monster Drops]]). Felling it gives experience equal to its maximum health.
