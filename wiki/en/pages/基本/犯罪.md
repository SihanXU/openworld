Crime in OpenWorld works as in The Elder Scrolls: **it counts only if someone sees it**. If no one sees, what you stole is simply stolen; if someone does, they remember who did it, tell others, and the watch comes looking for you. These rules are the same for everyone: villagers steal too, and are seen by others and taken away by the guards too (see [[People's Needs]]).

## Whose is it
Whom a thing belongs to is decided in this order:
1. It names its owner itself (for example the goods on [[Tobin]]'s wagon, or the bread in [[Hesper]]'s chest);
2. otherwise, whose hands it is in: what a person carries is theirs; what is in a chest or cupboard belongs to the chest's owner;
3. otherwise, where it lies: a thing on the step in front of a household's door belongs to that household.
4. The house itself too: the walls you see from outside belong to whoever owns the rooms its door leads into (all but the door's cell), in the wild and in the cities alike.

**A household's** things are shared by all of it: [[Marta]], [[Mia]], [[Pell]] and [[Lina]] are one household (the cook's family), [[Holt]], [[Fennick]] and [[Oskar]] are one (the guards' house), [[Gus]] and [[Cass]] are one, and so on.

When a thing is taken by someone who is not its owner, it **still belongs to its owner**: the stolen bread tucked in your bag is still Marta's. Only being handed to someone (as a gift, a sale, or giving it back) changes its owner. On merchants' shelves and in your bag, stolen things are marked “not yours”. Press {{key|E}} on someone else's thing lying loose on the ground or a table, and the prompt reads “Steal …”; pressing it first asks “Steal …?”, and the thing is taken only when you confirm: this is theft.

## Crimes at a glance

| Crime | What counts | Fine | Victim's opinion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Murder | Killing a person | 1000 (not pardoned by the watch) | −100 |
| Assault | Wounding a person | 40 | −35 |
| Pickpocketing | Taking something from someone's person (see [[Sneaking and Pickpocketing]]) | 25 | −25 |
| Arson | Setting fire to someone else's things | 40 | −30 |
| Theft | Taking someone else's thing | Half the thing's value, at least 5 | −15 |
| Lockpicking | Picking someone else's lock with a {{item|Lockpick}} | 5 | −8 |
| Vandalism | Smashing someone else's thing, hacking at the walls of someone's house among it (the walls do not give way, but the cracks are the evidence; see [[Durability and Breaking#The walls of houses|The walls of houses]]) | 10 | −10 |
| Trespassing | Lingering at someone's door at night and not leaving after a warning | 5 | −5 |

A single act counts only as its most serious crime: killing someone is murder, with no separate count of assault. “Victim's opinion” is how far their [[Friendship|opinion]] of you drops: the victim and the people of their household drop by this much, their relatives and friends (named in their relationships) by 60% of it, guards by half, and other passers-by by a quarter.

## Who can see
- A person can see 7 cells (halved after dark when far from a fire), and not through walls, trees or rocks in between. A sleeping person sees nothing.
- While [[Sneaking and Pickpocketing|sneaking]] ({{key|V}}), only people within half their sight range who have you **in front** of them can possibly see you, and it takes luck too.
- When a hand goes into someone's pocket, the person is the most likely to notice, and onlookers less so.

## Do they know you
Seeing it does not mean knowing who it was:
- **People who know you** (who have talked with you, met you up close, or are your relatives, friends or household) recognize your face when they see it. Each thing hiding your face gives them a chance of not recognizing you: wearing a {{item|Hood}} a half, a helmet a little over a third, darkness when far from a fire a little over a third, standing more than 4 cells away a little over a fifth; when these add up to full cover (a hood plus night plus distance, say), they certainly do not recognize you.
- **People who do not know you** see you clearly only within 2 cells, with light, and with no hood or helmet, and from then on they remember your face.
- When they cannot tell who you are, they remember **what you looked like**: “someone in a hood and a long cloak”. If you later come within 4 cells of them wearing the same clothes, they recognize you: “You! It was you — I remember that face!” Change your clothes or take off the hood, and the look no longer matches.

So a hood is a disguise: for what you do wearing it, the witnesses remember only the hood.

## After being seen
- **Guards** ([[Holt]], [[Fennick]]) who see a crime report it to [[Guards and Bounties|the watch]] on the spot, and the bounty takes effect at once; seeing a killing or a beating, they also move straight in to make an arrest.
- **The victim** (whose thing was stolen, or who was hit) cries out: “Hey — that is mine!” If a thing was stolen, they walk up to you and demand it back: “That is mine — give it back.” Give it back to them ({{key|G}}, or choose “Give back what I took.” in the dialogue) and the matter is dropped; if you do not, they go to report it an hour and a half later. If they were hit, they cry “Help! Guards!”, and everyone within 8 cells who can fight, and the guards, rush in, while the others get out of the way.
- **Passers-by** shout “Thief! A cutpurse!” or “Murder!”, and go to report it half an hour later. People like [[Tobin]] who prefer to mind their own business say nothing even when they see it.
- However many people report a crime, the watch fines it only once.
- When someone steals a villager's thing (other than food), and the owner sees it with their own eyes and knows who it was, the owner asks you to get the thing back: the commission [[What Was Taken]].

## Gossip
When people meet they talk about recent events: when two waking people stand within 3 cells of each other, every 15 minutes (game time) there is a 30% chance that the one who knows some news tells it to the other: “Did you hear? Vigo picked Marta's pocket (Bread).” Anything within three days counts as news. Hearing about something also lowers the listener's opinion of you, but by only half as much as seeing it with their own eyes.

To keep a witness quiet: “Bribe” in [[Dialogue|dialogue]] makes them forget the matter; “I'm sorry” works too, if the roll succeeds. (Before 2026-10-18 there was also “Intimidate”, since removed.)

“Rob” and “Steal” in [[Dialogue|dialogue]] are another matter: they compare strength or finesse face to face, the money and things you get are yours outright, not recorded as stolen goods, and they are not crimes in the sense meant here; the price is a beating when you fail, and a steep fall in their opinion of you.

## History
- 2026-10-02: Early versions had only “take a thing from its owner in front of them, and they say it is theirs”.
- 2026-10-09: Added the full crime system: ownership, crimes, witnessing, recognition and disguise, memory, gossip, and the watch (see [[Guards and Bounties]]).
- 2026-10-09: Theft between villagers, when seen by the owner, gives rise to the commission [[What Was Taken]].
- 2026-10-25: A house's walls belong to the owner of the rooms behind its door: hacking at someone's wall when seen is vandalism. (The walls do not give way: there is no hole to stand in.)
- 2026-10-21: The controls were settled as four inputs: using a held thing on something became the right click (the left click is the held thing's own use: a tool or weapon is swung, anything else is used on yourself), and picking up loose things became {{key|E}} (see [[Controls]]).
