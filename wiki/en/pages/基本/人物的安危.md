The people and monsters of the world know what is bad for them and find a way to keep away from it or put it right. This is not a reaction written for rain and another for fire: a person reads the world's own rules — what happens if they stand here, touch this, use that — so rules the world learns later count just the same (see [[The World Learns]]).

## What counts as bad
- **What harms**: bearing it costs health now and then, for example {{status|burning}}. This is a danger and comes before everything else (except a boss in a boss fight): it is seen to before schedules, fights and anything else.
- **What is only unpleasant**: {{status|wet}}, {{status|oiled}}, {{status|frozen}}, {{status|hungry}}. People deal with it before going to work, but never at the cost of something that harms.
- **Health under half**: they look for something or somewhere that heals.
- **The place underfoot**: if standing there makes them worse (standing on a burning cell, out in the rain), the trouble is the place, and leaving puts it right.

Not everyone minds the same things: farmers, guards, fishers, hunters, soldiers, sailors and ferrykeepers work out in the wind and rain and do not mind getting wet.

## What they do
They take the easiest of these:
1. **Leave**: get away from the place that makes them worse, for example step off a burning cell, or go somewhere covered when it rains. If there is nowhere near, they go back to one of their own places (home, the house they work in), as long as it does not have the same trouble.
2. **Go where it is put right**: for example, on fire, run to muddy ground — mud soaks whoever stands in it, and wet puts the fire out.
3. **Use what is at hand**: what they carry, or what lies about that they may take (their own, their household's, or nobody's — never someone else's). For example, eat the food they carry when hungry, or dry off by a lit fire when wet.
4. **Make something first**: if using something at hand on something nearby turns it into what they need, they make it first. For example, take an empty bucket to the water, fill it, then throw it on the fire.

If the trouble goes away halfway, they stop; if what they made is not right, they try another way. If they find no way at all, they think again a little later.

## Looking after their household and their things
People do not only look after themselves: if the companions they travel with, the people of their household, or their own and their household's things (a house's walls, a chest) bear something that harms them, that is their danger too, only after their own. When a house catches fire, the people who live in it fetch water in a bucket to save it.

## Rainy days
- When it rains, those who do not want to get wet go indoors, do not walk back out into the rain until it stops, and then go out again on their [[Residents|schedule]].
- Walk onto a map while it is raining and these people are indoors from the start.
- Those with a schedule for rain or storms (for example [[Hesper]]) keep to it, and are wherever it says.

## History
- 2026-10-28: People keep themselves safe: they keep away from places and states that harm them, go indoors in the rain, run to wet ground when on fire, and fetch water in a bucket to save their own things from fire; those who cannot buy and have little conscience take whatever would put them right, not only food.
