The **Nightbearer** is an identity the *Church of the Five Sufferings* setting reveals only late in the main story. It explains the true place of the [[The Five Sufferings|Five Sufferings]], the [[Seal-bearers|Seal-bearers]] and [[Astra|Astra]] in this world, and it decides the [[Ending|Ending]].

Almost everything on this page concerns the main story's second reversal. Readers who have not played that far and do not want to know can read the first layer of understanding in [[Main Story|Main Story]] first.

{{spoiler|What the Nightbearer is}}

**The Vessel**

- During the first Long Night, Astra discovered that “the First Night cannot be destroyed,” but that it can be “given a vessel.” If a living being strong enough carries the whole of the First Night within itself, the First Night can no longer invade the world (§3).
- An ordinary person's body and soul cannot withstand such power, so Astra designed the Five Sufferings: each one remakes an ordinary person into a being able to hold the First Night: wearing down the boundaries of self, adapting the flesh to pain and transformation, cutting the dependence on food and sleep, fastening the person to reality, and at last opening an “entrance” at the heart (§3, §4).
- Astra was the first Nightbearer. He locked the First Night inside himself and for centuries, neither dead nor alive, has existed as a vast vessel (§10).

**The rule of the world**

- “The First Night cannot be killed.” It can only be “carried”. The world must always have a Nightbearer (§11).
- If the Nightbearer dies outright with no new vessel to take their place, the First Night sealed within is released all at once, and the true Long Night swallows the world entirely in a short time (§11).
- So the most deeply hidden doctrine of the Church of the Five Sufferings is: “Someone must always bear the Five Sufferings.” In other words: “There must always be an Astra.” (§11)

**The Sifting**

- The Great Rite of the Five Sufferings needs not only the five [[Instruments of the Sufferings|Instruments of the Sufferings]] but also “a new Bearer of the Sufferings”. Only one who can defeat the five Seal-bearers and bear the power of the five Seals is fit to become the new vessel. The whole pilgrimage is in fact also a “Sifting” (§13).
- Astra cannot simply let the protagonist take his place: if the new vessel cannot withstand it, the world will only be destroyed sooner. Only “one who can kill the present Nightbearer” is fit to succeed him. The final battle is “the last trial” (§15).

**The Second Vessel: Lumi**

The team's decision (2026-10-05): [[Lumi|Lumi]] is **the Second Vessel**. Long ago, someone tried to bear the Five Sufferings after Astra and could not withstand them. The Five Sufferings took her power and her memory and left a small fairy who does not know what she once was. When and why that attempt happened is left to later chapters (docs/chapter-1.md §3b).

- She can catch the power of a Seal that scatters when its Seal-bearer falls (her [[Boons|Boons]]), because what remains of her still answers to the Seals, like water finding an old riverbed. She thinks of them as gifts.
- Chapter 1 shows only the edges: she shivers beside the [[five-wound shrine stone|five-wound shrine stone]] (“It's colder near it... I don't know why I don't like it.”); she does not know how old she is; the [[Church Courier|Church Courier]] stares at her for a long time and says nothing; [[The Grey Man|the Grey Man]] looks at her a moment too long after the White Knight falls. On the East Road the Grey Man says to her: “You of all of them should know, little one. ...No. You would not remember.”
- The last page of the watchman's log on [[Cloudtop|Cloudtop]] reads: “Found a small light asleep in the snow by the cairn, warm as a coal. It woke and asked me how old it was.” When Lumi reads it, she says she does not remember any snow.
- Her lines stay light: the sadness is left for players to discover for themselves, never spoken by her.

**The new Nightbearer**

- In the preview ending, the protagonist defeats Astra and the First Night runs out of control. The protagonist understands that “killing Astra does not kill the Long Night” and chooses to “inherit the Five Sufferings”. The whole of the First Night enters the protagonist. Outside, the sun rises over the world; in the depths of the Most Holy Hall, the protagonist takes Astra's place, becoming the new Nightbearer, and, in the religious history to come, “the Second First Martyr” (§16).
- The question the true ending means to answer is: “Can the First Night really only be borne forever by someone?” Whether there is a way to break the endless cycle “Astra → the protagonist → the next Nightbearer”; whether the system of Nightbearers is a law of nature, or a mistake kept up for far too long. The answer is not yet decided (§18). See [[Ending|Ending]].

## In the game

The word “Nightbearer” does not yet appear in the game. The only things related to it that are built so far are Lumi's [[Boons|Boons]] (“Crown's Insight”, given by the First Seal) and the clues at the edges in Chapter 1 described above.
