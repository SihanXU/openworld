Besides its plain attack, every kind of monster has a skill or two of its own: [[green slime|slimes]] hop next to you, [[wolf|wolves]] lunge along a straight line, [[boar|boars]] lower their heads and charge, [[cave spider|cave spiders]] spit webs to bind you… As with [[Bosses|bosses]], a monster marks the cells it will strike on the ground before the move, and the blow lands a moment later, so every move can be dodged.

## How to tell a move is coming
- **The move's name rises over its head**, such as “Lunge” or “Web Spit”.
- **Marks light up on the ground**: the marked cells are the cells that will be struck, and the color deepens as the blow draws near.
  - **Red**: damage.
  - **Purple**: control, such as webbed, Mired, stunned.
  - **Amber**: calling companions or strengthening itself, such as Howl or Shell Up. These moves mark no cells.
- **The monster takes a stance**: one about to leap squashes down, one about to charge draws back, one about to smash all round puffs up, a caster sways side to side, one about to howl rears up trembling. The fuller the stance, the nearer the blow.
- **The monster flashes white**: the wind-up is nearly over, and the blow is about to land.
- **Listen**: casting and howling make a sound as they begin; leaps, charges and smashes make one as they land.

The marks are fixed the moment the move begins: walk away and it still strikes the cells it marked.

## How to answer
- **Step out of the marks.** Step one cell sideways and the straight-line moves (Lunge, Charge, Bone Toss, Swoop) miss.
- **Interrupt it.** Stunned, frozen or bound during the wind-up (by handing it a {{item|Rope}}, for example), the move is wasted.
- **Use the ground.** A [[boar|boar]]'s charge cannot stop: ram it into a wall, a tree or a stone and it is the one stunned.
- **Strike back during the wind-up.** A [[stone golem|golem]]'s Ground Slam has the longest wind-up; when it raises its fists is the time to hit it.

## Footwork
Besides their moves, some monsters move about as they fight:
- **Keeping their distance**: when you close in on a [[fire imp|fire imp]], a [[storm wisp|storm wisp]] or a [[ghost|ghost]] and it cannot use its move, it backs away to a distance where it can. Chase it as you strike, or drive it into a corner.
- **Fleeing when badly hurt**: [[wolf|wolves]] and [[frost wolf|frost wolves]] turn and flee below two-tenths of their health (they cannot run beyond their range).
- **Flanking**: when one wolf is already on you, another circles round to your other side. Fight a pack with your back to a wall or a tree, and they cannot get round.

## Where they come from
In the story of [[The Five Sufferings|the Five Sufferings]], when the First Night seeped into the world, it first showed itself as “plants and animals began to turn strange, and at night creatures appeared that did not belong to the real world.” These moves are that strangeness: beasts grown fiercer, the dead who will not rest, things that come by night and do not keep this world's rules.

## How the rules are written
A monster's move is an ordinary act too: it “uses” its own body on the cells it fixed, with the move's name carried along as a parameter. What happens when the move lands (damage, an element, webbing, a knockback…) is answered by that kind of monster's own rules, like every other rule in the game. So [[The World Learns|the world learns]] can write new moves for new things too.

The minor monsters' moves and the [[Bosses|bosses']] moves are **one system**: marking cells, fixing them, the wind-up and the landing are all the same, and the damage is always a multiple of the caster's attack. The only difference is how a move is picked: minor monsters use them as they come, by distance, cooldown and chance; bosses take them in turn, by phase. So one move can have a large and a small version: the [[boar|boar]]'s Charge and the [[the dragon of Cinder Peak|dragon of Cinder Peak]]'s Searing Dive are both a “charge”, and both stun themselves on a wall; the [[dragon whelp|dragon whelp]]'s Ember Breath is the small one of the dragon's Cinder Breath.
