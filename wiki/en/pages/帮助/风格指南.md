title: Style Guide
summary: How to write: an encyclopedia's voice, facts only, the game's own names, a source for every fact, and a faithful translation of the Chinese page.
---
This wiki is OpenWorld's encyclopedia, and also the game's design proposal: what the game already has is described as it is now; what is decided but not built is marked "Planned"; what is not decided yet is marked "To be designed".

The English edition is a translation of the Chinese one, which is the source. Everything below holds for what a page says in either edition, and each section adds what is particular to translating. How a translation is written:

- **Translate faithfully and fully.** Everything a reader reads, the same paragraphs, lists, tables and notes, with nothing summarized, dropped or added. An English page says what its Chinese page says.
- **Keep the headings one for one with the Chinese page**: the same number of headings, at the same levels, in the same order. The two editions match sections by their position, so a heading added, dropped or moved breaks every link into the page's sections.
- **Leave the code alone.** Inline `code`, code blocks, ids, file paths, numbers and keys stay exactly as they are.
- **Links and templates.** A link target or a template argument may stay as the Chinese page has it; `npm run wiki:translate -- relink <file>` rewrites them into English (see [[编辑入门#英文版]]). Translate the free text of a template: a quotation and its speaker, a spoiler's title, an image's caption, the text of `{{planned|…}}` and `{{tbd|…}}`.
- **The game's lines.** Dialogue the page quotes from the game exists in the game in English too; use the game's English line where you can find it, otherwise translate it naturally, in character.
- When you are done, stamp the file (`npm run wiki:translate -- stamp <file>`), so the wiki knows which version of the Chinese page it translates.

## Voice

- **State facts; do not judge.** Write "the axe's efficiency is 1 on trees and 0.25 on stone", not "the axe is very useful". How to play is up to the reader.
- **Write what the player can see and do.** The readers are players and team members, not programs. Write "stand on the bed and press {{键|E}} to sleep", not "the bed's handsRange is 0".
- **One sentence, one thing.** Short sentences, few clauses. Lists and tables read better than long paragraphs.
- **No "we", no "you should".** For the player, write "the player" or use the imperative ("press E to pick it up"); "you" is fine where the Chinese page addresses the player directly.
- **Plain, precise English.** Present tense. American spelling in prose ("color", "behavior", "armor" as a common word); the game's own names exactly as the game spells them, even where that is British ("Armour" when it is the game's name for the category). Chinese punctuation does not carry over: 「」 become “ ”, 、 and ， become commas, full-width brackets （） become ( ).

## Use the game's own names

Whatever the game calls a thing, the wiki calls it the same. In the English edition, the names are the game's own English (what you see with the game set to English): every person, item, monster, location, quest, status, skill and region already has one, and it is shown on the thing's infobox. Do not coin a translation of your own, and do not transliterate the Chinese.

When you are not sure of a name, look at the thing's page and its infobox, at the game's code under `games/openworld/content` and `games/openworld/tools/src/demo` (the `en` fields), or at how the [[图鉴]] writes it. The setting's own terms (the Five Sufferings, the Seal-bearers, the Long Night…) are those of the [[术语表]].

## Numbers

- Wherever `{{value|name}}` or a data table can give a number, do not write it by hand.
- A number you must write by hand gets its unit: game minutes, game hours, real seconds, tiles. In the game, 1 game minute = 1 real second.
- Write chances as percentages: "a 20% chance".
- A translation keeps every number of the Chinese page as it is.

## Sources

Every fact must be traceable. For the game's numbers and names, the source is the code; for how a rule was decided, the source is a design discussion or a design document; for the setting, the source is the original text of "The Church of the Five Sufferings: Setting and Main Story" ([[世界观原文]]). Lore and chapter pages end with a `## Sources` section that names where they come from.

A translation adds no facts of its own. If the Chinese page is wrong or missing something, fix the Chinese page (or raise it on its Talk tab); the English page follows.

## Spoilers

What a person leaves unsaid, and the story's twists (the first and second twists of the [[主线剧情]]), always go in a `{{spoiler|…}}` block. A page's opening paragraph never spoils. A translation keeps every spoiler block where the Chinese page has it, and translates its title.

## Things not built yet

- Designed but not in the game yet: put `{{planned}}` at the top of the page, and write what has been decided.
- Not designed yet: put `{{tbd}}`, and write only what the original text and the discussions already say; do not fill in the setting for the team. To make a proposal, press the page's "Talk" tab and open an issue.

## Trivia

Write only what can be confirmed: details you can see in the game, decisions taken during development. No guesses like "possibly a tribute to such-and-such".

## History

When the game changes something, add a line, by date, to the `## History` section of its page.
