Crops are planted in farmland, watered once per stage, and grow stage by stage until they are ripe for harvest. How to plant them is in [[Farming|Farming]]. The table below lists every crop in the game; seed prices and produce prices are those of the [[trading stall|trading stall]] on [[The Held Ground]] (seed prices are written as “coins / seeds per packet”).

## All crops

{{crop table}}

Every crop has three stages: sprout → young plant → ripe. Turnips, parsnips and radishes take 1 game hour per stage; pumpkins, cauliflowers, melons, corn and blueberries 4 hours per stage; the rest 2 hours per stage. Normally a crop is watered once per stage; irrigation makes one watering serve two stages, at the same speed. A ripe crop cut with a sickle gives 2 produce and 2 seeds; picked by hand with {{key|E}}, it gives 1 produce and 1 seed.

## Vegetables

There are fourteen vegetables. All can be eaten as they are; they restore only [[Satiety|Satiety]] and cannot be roasted.

- **Carrots, turnips, tomatoes, pumpkins**: the vegetable garden in [[Firstlight Village]] grows these four at every stage, and the “garden toolbox” beside it holds 2 seeds of each (see [[Farming#Firstlight's vegetable garden|Firstlight's vegetable garden]]). The stall sells them too.
- **Parsnips, potatoes, radishes, cauliflowers, melons**: seeds are sold only at the stall. Once harvested they are gone.
- **Corn, blueberries, strawberries, hot peppers, eggplants**: seeds are sold only at the stall. They fruit again after harvest (see below).

The last ten were added after the fields of Stardew Valley: root vegetables, cauliflower, melon, and five that fruit again after harvest.

## Crops that fruit again

Tomatoes, corn, blueberries, strawberries, hot peppers, eggplants and berry bushes are **perennial**: after you cut or pick them, the plant does not disappear but goes back to the young-plant stage; water it once more and it ripens again after one stage of its kind's time. Corn and blueberries take 4 hours, the rest 2 hours. Fertilizer is used up by the harvest and must be applied again; water held over by irrigation is not kept across a harvest either.

A crop that fruits again needs its seeds bought only once; after that, every watering brings another harvest. A packet of seeds for the last five holds only 3, and each seed costs more than most annuals (see the table above).

## Wheat, herbs and berry bushes

- **Wheat** ({{item|Wheat Seeds}}): the produce, {{item|Wheat}}, is a material and also animal feed (see [[Animals#Feeding|Feeding]]). Ripe wheat is dry and catches fire easily.
- **Herbs** ({{item|Herb Seeds}}): the produce, {{item|Herb}}, can be eaten and also restores health.
- **Berry bushes** ({{item|Berry Seeds}}): perennial; the produce is {{item|Berries}}.

The seeds of these three are bought on the “Farming” page of the stall; no map has them planted. Herbs and berries can also be picked up in the wild (see [[Foraging|Foraging]]). They have no sowing option in [[Building|Building]] mode; hold the seeds in your hand to sow them.

## Selling the harvest

Take the harvest to the stall on the Held Ground and sell it on the “Sell” page; prices are in the “produce price” column of the table above, in [[Dawnmark|Dawnmarks]]. Everything the stall buys and for how much is in [[Money#What the stall buys|What the stall buys]].

## Trees

An {{item|Acorn}} planted becomes an oak sapling and grows into a tree in two game days. It needs no farmland and no watering, only open sky and no one standing on it (see [[Woodcutting|Woodcutting]]). Acorns drop when you chop trees, and the stall sells them too.

## Tips

- To stock up on seeds, cut with a sickle: 2 seeds every time.
- A fertilized crop (with ash or fertilizer) gives 1 more produce when cut.
- A crop that fruits again needs its seeds bought once; after that, one watering, one harvest.
- To earn money, look at the “produce price” in the table: among annuals, a single cauliflower or melon is worth a dozen or twenty coins.
