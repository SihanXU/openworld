A house can be built several storeys high. Every storey is made of the same few things: the floor underfoot, the walls and furniture on that storey, and the ceiling overhead. You go upstairs by {{item|Stairs}}. This page covers how the storeys are put together and what holds up what; how to place things is in [[Building|Building]], and when a house counts as a room is in [[Rooms|Rooms]].

## What a storey is

- Above the ground storey you can build up to **4 more storeys**.
- The view is top-down and slanted: one storey is drawn one cell high. So things on the second storey are drawn one cell higher than the cell they are in, and things on the third storey two cells higher.
- A cell has two slots on every storey:
  - **The floor slot**: what you stand on. On the ground storey the floor slot is the terrain itself (grass, dirt…), which you can cover with a floor laid on top. Upstairs the floor slot either has a floor laid or is empty (open air).
  - **The space slot**: the one solid upright thing on that storey, at most one: a wall, a door, a flight of stairs, a chest, a workbench, a campfire. Beds, people and loose items do not take this slot (a person cannot share a cell with something solid).
- **The ceiling of one storey is the floor of the storey above.** The same planks are a ceiling from below and a floor from above.

## Each storey builds only its own storey

Someone standing on a storey can reach only three things: the floor slot of their own storey, the space slot of their own storey, and the ceiling overhead. Every other storey is out of reach.

- This is the same for every action: {{key|E}}, {{key|G}}, the left button and the right button.
- The choices listed by holding {{key|E}} or {{key|G}}, or by Alt+clicking, also list only your own storey (plus the ceiling overhead).
- To put something on the second storey, go up the stairs to the second storey first.
- The one exception is the ceiling: a one-storey hut needs a roof too, so the ceiling overhead can be laid from below (but you have to choose it on purpose; see [[#Ceilings|Ceilings]]).
- Things never find a place by themselves: if the cell has no room on that storey, or nothing to hold it up, it cannot be placed, and the preview says why; it never moves itself up or down a storey.

Someone standing on stairs counts as on the storey the stairs are on.

## Walls

{{item|Wall}}: 2 planks, 12 durability, solid; it blocks the way and the line of sight.

- It goes in a cell's space slot, with a floor underfoot (on the ground storey, the terrain itself).
- Walls can be stacked: on the second storey, put a wall right above a first-storey wall, and it stands on the wall below, with no floor needed between the two storeys. So a “two-storey wall” has to be added from upstairs; it cannot be stacked from the ground.
- Every wall holds up the floor on the storey above within 2 cells of itself. So do the stone walls and wooden walls that are on a map from the start.

## Floors

{{item|Floor}}: 2 planks, 8 durability.

- **Laid on the ground storey**: it covers the terrain that was there and becomes the cell's surface; laid on flowers or dry grass, it covers the flowers and tall grass drawn on the ground too, and they are no longer drawn. Take the floor away and the terrain and its plants show again. It can only be laid on ground that can be stood on, not on a cell of water, lava, tree or rock (“A floor cannot be laid on this ground.”). When a bed, stairs or a chest is already on the cell, the floor is laid underneath it.
- **Laid upstairs**: a wall or door on the storey below, within 2 cells of this cell (across, down or diagonally), has to hold it up. Laying outward from upstairs, as long as it is still held up, you can lay out a balcony.
- A cell can have only one floor per storey: “There is already a floor here.”

## Ceilings

A ceiling is the floor of the storey overhead. By default, right-clicking with a floor in hand always lays it on the storey under your feet ({{key|G}} only puts the floor on the ground and does not lay it); to lay a ceiling you have to choose it on purpose:

- **From your hand**: holding a floor, press {{key|R}} and the right click lays a ceiling instead (press {{key|R}} again to switch back); hold and drag to lay a row. When switched to ceiling, as in Building mode, only your own storey is drawn, and ceilings already laid are drawn pale blue.
- **Building mode**: choose “Floor” and press {{key|R}} to switch between “lay on this storey” and “lay as a ceiling”; the panel says which it is now. Dragging lays a row too. When switched to ceiling, the ceilings already laid above your storey are drawn as pale blue translucent cells that do not hide what is below.
- **Alt+right click**: Alt+right-click with a floor in hand, and among the levels listed there is a “Ceiling” entry.
- **Where to point**: a ceiling is drawn one cell higher than its cell. So to lay a ceiling over a cell, point the mouse **one cell above** that cell, where the ceiling will be drawn. The preview is drawn right under the mouse.

Rules:
- A ceiling has to be held up: a wall or door on this storey within 2 cells of the cell.
- **No ceiling directly above stairs**: “The space above the stairs must stay clear.” That is the way up. The roof over a stairwell has to wait until you are upstairs and be laid from there (it is the ceiling of the storey above).
- **Taking a ceiling away** also has to be chosen on purpose: holding an axe, pickaxe or hammer, Alt+left-click and choose “Ceiling” among the levels listed, and the whole piece comes loose and falls; press {{key|E}} to pick it up (close by, it is drawn into your bag by itself). An ordinary left click never knocks a ceiling. A ceiling with something on it (a wall, a bed, someone standing upstairs) cannot be knocked loose.

## Stairs

{{item|Stairs}}: 3 planks, 8 durability. A flight of stairs is a block one storey high with steps on top.

- **The direction is set when you place it**: in Building mode the preview has an arrow pointing the way up; {{key|R}} turns it clockwise. If you have not turned it, the stairs climb away from the player.
- **Place it in front of ground one storey higher**: the cell the arrow points to must have a floor on the storey above (the first storey's ceiling, for instance), or walking up the stairs will not take you upstairs.
- **Going up**: walk onto the stairs from the front or the sides (stepping on raises you half a storey), then take one more step in the direction of the arrow, and you are standing on the floor of the storey above.
- **The back of the stairs** is a solid block one storey high: you cannot walk up from the cell behind the stairs, and standing on the stairs you cannot walk down off the back (on that side, a floor on the storey above is the way up). When someone standing behind the stairs is hidden by them, the stairs turn translucent.
- **Going down**: on the storey above, walk toward the cell the stairs are in, and you are back on the stairs (half a storey lower); then walk down.
- The cell above the stairs must stay clear; no ceiling can be laid there.
- In a room, the stretch of stairs going up through the stairwell counts as the room's own, so the room does not stop being a room because that one cell overhead is open.

## Doors

{{item|Door}}: 2 planks, 10 durability.

- Press {{key|E}} to open and shut it (“Open the door”, “Shut the door”).
- A shut door blocks the way and the line of sight like a wall; an open door lets anyone through and lets you see through.
- Open or shut, a door is an edge of a room, just like a wall.
- Like a wall, a door holds up the floor on the storey above within 2 cells, and can be stacked on a wall.
- Creatures do not open doors. Shut the door and stay inside, and nothing outside can get in.

## Support and collapse

- Every upstairs floor is held up by the walls and doors on the storey below.
- Take away or break a wall, and the floor it was holding up, if no other wall holds it, falls in: “The floor collapses.” The fallen floor breaks, leaving only 1 plank; people and things standing on it fall to the first storey below that has a floor.
- **Falling**: falling 1 storey does no harm; every storey more costs 6 health (defense does not count). Falling from the 2nd storey above the ground to the ground costs 6; falling from the 4th costs 18. Things that fly do not fall.
- When an upstairs floor has something on it (a wall, a bed, an item, someone standing there, yourself included), that floor cannot be knocked loose; take what is on it away first. Floors on the ground storey have no such limit: knocked loose, what was on them stays on the terrain beneath.
- Picking up with {{key|E}} or knocking with a tool (left click) always goes to the topmost thing first: loose things, furniture, walls and the like, and the floor last. To reach what is beneath, hold {{key|E}} or Alt+click to choose.

## What you see indoors

- **With something overhead, what is above is not drawn.** As long as the cell you stand on is covered, the storeys above the walled, covered space you are in, together with a ring around it, are not drawn. You need not shut the door: with the door open or a gap in the wall, you still see inside.
- Inside a room, the room's south wall (the side toward the camera) turns translucent and its top is not drawn, so you can see the room clearly.
- A wall or floor that hides the player's body turns translucent too, the whole wall or the whole row of floor at once.
- From outside, a house is drawn as usual, upper storeys and roof included.
- With Building mode open, only your own storey is drawn, not those above.
- Covered cells keep the rain off; crops do not grow under a roof (see [[Farming|Farming]]).

## A two-storey house

Starting from open ground, every step uses only “build on the storey you stand on”:

1. **On the ground**: put up a ring of walls, leaving one cell for a door. Press {{key|B}}, choose a wall, hold the left button and drag along where the ring goes, walking along so every cell is within 1 cell of you. Then choose a door and put it in the cell left open.
2. **On the ground** (optional): lay floors. Choose a floor and drag across the inside; the floor covers the ground that was there.
3. **On the ground**: lay the ceiling. Choose a floor, press {{key|R}} to switch to “lay as a ceiling”, point the mouse one cell above the cells to cover, and drag across the inside. If there is to be a second storey, **leave clear the cell right above where the stairs will go**. Fully covered and closed in on every side, it becomes a room; with a bed inside it is a bedroom.
4. **On the ground**: place the stairs. Choose stairs, press {{key|R}} until the arrow points at a stretch of ceiling already laid, and put the stairs in the cell left clear. Walk up the stairs and take one more step in the arrow's direction, and you are on the second storey.
5. **On the second storey**: put up a ring of walls. Put the walls on the upper floor, or right above the first-storey walls to join them into two-storey walls.
6. **On the second storey**: lay the roof, that is, the second storey's ceiling, the same way as step 3. The cell right above the stairwell is covered at this point too.

To take it down, go backwards, step by step. Every step is within reach, and no step needs guessing.

## Tips

- If you mean to lay a ceiling but it keeps going under your feet: in Building mode press {{key|R}} and check that the panel says “Ceiling”.
- Before building a second storey, decide where the stairs go, and leave the cell right above them clear when laying the first-storey ceiling.
- Do not put anything important on an upper floor with no wall holding it: take down the wrong wall, and the whole stretch of floor falls in with everything on it.
- Lesson six of the tutorial teaches steps 1 and 3: put up walls, fit a door, place a bed, lay the ceiling.
