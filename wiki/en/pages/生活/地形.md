Terrain is the bottom layer of every cell of a map: grass, dirt, water, trees, rock… Some of it can be stood on and some blocks the way; some can be hoed into farmland, and some can be chopped down or broken up. Terrain is itself a thing in the world, under the same rules as everything else.

## Ground you can walk on

- **Grass, dirt, sand, snow**: can be hoed into farmland (see [[Farming|Farming]]), and acorns can be planted in it. Sticks, stones and cut grass grow on grass (see [[Foraging|Foraging]]).
- **Dry grass**: dry, catches fire very easily, and fire spreads fast across it (a 30% chance every half second of passing to its neighbors). Cut grass grows on it too.
- **Flowers, gravel, ash, cave floor**: ordinary ground.
- **Dirt road, cobblestones**: roads.
- **Farmland**: hoed ground, ready to sow.
- **Wooden floor, stone floor, carpet**: the ground inside buildings on maps.
- **Wooden bridge**: wooden, and it burns. A plank put on shallow water lays one cell of wooden bridge; a dead tree chopped down across a river becomes a wooden bridge too (see [[Woodcutting|Woodcutting]]).

Any ground that can be stood on can be covered with a {{item|Floor}} (see [[Storeys|Storeys]]). The ground itself has no durability and cannot be broken.

## What blocks the way

- **Tree, pine tree**: 10 durability; two chops of an axe fell it, leaving a stump (see [[Woodcutting|Woodcutting]]). It burns, and burnt out it becomes a charred tree.
- **Bush**: 4 durability; drops 1 stick when broken. It burns.
- **Rock**: 10 durability; two strikes of a pickaxe break it, dropping 1 stone (see [[Mining|Mining]]).
- **Cave wall**: the walls of a cave, 16 durability; drops 1 stone.
- **Stone wall**: 24 durability; drops 2 stones. It can hold up a floor on the storey above.
- **Wooden wall**: 12 durability; drops 1 plank. It can hold up a floor on the storey above. It burns, and burnt out it is a burnt wall (4 durability; broken, it gives coal).
- **Canvas wall**: the canvas of tents and the smithy, 6 durability; a blade opens it at once; drops 1 cloth, with a chance of 1 stick. It burns, and burnt out it is only ash.
- **Thatch wall**: the walls of a straw hut, 6 durability; an axe is quick on it; drops 1 grass and 1 stick, with a chance of 1 more grass. It burns, and burnt out it is only ash.
- **Snow-block wall**: the walls of an igloo, 10 durability; cut it with a pickaxe; drops 1 snowball, with a chance of 1 more.
- **Roof**: the roofs of houses on maps, 8 durability.
- **Charred tree**: a burnt tree, 4 durability. One chop of an axe breaks it into 1 coal.
- **Void**: the black beyond the map; it blocks the way.

When terrain that can be broken is broken, the walkable ground beneath shows through: whatever the street or the grass round it is, never a door or stairs.

### The walls of houses

A house on a map ([[初原村|Firstlight]]'s homes, the shops, tents, igloos…) is a solid block of wall cells with the house's picture drawn over it. Every cell of it is a real wall, of whatever the house is built from: plaster and stone cottages and the temple are stone walls; timber houses, the general store, the teahouse, the inn, the longhouse and the towers are wooden walls; tents are canvas walls; straw huts thatch walls; igloos snow-block walls. Striking one is like striking any wall, see [[Durability and Breaking#The walls of houses|The walls of houses]]: the picture cracks, and broken through it is a hole you can walk into. If the house is someone's, that is [[Crime|vandalism]].

## Touching by hand

Press {{key|E}} at terrain with empty hands (“Touch the grass”) and you only touch it: “Your hand brushes the grass. Nothing happens.” A little dust rises, and nothing changes. It is the same for every terrain: grass, dirt, water, trees, rock, stone walls, the wooden floors indoors… This is defined: the prompt carries no “?”, and the AI is not asked. A {{item|Floor}} you laid yourself or a wall you built is not terrain; pressing {{key|E}} at one bare-handed is not covered by any rule.

- Terrain on [[The Lands of the Five Seals]] (the world map) does not count: there, pressing {{key|E}} bare-handed is not covered by any rule (“Interact with plains ?”); pressing {{key|E}} on a place enters it.
- Touching the ground does not steal {{key|E}}: when you are not using the mouse and the thing in front of you has no {{key|E}} defined, {{key|E}} does not fall through to the ground under your feet (see [[Controls#Pointing and targets|Pointing and targets]]).

## What grows back

- **Stump**: what is left when a tree is felled, 6 durability. In the open with no one standing on it, it grows back into a tree after two game days. Chop the stump away too, and nothing grows there again.

## Water and ice

- **Shallow water, deep water**: cannot be walked into. Hold an empty container and left-click water to fill it. Water conducts: lightning striking the water passes to conductive and wet things within 2 cells. Nothing catches fire on water. Something put into water is refused (“It would sink.”) and is not lost.
- **Ice**: can be walked on. The thin ice that the ice element freezes from shallow water melts back into water after 150 seconds, and whoever stands on it is put ashore; ice that is on a map from the start does not melt. Fire melts any ice into water.

## Special ground

- **Mud**: stepping in gets you wet.
- **Healing spring**: there is one in [[Firstlight Village]]. Stepping in restores 1 health, and while you soak in it you regain 1 more every half second; it also removes burning and frozen ({{status|soaking}}).
- **Lava**: cannot be walked into. Anything standing on it or falling into it is burnt continuously by fire of force 6. Anything put into lava is taken by it, and burnt if it can burn. No map in the game uses lava yet.
- **Open air**: the cells upstairs that have no floor. You meet it only when putting or taking things: laying a floor here, or building a wall on top of a wall on the storey below (see [[Storeys|Storeys]]).

## Terrain on the world map

[[The Lands of the Five Seals]] is the map of the whole continent; each cell of it stands for a local map. A step on it takes 30 game minutes, multiplied by the terrain's factor:

| Terrain | Walkable | Factor |
|---|---|---|
| Road, city ground, bridge | Yes | 0.5 |
| Plains, steppe, fields, salt flat | Yes | 1 |
| Forest, hills, snowfield, badlands, dust plain, dead wood, white highland | Yes | 1.5 |
| Marsh | Yes | 2 |
| Sea, mountains, river | No | |

A bridge is the cell where a road crosses a river: drawn as river, with planks laid across it. Which terrain each region has is in [[The Lands of the Five Seals#Regions|The Lands of the Five Seals]].

In rain or a thunderstorm every step is half as slow again. See [[World Map|World Map]].

## Height

Some maps have differences in height: the cliffs of [[Cloudtop]], [[Mountain Foot]] and [[Dawn Hill]], the ledge of [[Thornwood Hollow]]. Height is crossed only by stairs and ramps. The rule is the same for the player, the townsfolk and the monsters, and they keep to it when they find their way.

- **Stairs and ramps**: each step is half a level; you walk up and down them as usual. They are drawn as steps cut into the rock.
- **Rock faces**: the view looks down from the south at a slant, so where the ground drops away to the south you see a face of rock, drawn on the lower ground below, one tile for each level (a cliff one level high is one tile tall, two levels two tiles). Those tiles are rock: no one can stand on them or walk along them, only at their foot.
- **Up**: anything more than half a level up cannot be climbed.
- **Jumping down**: only from the edge of a cliff one level high, and only down, never back up (like the ledges in Zelda). Walk south off the top and you jump over the rock face below and land at its foot; walk east, west or north and you land on the tile beside. The jump does not hurt.
- **Two levels or more**: a wall going down as well; you cannot jump it, and must find the stairs.
- Things that fly are not affected by height, but they cannot enter the rock-face tiles either.
- High ground blocks the line of sight.
- The colour of the rock faces, edges and stairs follows the region: on the Frostfang Coast it is a slate grey with a touch of green. Where the high ground is under snow a cornice of snow hangs over the edge; where it is grassy, a fringe of grass.

Falling from an upper storey has its own rules; see [[Storeys]].

## Tips

- On a rainy day, open ground gets you wet; step under cover and you dry off.
- To cross a river, lay planks on shallow water and you have a bridge.
- Do not light a fire on dry grass.

## History

- 2026-09-30: The game's first version already had grass, dry grass, water, ice, trees, bushes, rock, walls, roofs, caves, lava, ash, charred trees, stumps, wooden bridges, farmland, mud and other terrain.
- 2026-10-02: Ice freezes water into thin ice that melts.
- 2026-10-04: Trees, rock, cave walls and walls got durability and material; stumps grow back into trees in two days (W2b, W7). Terrain got height, with ramps, cliffs and falling (W2d). The terrain of Frostfang Coast added (W3).
- 2026-10-07: Healing springs added.
- 2026-10-09: Pressing {{key|E}} bare-handed at terrain only touches it and no longer asks the AI.
- 2026-10-25: Height really blocks the way: the rock-face tiles cannot be stood on, and height is crossed only by stairs and ramps; from a cliff one level high you can jump down (down only, never back up), and two levels or more is a wall, with no more falling off it. The rock faces were redrawn as mountain rock with beds, cracks, a lit edge and fallen stones.
- 2026-10-25: The houses on the maps got walls of what their pictures show: stone walls, wooden walls, and the new canvas, thatch and snow-block walls. A broken wall shows the ground round it, never the door beside it.
