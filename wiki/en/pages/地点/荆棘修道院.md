{{信息框}}
**The Abbey of the Thorn** stands on the hill north of the middle of [[朝圣者之路|the Pilgrim's Road]]. It is the old seat of the Order of the Thorn, and the place where [[白骑士·阿尔贝里克|Alberic]] was knighted. It lies abandoned now, yet the dead monks still keep the hours of prayer. The Order's knights are buried in [[荆棘骸骨堂]] beneath [[荆棘谷|Thornwood Hollow]]; their memorial stones stand in the abbey's court.

It is a [[和平区域与战斗区域|combat zone]].

## Areas
- **The Thorn Yard**: the weed-grown court in the middle. In it stands the **Statue in the Thorn Yard** (an [[祭坛|altar]] plinth); press {{键|E}} to read: “A young knight on one knee before a child, holding out a wooden horse he has mended. The face is worn smooth by hands. The plinth: ALBERIC, ON THE DAY HE WAS MADE A KNIGHT.” The first time you enter the court, Lumi says: “That's him? The White Knight? He used to kneel to people?”
- **Memorials and grave**: a row of four stones on the south side of the court. Three are the Order's [[墓碑|memorial stones]], the people themselves buried in the Ossuary of the Thorn:
  - “BROTHER ODO OF THE THORN, who held the gate at Varn three days and did not kneel. He lies with his brothers under Thornwood Hollow; his name is kept here.”
  - “SISTER MAREN OF THE THORN, who carried water to both armies. She lies with the Order under Thornwood Hollow; her name is kept here.”
  - “BROTHER TOMAS OF THE THORN, sworn brother to the Commander, sent to ward the Order's dead under the hollow. He keeps them still.” (He is the warden in [[守墓者圣所]].)
  - The fourth is the gardener's grave: “BROTHER GILES, gardener, buried in the yard he kept. He grew roses in the thorn yard. The roses died with him.”
- **The Chapter Hall**: the long house northwest of the court, its door facing south. See [[骑士团大厅]].
- **The abbey church**: the small stone church northeast of the court, its door facing south, with [[修道院地下墓室]] beneath it.

## Lore
{{剧透|The Order and the Writ of Relief}}
The Order's roll ends with Alberic: “after him, no one else was knighted”. His journal records that the Prior called the Crowning an honor, and his brother Hugh called it a sentence; “forty years, and then someone comes to relieve you”; then, “The forty-first year. No one came.” The [[替任文书|Writ of Relief]] in the hall is the very decree that should have relieved him, its seal unbroken, never sent, and on its back is written: “No successor willing to bear it can be found. Let him keep watch. He will not know the difference.”

The one who held it back was the abbey's prior. [[温德尔院长|Prior Wendel]] still keeps watch in the crypt after his death, guarding the Order's seal.
{{/剧透}}
